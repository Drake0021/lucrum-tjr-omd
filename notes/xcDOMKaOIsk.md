## Summary
Long remake of the FVG lesson (Feb 2025) covering FVGs, imbalances and **inverse FVGs**. FVG = three-candle pattern with a gap between candle-1 and candle-3 wicks; only the middle candle's direction matters; size doesn't matter. FVGs are **continuation confluences** (best on the first retracement after a liquidity sweep + BOS); IFVGs are **confirmation confluences equal to a BOS** — whichever comes first. New rules: in a stack of FVGs formed without a retrace, only the last (outermost) one's inversion counts; once price retraces, fills a gap and makes a new extreme in the trend, everything behind that swing is "balanced" and all older FVGs/OBs/breakers there are ignored; IFVGs do not need to be filled. Examples on NQ/ES market opens (week of 27–30 Jan 2025): sweep → BOS → retrace into FVG → continuation (one ~1:2.8).

## Concepts & definitions
- **Fair value gap / imbalance** — three-candle pattern: candle 1, expansion candle 2, candle 3. Bullish FVG = range between candle-1 top wick and candle-3 bottom wick (a price range with a lack of sell orders). Bearish FVG = candle-1 bottom wick to candle-3 top wick (lack of buy orders). [01:39–03:20; 04:59–05:35; 18:57]
- **Candle colors** — colors of candles 1 and 3 don't matter; only the middle candle's direction matters. Candle 3 with no wick still counts. [04:26–05:35; 12:25]
- **Not an FVG** — if the wicks of candles 1 and 3 overlap, no imbalance, even if the middle candle is huge. [06:08–07:15; 12:57–13:31]
- **Why it fills** — imbalanced price action tends to be balanced: price returns into the gap, the missing orders fill, and if candles move out of it in trend direction price continues. [03:20–04:26]
- **Size doesn't matter** — tiny FVGs are as valid as large ones. [08:56–11:16; 46:00]
- **FVG = continuation confluence** — "when price retraces more often than not it's going to fill a fair value gap". [20:38–21:10]
- **Inverse FVG** — an FVG that a candle **closes** beyond (bullish FVG closed below / bearish FVG closed above). It is a "confirmation confluence" and "equal to break of structure"; signals order flow is disrespected and the trend likely reverses. [23:28–26:15; 28:26]
- **IFVGs need not be filled** — "inverse fair value gaps do not need to be filled at all". Once inversed, it is no longer an FVG. [27:53]
- **Stacked FVGs** — consecutive FVGs in the same leg with **no retrace in between**: closing through the inner one(s) is not an IFVG; you need the last FVG of the stack (top one for bearish stack/bullish reversal; bottom one for bullish stack/bearish reversal) inversed — "at that point we might as well just look for a break of structure". [28:58–31:47]
- **A retrace resets the FVG cycle** — after a retrace fills a gap and a new leg forms, only the FVGs in the new leg count for the stack rule. [37:13–38:18]
- **Balanced price action** — in an uptrend, once price retraces into an FVG and then pushes past the prior high, all price action below that retrace low is balanced: ignore any unfilled FVGs, order blocks, breaker blocks below it. Mirror for downtrends (push below the low → ignore everything above the retrace high). [32:18–34:29; 35:01; 43:16]
- **Order flow** — uptrend = bullish order flow, downtrend = bearish order flow (identified after liquidity sweep + BOS). [14:36–15:09]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Never buy/sell just because price enters an FVG; needs daily bias, liquidity sweep, IFVG/BOS, OB/breaker etc. [07:50–08:24]
- Core sequence: liquidity sweep → BOS (or IFVG, whichever comes first) → retrace into FVG in the new direction → enter → targets at draws on liquidity. [18:24–19:31; 22:19; 23:28; 26:15]
- Prefer the **first retracement** after the sweep + BOS; second is still good; third is weaker ("slow stroke"); after full take-profit hit, no more trades ("our bias has been completed for the day"). Late in the trend (many retracements), don't enter: overextended and draws already taken. [15:09–16:46]
- Bullish FVG closed below by a candle = treat as bearish BOS even if no low was closed below; look for bearish FVGs to fill for shorts. [26:15–26:48; 27:20]
- In a stack without a retrace, require the outermost FVG to be inversed. [29:33–31:47]
- Ignore all confluences behind the last balanced swing; after a new BOS against the trend, only care about the new BOS and the new-direction FVG. [40:27]
- Use at "the right time and right place" — London/random-time examples flagged as out of context; wants NY session. [42:43; 45:27]
- Stops/TP: not covered here ("other videos ... how to set take profits / stop losses"). [20:38]

## Process / order of operations
1. Know order flow / daily bias. [14:36]
2. At the NY open, wait for a liquidity sweep. [18:24]
3. Wait for BOS or IFVG against the prior move (whichever first). [26:15]
4. Mark the FVGs created in the new leg (including small ones). [18:57]
5. Enter when price retraces into the FVG and shows a reaction (selling/buying pressure out of it). [19:31]
6. Target draws on liquidity; stop trading once full TP is hit. [19:31; 16:46]
