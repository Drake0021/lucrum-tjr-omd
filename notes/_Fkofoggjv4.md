## Summary
A dedicated lesson on liquidity and liquidity sweeps, which he calls "probably the biggest concept in trading" and the most foundational part of his daily strategy. He explains why resting orders sit above highs and below lows (breakout entries + counter-trend traders' stops), why market makers must sweep them to fill size at good prices, and ranks the "higher quality" draws on liquidity: (1) session highs/lows (Asia/London/NY), (2) relative equal highs/lows, (2a) exact equal highs/lows, (3) low-resistance ("trend line") liquidity = stacked highs/lows inside a trend. Timing matters: sweeps are expected at session opens; he only trades the US market open and "really never" an hour after. Execution: key level swept → drop to 1m → BOS → FVG/EQ fill + reaction candle → enter, stop beyond the sweep extreme, TPs at the next draws on liquidity (TP1 ≈ 1:1). Includes two worked example trades (ES/US index same-day NY open short; US index London-open short).

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity** — "pending and resting orders." Lies above highs and below lows. [02:46; 06:37]
- **Liquidity sweep** — "when we go and take out and fill those pending orders to be able to move price in the opposite direction." [02:46]
- **Why orders sit above highs** — two sources of buy orders: (a) breakout/trend traders buying when a high is pushed above, (b) stop-losses of traders who sold the pullback (they must buy back). Mirror below lows: breakout sellers + stops of pullback buyers. [03:19–05:30]
- **Market maker logic** — large players can't fill size by just pressing buy (price would run away from them); to buy they push price into a pool of pending sell orders (below lows), fill, then price reverses. Mirror for sells above highs. Retail can't move the market. [08:15–11:36; 13:16]
- **Sweep vs trend continuation** — "more often than not, when we push above highs [in an uptrend] it's not going to be a liquidity sweep. It's just the trend continuing." Hence the need for quality filters, timing, and LTF confirmation. [14:54; 07:11]
- **Fractal** — sweeps occur on every timeframe (examples on 30m, 4H, 1m, 5m), but he does not want to trade 1m sweeps: "high time frames hold higher power"; by the time a 1m sweep moves "it's already said and done." Look for draws on liquidity on the higher timeframes. [15:26; 16:30–17:04; 19:50–20:58]
- **Sessions as new money** — Asia, London, NY each bring new traders/market makers who must fill orders → sweep the previous session's liquidity at the open. [07:43–08:48; 21:33]
- **Session clock (ET)** — Asia starts 18:00 ET; London starts 03:00 ET; New York opens 09:30 ET. He advises against trading Asia (low volume); London and NY have more volume. [34:13; 09:21] (answers OPEN §12.8 partially: session *start* times; end times not given)
- **High-quality draw #1: session highs/lows** — Asian, London, (prior) NY session H/L. "High confluence for the new traders to take out the old traders." He uses an indicator that plots them (blue = London high, red = Asia high). [21:33–22:06]
- **High-quality draw #2: relative equal highs/lows (REH/REL)** — two or three highs nearly at the same level, not yet swept; stacked buy orders → "super strong draw on liquidity." [23:41–25:20]
- **#2a: exact equal highs/lows** — "just as good if not better"; rarer. If one is slightly higher it's a relative-equal high/low. [30:52–32:03]
- **#3: low-resistance liquidity ("trend line liquidity")** — successive higher lows in an uptrend (or lower highs in a downtrend) stacked close together; price can take all of them in one move and fill "four times the amount" of orders. He treats it as "pretty much the same thing" as relative equal highs/lows. [26:25–27:31; 30:18]
- **High- vs low-resistance liquidity** — he promises to define high-resistance liquidity but never does (vague). [25:20–25:53]
- **Manipulation at the open** — "every single market open ... there's going to be some form of manipulation" taking out session H/L, REH/REL, EQH/EQL or low-resistance liquidity. The sweep doesn't have to happen right at 9:30; EURUSD example swept London high ~30 min after the open. [33:08–33:40; 36:25]
- **Market has no obligation** — we don't know which high/low will be the reversal point; mark all high-probability ones and wait for confirmation. [45:48–48:03]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Mark, before each session open, the high-quality draws: session highs/lows, relative equal & equal highs/lows, low-resistance (stacked trend) liquidity. Mark multiple levels — he doesn't know which will be swept. [30:52; 47:30]
- Prefer HTF draws on liquidity; don't trade 1m sweeps of 1m liquidity as the setup. [20:24–20:58]
- Time filter: he "pretty much only trade[s] session open ... only US market open. I'm really never going to be trading an hour after New York market opens." [32:36–33:08]
- A swept key level alone is never an entry: "just because we're moving above a session high doesn't mean we can just ... instantly press sell." Wait for confirmation: BOS, IFVG, FVG fill, equilibrium, breaker blocks, order blocks. [43:34–44:08; 48:03–48:37]
- Execution sequence shown: key level swept → scale down to 1m → 1m BOS → retrace fills the FVG (or EQ) → a candle closes out of it in trade direction → enter. [38:36–39:11; 42:29]
- Stop: above the swept highs (for shorts). [39:11; 42:29]
- Targets: "other draws on liquidity" — low-resistance liquidity, relative equal lows, prior lows. TP1 example = 1:1. "We're not just taking profits off of random draws ... not ... Fibonacci extensions." "Just like how we enter on liquidity sweeps, I'm exiting on liquidity sweeps." [39:11–41:22]
- Take partial profits as price goes down; first TP shouldn't be expected at 1:9. [43:01; 45:15]

## Process / order of operations
1. Pre-open: mark session highs/lows (Asia 18:00 ET start, London 03:00 ET), REH/REL, EQH/EQL, low-resistance liquidity on HTF and 5m. [34:13; 47:30]
2. At the session open (NY 09:30 ET for indices) expect manipulation into one of those levels; may take up to ~30 min. [33:40; 36:25]
3. When a key level is swept, drop to the 1m. [38:36]
4. Wait for 1m BOS opposite to the sweep. [38:36]
5. Wait for retrace into the new FVG (or EQ) and a candle closing back out in trade direction → enter. [39:11; 42:29]
6. Stop beyond the sweep extreme. [39:11]
7. TPs at the next draws on liquidity (TP1 ~1:1, then further low-resistance liquidity/REL). [39:45]

## Risk & psychology rules
