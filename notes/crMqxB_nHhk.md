## Summary
Day 4 of the 2026 Path to Profitability series. TJR defines liquidity as resting orders and calls it "the goat of confluences" — his strategy "begins and ends with liquidity". He derives where liquidity rests from retail behavior: breakout traders buy above highs / sell below lows, and opposite-side traders keep stop losses above highs / below lows, so there are "two sets" of buy orders above highs and two sets of sell orders below lows. Smart money / market makers need opposing orders to fill large positions, so they push price through highs/lows to trigger these orders and fill in the opposite direction (a liquidity sweep). Crucially, a sweep only gives the *opportunity* for reversal; it is not an entry signal by itself — later confluences must confirm. Relative equal highs (stacked highs) multiply the resting orders and are previewed for the advanced video.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity** — "resting orders" on the chart. The market needs orders to be filled to move; without fills it goes sideways. [01:40]–[02:49]
- **Liquidity as primary confluence** — "the goat of confluences... this is where everything of our strategy begins... it begins and it ends with liquidity." [04:32]
- **Where liquidity rests** — above highs (buy orders) and below lows (sell orders). [09:34], [13:29]
- **Two sets of orders above a high** — (1) breakout buyers who buy when the high is pushed above; (2) buy-stop orders of short sellers whose stop is above the high. [11:15]–[11:50], [14:40]–[15:47]
- **Two sets of orders below a low** — (1) breakout sellers who sell when the low is broken; (2) sell-stop orders of longs whose stop is below the low. [12:24], [16:24]–[17:36]
- **Retail invalidation logic** — in an uptrend retail buys above the high with stop under the higher low (the invalidation point); mirror in a downtrend. [07:53]–[08:27], [10:40]
- **Smart money / market makers** — need an equal number of opposing contracts to fill large orders (e.g., to fill 1,000 sell contracts they need 1,000 buy contracts); they push price above highs to trigger buy orders and fill their sells (and below lows to fill buys). [18:47]–[23:19]
- **Liquidity sweep ("reverse psychology")** — price pushes above a high/below a low, triggers resting orders, then reverses. Occurs on every timeframe (5m, 15m, 1H, weekly, quarterly, monthly). [22:10], [27:49]
- **Relative equal highs (stacked highs)** — two highs at roughly the same level; called "low resistance draws and liquidity stacked up"; "four times the amount of orders" (two sets per high). Detailed in the next video. [26:41]–[27:49]
- **Sweep = opportunity, not certainty** — "All that liquidity does for the market makers is it gives the market makers the opportunity to change the trend... It doesn't mean that the trend is going to change every single time." New trends form through liquidity being swept. [30:43]–[32:26]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Do NOT trade breakouts (buying above highs / selling below lows) — that is retail behavior; think in the opposite direction like smart money. [17:36]–[18:10]
- Do NOT blindly fade every sweep (sell every push above a high / buy every push below a low) — many sweeps continue; it "is not a good strategy". [30:43], [33:01]–[34:05]
- Step 1 of the strategy = locate where liquidity rests (above highs / below lows) and treat a sweep as a potential reversal point; then require other confluences confirming that orders are being filled and trend is reversing before entering (confirmation confluences deferred to later videos). [32:26]–[34:39]
- Goal is to catch tops of reversals down and bottoms of reversals up from liquidity sweeps. [33:01]
