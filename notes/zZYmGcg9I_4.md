## Summary
A mindset primer for the 2024 "Trading Transformation" series, given before any concepts. TJR argues that strategy is "far from the determining factor" of profitability. Time, repetition and consistency are what matter. He uses basketball analogies (watching videos vs. practicing; Steph Curry is great at the basics) and a construction-worker analogy (same toolbox and blueprint, but more correct repetitions win). He warns against strategy-hopping and against skipping parts of the plan. He tells viewers to use the concepts they are best at and to add new tools only in backtesting. No chart content.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Trading as a skill** — Learned by consuming and practicing in roughly equal measure. Consuming alone (the "YouTube black hole") does not make anyone profitable. [01:09–05:35]
- **The basics win** — The best performers are excellent at simple fundamentals, not fancy moves. Applied to trading, master the basics he will teach. [05:35–07:17]
- **Blueprint = trading plan/strategy; toolbox = concepts** — Skipping steps of the blueprint, or getting a new blueprint every month (strategy-hopping), sets you back to square one. [12:19–14:33]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Follow the same plan every day. Do not change strategy monthly and do not skip steps. Trade on confluences, not emotion. [13:27–14:33]
- Use the concepts that make sense to you and that you execute best (e.g. liquidity sweeps vs. FVGs vs. order blocks). Don't force a tool you don't understand. [19:35–21:13]
- Add any new concept **only in backtesting / in private**, never in live trading, until it is "overwhelmingly obvious" it belongs. [21:13–21:49]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Discipline and consistency are "two of the biggest lessons and two of the hardest things to learn." [14:33]
- Practice volume matters: more correct repetitions per day means faster progress. Incorrect repetitions (skipping blueprint steps) are worse than fewer correct ones. [11:12–13:27]
- He mentions his own "one to two trades a day" guidance as a reason people get too little live practice. This implies they should practice more (backtest/replay), not that he raises the limit (inferred). [05:00–05:35]
