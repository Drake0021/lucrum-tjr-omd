## Summary
Day 5 of the 2026 Path to Profitability series. TJR lists the higher-probability draws on liquidity (DOL) to use as reversal (entry) points and as targets (exit/take-profit points): (1) session highs/lows (Asia, London, New York), (2) previous day high/low, (3) low resistance liquidity (= stacked trending highs/lows, "trend line liquidity"), (4) relative equal highs/lows, (5) data highs/lows (high/low of high-impact news candles). He gives exact session times in New York time and explains the session-to-session manipulation narrative: each new session's money sweeps the prior session's high or low and then targets the opposite side. He repeats that a sweep only gives an opportunity to reverse and that other confluences are required to enter. He closes by introducing the external vs internal liquidity framework (price either takes highs/lows or fills imbalances such as FVGs) and the core of the strategy: sweep highs -> look for sells to lows; sweep lows -> look for buys to highs.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Higher-probability draws on liquidity** — liquidity levels stronger than "a random high/low". He says "1 2 3 4 5 6 7 forms" at the start but actually covers five (session H/L, PDH/PDL, low resistance liquidity, relative equal H/L, data H/L). [00:45]–[01:59]
- **Sessions (New York time)** — Asia 18:00–03:00; London 03:00–08:30; New York 08:30–18:00 (NY pre-market 8:30, NY market open 9:30; he groups pre-market with NY). NY has AM and PM sub-sessions (to be covered in "time" lesson). [02:35], [11:08]–[11:43], [19:41]–[20:15]
- **Session highs/lows** — highest/lowest price of each session window (e.g., London high = highest point 03:00–08:30). Each new session brings "new money" whose market makers manipulate (sweep) the previous session's high or low, fill orders, then target the opposite side. [04:15]–[09:58], [11:43]–[12:20]
  - London bullish template: sweep Asia low -> fill buys -> target Asia high. [05:56]–[07:36]
  - NY bearish template: sweep London high -> fill sells -> target London low. [08:12]–[08:47]
- **Previous day high/low (PDH/PDL)** — the highest and lowest point of all three sessions combined (a full trading day 18:00–18:00 ET; inferred from session definitions); significant DOL. [20:15]–[20:50]
- **Low resistance liquidity** — "just stacked up highs and lows", also "trend line liquidity / trend liquidity": the successive higher lows of an uptrend (or lower highs of a downtrend) that have not been taken. Once price reverses, it tends to run through all of them ("cookie jar" analogy: market makers are greedy and keep taking the next cookie). [24:56]–[29:21]
  - Use as targets: after a sweep + entry confluences, the stacked untaken lows/highs are take-profit points. [29:56]
  - Use as entry context: if a session has already run through stacked lows (orders executed), that gives the opportunity to fill buys in the opposite direction. [31:02]–[31:36]
- **Relative equal highs/lows** — two highs (lows) at the exact same price or very nearly ("right underneath it"); four sets of orders (two per swing). Example given: two lows "literally 50 cents apart" counted as relative equal lows. Easier to find on lower timeframes. [37:47]–[38:52], [40:44], [42:44]
- **Data highs/lows** — the high and low of the candle(s) printed at a high-impact (red folder on Forex Factory) news release (e.g., CPI, PPI, unemployment claims at 8:30 ET; ISM Manufacturing PMI at 10:00 ET). Treated as DOL like any other. [44:23]–[46:04], [50:47]
- **External vs internal liquidity** — "price is always either looking to take out external liquidity, which is... highs and lows, or fill imbalances, which is internal liquidity" (FVGs and other imbalances; advanced imbalance concepts coming in later videos). [51:22]–[51:55]
- **Entry vs exit use of DOL** — every one of these levels can be an entry point (sweep = potential reversal) or an exit point (target). [33:16]–[33:50]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Core logic: "we sweep out highs, we're looking for sells down to lows. We sweep out lows, we're looking for buys up to highs." Use draws on liquidity to target other draws. [53:03]–[53:36]
- Do NOT sell simply because price trades above a data high / session high / PDH, or buy because it trades below a low — need other confluences to confirm (taught later). [14:36]–[15:09], [23:48], [47:17]
- Session sweep -> target opposite session extreme: if NY sweeps London (and/or Asia) high, target London low / Asia low / PDL. [33:16], [23:14]
- After a reversal from a sweep, target the stacked low-resistance lows (for shorts) or highs (for longs) as take-profit points. [29:56], [35:30]–[36:04]
- Pairing example: session high sweep as entry DOL + low-resistance lows as exits. [35:30]–[36:04]
- Mark the high and low of the high-impact news candle as liquidity. [47:51]
- Session-to-session manipulation "doesn't happen every single day". [14:04]

## Process / order of operations
1. Chart in New York time. [11:08]
2. Mark Asia (18:00–03:00), London (03:00–08:30) and NY session highs/lows (he uses a session indicator). [10:33]–[12:20]
3. Mark previous day high/low. [20:50]
4. Mark low-resistance (stacked trend) highs/lows and relative equal highs/lows. [24:56], [37:47]
5. Check Forex Factory for red-folder news; mark data candle high/low. [44:23]
6. Watch for a sweep of one of these levels as the potential reversal point; look for entry confluences (later lessons); target the opposite-side draws. [53:03]
