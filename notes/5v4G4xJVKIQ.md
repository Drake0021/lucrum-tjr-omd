## Summary
Recap of a losing GBPUSD long (about -$10k) and an ES long that hit TP1 and then BE. The net was a small losing day (~-0.25%, ~-$5k). The GU loss was a mistake: he entered after breaking a high that was not "prominent". The main lesson is choosing between multiple setups and splitting daily risk across them. Rank setups by confluence, alignment with daily bias, and RR, and skip the worst ones (he skipped a GJ setup at about 1:0.7). He split risk 50/50 across two trades so that one loss used only half the daily risk budget.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Prominent high** — a significant swing high whose break or close-above counts as real structure confirmation. A minor internal high does not count. He would have waited for one of the prominent highs to be taken out before entering. [01:42], [02:49]–[03:22]
- **Area of accumulation prior to big extensions** — "how I've been trading recently": find HTF bias, then HTF order blocks / areas of accumulation before big legs, and trade off them. [00:33], [04:29]
- **Order block (2.0 usage)** — "just an area where orders were filled prior". He uses it loosely and interchangeably with the accumulation area. [06:06]
- **Invalidation point** — for the GU trade, a trade is invalid once the London session low sweep is itself swept, so the stop can sit below the sweep low instead of a nearer low. [01:42]
- **August "SMC trap" observation** — the market gives an SMC-looking setup with only 1–2 confluences, people enter, and they get stopped out. He wants more confluences (sweep + 5m BOS + 1m BOS + OB reaction). [05:02]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- A 5m BOS of a non-prominent high is not enough. Wait for a reaction (FVG fill + 1m BOS) or a break/close above a prominent high. [01:42], [03:22]
- Correction (lesson): only enter after a prominent high is taken out or closed above on the 5m. [02:49]–[03:57]
- Stop: may sit at the true invalidation (below the liquidity sweep low) instead of the closest low, but this adds risk. [01:42], [06:38]
- ES model: area of accumulation → breakout → OB where orders were filled → 5m BOS (not enough alone) → leg down sweeps liquidity → 1m BOS up → entry on a 5m bullish candle close in the OB. Stop under the lows. He notes he could have tucked the stop tighter for better RR. [04:29]–[06:06]
- Targets: OBs / high-confluence areas where price could react. [02:15]
- Choosing between setups: pick the one with (1) the most confluence, (2) the best alignment with daily bias, (3) the best risk-to-reward. "Those are kind of the top three." [08:18], [11:13]
- If two setups are roughly equal, take the one with the better RR. GJ offered about 1:0.7 to the first target at a high-confluence area, versus GU at 1:1.5 at TP1, so he took GU. [08:52]–[09:28]
- Do not take a trade whose RR to the first reaction area is under 1:1 ("that's not worth it to me"). (Inferred as a rule from the GJ example.) [08:52]
- Splitting risk: if you expect 2 trades, risk about 50% of the daily risk budget on each, so total risk equals one full trade. With 3 candidates, drop the weakest and split across the 2 best. [10:03], [12:19]–[12:51]
- If the second trade never triggers, you only lost 50% of the day's budget. [13:26]
- If you can't afford two trades within your risk tolerance, cut the lot size. [15:08]–[15:43]

## Process / order of operations
1. HTF bias (4H), noting conflicting lower-timeframe trends (hourly).
2. Mark HTF areas of accumulation / OBs before big legs (15m).
3. Wait for a liquidity sweep (e.g., the London session low), then a 5m BOS of a prominent high, then a reaction in the zone (FVG/OB) and a 1m BOS.
4. If several setups exist, rank them by confluence, daily bias alignment, and RR. Take the top 2 at most.
5. Split daily risk across the trades you plan to take (e.g., 50/50).
6. Manage: TP1 → partials, BE.
