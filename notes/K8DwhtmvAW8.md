## Summary
Part 3 of the execution ("putting the pieces together") series. After ~6 minutes of banter, TJR covers (1) liquidity sweep + break of structure (BOS) + fair value gap (FVG/imbalance) entry, and (2) liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG or order block (OB) + equilibrium (50% of the leg), which he calls a high-confluence trade. Examples include an S&P (ES) 15-minute setup from that day (Powell speaking — he didn't trade), hourly and daily examples. He frames FVG and equilibrium as "last chance" entries, says lower-timeframe BOS is the confirmation once price is in the zone, and ends by stating that setups against the daily bias tend to give short-lived moves — leading into the daily bias series.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG entry** — after a sweep and BOS, the displacement leg leaves an imbalance (FVG); entry is when price retraces back into that imbalance (not an OB entry). [06:52]
- **Imbalance / FVG** — price range left by the impulsive leg; "this whole thing in itself is just an imbalance" for a large one-sided move. Price tends to come back to fill it, then targets previous liquidity. [06:52], [10:18]
- **Lower-timeframe confirmation inside the FVG** — when price is in the FVG, "scale down to a lower time frame to find confirmation" (a BOS, e.g. on the 15m with a candle close above) then execute. [07:26], [09:44]
- **Equilibrium** — 50% of the leg: measured from the swing low to the swing high of the BOS leg for a bullish setup ("measuring from the low up until here") — he corrects himself mid-sentence on a bearish example to "swing high to the swing low". As soon as a retracement (opposite) candle forms, he looks for equilibrium. [15:00], [16:12], [19:49]
- **High-confluence trade** — equilibrium lining up with the FVG (and/or OB) = "high confluence trade". [15:00]
- **FVG and equilibrium as "last chance entries"** — if you missed the OB/BOS entry, FVG and equilibrium are the later entry opportunities. [19:16]
- **Stacked confluences** — e.g., sweep, BOS, OB, price wicks exactly into equilibrium, 5m OB, 5m FVG, LTF BOS — counting many confluences builds confidence to "spam the buy button". [22:27], [23:01]
- **Displacement leg not requiring sweep** — some examples show BOS + imbalance fill without a clean liquidity sweep; still valid imbalance reaction (shown as variant). [11:23]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Model C: IF liquidity sweep → BOS creates an FVG THEN wait for price to retrace into the FVG; optionally drop to LTF and enter on a BOS (candle close) confirmation; target previous draws on liquidity. [06:52], [09:44]
- Model D: IF sweep → BOS → FVG and/or OB, THEN draw equilibrium (50%) of the BOS leg; enter where equilibrium overlaps the FVG/OB. [13:52], [15:00]
- Stop example (not yet formally taught): under the low(s) of the confirmation structure. [07:26]
- Price may partially fill an FVG (fills one imbalance, not the next one) — still reacts. [10:50]
- Filter: a LTF sweep/BOS setup not aligned with daily bias tends to be short-lived and may not reach the first take-profit. [23:34]
- Didn't trade on day of Powell speech (high-impact news) (inferred as a filter, he only says "I didn't trade today because Powell was speaking"). [06:52]

## Process / order of operations
1. Liquidity sweep. 2. BOS (close beyond structure). 3. Mark imbalance (FVG) / OB created by the BOS leg. 4. When the first retracement candle prints, draw equilibrium of the leg. 5. Wait for price into FVG/OB/equilibrium overlap. 6. Drop to LTF for BOS confirmation. 7. Execute; target previous liquidity pools / HTF highs/lows. [06:52]–[10:18], [15:00], [21:54]
- Upcoming curriculum order: daily bias → take profits → stop losses → check-in. [17:36]
