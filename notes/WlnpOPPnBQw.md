## Summary
A recap of a Monday ES trade day (inferred 2025-03-17; uploaded Thursday 2025-03-20) with one loss and one win, net ≈+$4,000 by his account (+$4,735 per the title). The draws were London session highs and **CPI "news data highs"** from the prior Wednesday. He says news-data highs are as strong a draw as session highs. At the open NQ took those highs and ES did not, forming a 5m SMT (NQ high then lower high; ES lagging). He traded ES short as the lagging index. Trade 1 was a short off a 1m breaker after "the macro" opened. He emotionally moved his stop down under the highs at ~2R and got stopped (~−$19K). Trade 2 re-shorted on a new BOS with the same 5m bias (bearish order flow + SMT): partials at the equal lows/EQ, a second TP, the runner targeted hourly lows and was stopped at BE. He also explains why early-Trump-era tariff news makes price action choppy.

## Concepts & definitions
- **News data highs/lows** — the highs or lows made on a high-impact release (here CPI). He treats them like session highs: "news data highs are a strong draw on liquidity" [00:35–01:11].
- **Lagging index (SMT)** — after both indexes approach a draw: NQ made a high then a lower high after taking the data highs, while ES made a high then a higher high without taking them. "The S&P 500 was the lagging index ... so obviously I was going to want to target trades on the S&P" [02:21–02:55]. Note: in his description, the index that *failed* to take the draw (ES) is the one he trades in the reversal direction (inferred from context).
- **5m SMT → drop to 1m** — "if we took out highs and we make a 5-minute SMT, I'm willing to scale down" to the 1m [04:02].
- **The macro** — he waits until "the macro had opened" before the 1m entry [03:28–04:02]. He doesn't define it; probably the ICT 9:50–10:10 macro window (inferred).
- **1m breaker (breaker structure)** — "we get a one minute breaker structure. For me, I'm willing to enter purely off that" [04:42].
- **Equal lows** — "a strong draw on liquidity even on the low time frame" [05:15].
- **5m bearish order flow** — bias is maintained while 5m structure stays bearish and the 5m SMT holds [06:22].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Draws for the day: the nearest unswept session highs/lows plus news-data highs/lows close to the NY open [00:00–01:11].
- After a draw is taken by one index while the other fails (5m SMT), trade the lagging/failing index in the reversal direction [02:21–02:55; 03:28].
- Entry: 5m SMT → 1m breaker → enter; the stop goes above the highs (the swing that formed the SMT, inferred) [04:42].
- TP1 at equilibrium (of the dealing range, inferred) [04:42]; later TPs at equal lows, the order block and hourly lows [06:22–06:57; 08:04].
- **Never trail stops**: "I never trail stop losses." Moving the stop under the highs to cut risk at ~2R was "an emotional decision" and caused the loss [05:15–05:49].
- After a stop-out, the bias holds as long as 5m bearish order flow and the 5m SMT are intact. Wait for the next setup (a new BOS) and re-enter [06:22–06:57].
- After TP2, the remainder sits at BE; the final TP is the hourly lows "for a really crazy risk-to-reward" [08:04].

## Process / order of operations
1. Pre-open: mark London/Asia session highs and lows and news-data (CPI) highs. Pick the draw closest to the open (London highs + CPI highs) [00:00–01:11].
2. Open: wait for the draw to be taken; check SMT between ES and NQ on the 5m [02:21].
3. Choose the instrument = the lagging index (ES) [02:55].
4. After the macro opens, drop to the 1m; enter on a 1m breaker (Trade 1) or a 1m BOS (Trade 2); stop above the highs [04:02–04:42; 06:57].
5. Targets: EQ / equal lows → OB → hourly lows; partials, then BE [06:57–08:04].
