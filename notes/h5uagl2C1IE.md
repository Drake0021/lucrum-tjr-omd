## Summary
TJR shows how to identify FVGs/imbalances as a three-candle pattern and how to draw bullish and bearish FVG boxes from the wicks. He covers when there is no FVG: the candle 1 and candle 3 wicks overlap. He then marks examples on a chart (instrument unnamed; likely ES, inferred). He stresses that FVGs are only identified here, not traded alone, and that many will never be filled. He opens by noting he hadn't traded for several sessions because "market has been sh*t."

## Concepts & definitions
- **Three-candle pattern** — Candle 2 "pretty much creates the imbalance." It is usually a big displacement up or down, but the FVG can sometimes be "an itsy bitsy price range." [01:42–03:26]
- **Bullish FVG** — From the **top of candle 1's wick** to the **bottom of candle 3's wick**. Logic: no wicks traded into that range, so there were no resting sell orders there. [03:26–05:09]
- **Bearish FVG** — From the **bottom of candle 1's wick** to the **top of candle 3's wick**. [05:51–06:24]
- **Not an FVG** — If candle 1's and candle 3's wicks overlap, the "box would be inverted," so there is nothing to mark. [08:07–08:42]
- **"Buying power out of it"** — In the example, price revisits the bullish FVG and then a **bullish close** forms; price continues higher. He promises to explain more about identifying buying power later. [12:12–12:45]
- **Partial fill** — When a later wick has dipped into the gap, he notes "obviously it filled the fair value gap." The FVG is still drawn from the original 3 candles. [11:37–12:12]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Don't trade FVGs on their own ("you would lose billions"). Wait for the full toolbox. [11:02; 15:00]
- Not every FVG gets filled; a revisit is optional. [09:50; 13:21]
- Ignore tiny-wick noise ("pubic hair looking wicks"). Focus on clear big gaps. [14:27]

## Process / order of operations
1. Find a displacement candle.
2. Compare the wicks of the candle before and after it.
3. If there is a gap, box it (bullish: C1 high → C3 low; bearish: C1 low → C3 high).
4. On a revisit, look for a candle closing in the original direction.
