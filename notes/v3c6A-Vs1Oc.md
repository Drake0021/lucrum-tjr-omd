## Summary
A confluence lesson on inverse fair value gaps (IFVG). An IFVG is an FVG that gets "disrespected": price closes through it (a candle closes beneath a bullish FVG, or above a bearish one), and this does not have to be the next candle. TJR treats an IFVG as **equivalent to a break of structure**, often appearing before the BOS: a sign that the prevailing structure is weakening. So his core sequence becomes liquidity sweep → BOS **or** IFVG → FVG / breaker / OB / EQ entry. An IFVG alone does not flip bias; daily bias and other confluences are still required. Examples on the 4H, 15m and 5m (ES/NQ) show the IFVG giving an earlier confirmation than the BOS. He frames trading as identifying where price wants to go on every timeframe, not just entering.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Inverse fair value gap (IFVG)** — "literally just a fair value gap that gets disrespected", i.e. price closes through it. A bullish FVG with a candle closing underneath it → bearish IFVG; a bearish FVG closed above → bullish IFVG. The closing candle "doesn't even have to be the next candle". [05:09]–[06:17]
- **Meaning** — the market structure the FVG belonged to "is no longer holding up / starting to get weaker"; "similar to how a break of structure is a break of market structure". [06:17]–[07:20]
- **IFVG ≈ BOS** — "I pretty much treat these as equal to a break of structure"; "break of structure before break of structures even happen". [04:03]–[04:36] [07:20]
- **Fractal** — liquidity sweeps, OBs and IFVGs happen on every timeframe. Do not think execution is only allowed on the 15m/5m; he gets "really upset" when students miss this. [08:28]–[09:01]
- **Trading philosophy** — the goal is to identify where price wants to go on any timeframe at any time, not just "this plus this equals entry". [02:58]–[03:31]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Core TJR strategy (stated): liquidity sweep → break of structure → FVG / order block / breaker block fill → enter. [07:20]
- Updated: liquidity sweep → **IFVG or BOS** → FVG / breaker block / order block / another FVG / equilibrium → enter. [07:53]
- An IFVG (like a BOS) does **not** by itself flip the bias: "we have to have overall market sentiment, we have to have daily bias", and it is needed "in confluence with a lot of things". [10:07]–[10:42]
- Use the IFVG to confirm a structure shift after a liquidity sweep "without necessarily having to wait for a break of structure", on whatever confirmation timeframe you use. [12:30]–[13:03]
- An IFVG and a BOS on the same candle = "double confirmation" / extra confluence. [14:13] [15:54]
- Use both BOS and IFVG ("not only use IFVG or only BOS"): it gives more options. [17:45]
- An IFVG that forms before the BOS gives a better (earlier) entry. [17:13]

## Process / order of operations
1. Daily bias / overall sentiment. [10:07]
2. HTF liquidity sweep (e.g. 1H sweep, daily sweep). [11:51] [14:13] [15:54]
3. Scale down one level or more (daily → 4H, 1H → 15m, → 5m) and look for an IFVG or BOS. [11:51] [14:13] [15:54]
4. Then an FVG (or breaker / OB / EQ) retrace → enter. [14:45]–[15:17]
