## Summary
A motivational, psychology-only video. TJR gives four pieces of advice to his younger self: (1) you won't get rich quick, so expand your time horizon, because a get-rich mindset causes overtrading and overleveraging; (2) protect your capital, meaning demo-trade for months and don't fund live or prop accounts before you are skilled; (3) stop over-educating, and instead pick one strategy and get screen time and repetitions; (4) be consistent and don't set a profitability deadline or quit. There is no strategy content.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Get-rich mindset → bad actions** — a short timeline ("get rich this month to pay rent") leads to risking nearly the whole account per trade and overtrading. That only works with a 100% win rate, and "nobody has a 100% win rate ... it's all based on probability" [01:04–01:37].
- **Time horizon** — accept 2–4 years of work to change the rest of your life [04:25].
- **Edutainment / over-education** — after about a year of learning "you know everything that you need to know". Watching more strategy videos is entertainment, and there is no "secret pill" that makes trading click [09:54–11:37].
- **Screen time** — what made him good was "watching markets open every single day for hours", every session open and close, and how candles reacted to session opens and closes and to highs and lows [13:15–13:48].

## Risk & psychology rules
- Don't risk most of your account per trade, and don't overtrade [01:37].
- Trade demo/paper for months until you are confident. Two green demo days are not proof [06:06–06:37].
- "You are not going to be profitable within your first 3 months" [07:09].
- Don't trade with rent or food money; stop buying funded accounts until you are profitable [08:18–08:49].
- Expect the first live attempt to fail; return to demo rather than repeatedly losing [09:22].
- Pick one strategy that makes sense to you (why price reacts at levels), stick to it, and back-test and journal [11:37; 14:21].
- Don't set a deadline for profitability, because hitting it unprofitable leads to quitting. Persistence through the urge to quit separates the successful; he quit once and came back [14:21–15:26].
