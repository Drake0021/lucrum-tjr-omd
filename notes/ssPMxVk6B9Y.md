## Summary
A short no-trade day (Thursday 2025-05-22, unemployment-claims day) during a choppy week. Asia and London consolidated, London swept its lows and ES then took the Asia/London session highs before the open. He had no strong bias, did not want to short into a rising market ("rising knife"), and sat out. Most of the video explains why he de-risks after winning streaks: overconfidence after good weeks is what "kills traders". The recording of his pre-market analysis was lost, so chart detail is thin.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Chop week / bad price action** — a week with few clean setups, where people "blow" live and funded accounts; "every single day is different in the market" [03:30; 07:57].
- **Rising knife** — shorting right after price has taken session highs and the last draw on liquidity, when the move "very well could just be a five-minute retrace" [02:15–02:50].
- **HTF retrace on NQ** — NQ daily formed down candles and then a higher low; he reads it as a high-timeframe retrace, so a "solid low" on NQ. ES was "getting a lot more movement" toward the downside lows [01:10–01:43]. He compares the indexes but does not name SMT (inferred).

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- No strong bias → no trade. "I probably am not going to take a trade today" [01:10]; "I think I'm just not going to take any trades" [02:50].
- Don't short (or long) into a move that could be only a 5m retrace of the opposite direction [02:15–02:50].
- Targets for a bearish idea were ES lows and the new-week opening gap (NWOG) below. Those lows had already been hit overnight, which weakened the idea [00:36; 01:43].
- Don't FOMO into a sudden drop after deciding to sit out [04:01].
- After a strong run (two big weeks), de-risk and take fewer trades the following week/day [04:34; 06:15–06:49].

## Process / order of operations
1. Read Asia/London (consolidation, London sweep of lows, then legs up; Asia and London session highs taken on ES) [00:01–00:36].
2. Check whether the prior day's bias played out (it did: down to a series of lows) and what draws remain (last low, NWOG) [00:36].
3. Compare ES vs NQ on the HTF (NQ holding up as a HTF retrace; ES moving more) [01:10–01:43].
4. If there is no clean bias and the only trade would be against a fresh move, sit out [02:15–02:50].

## Confluences mentioned

## Trade examples
- 2025-05-22 | ES/NQ | NY open | no trade. He showed the platform with "zero trades taken" [07:21]. Reason: no strong bias, price above the Asia/London highs, and a short would be "catching a rising knife" [02:15–02:50].

## Risk & psychology rules
- Treat every day as a new day; don't scale risk up after winning streaks [04:34; 05:41].
- "What is the only thing that could potentially follow a winning streak? At least one loss", so it is "dumb to be increasing risk during a winning streak" [06:15].
- De-risk after a really big day or week [06:15–06:49].
- Being flat on a chop day is acceptable. He says he was up ~$6,000 on the week and later "break even on the week" (he contradicts himself) [02:50; 05:08].
- He'd be fine sitting out the rest of the week [05:41].
