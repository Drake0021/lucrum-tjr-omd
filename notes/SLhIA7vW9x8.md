## Summary
Part 2 of "putting it all together" (execution). TJR covers two of his bare-bones execution templates: (1) liquidity sweep + break of structure (BOS) → execute, and (2) liquidity sweep + BOS + order block (OB) entry → execute when price retraces into the OB. He runs through many chart examples (forex, GU, ES/S&P low timeframe, a daily example) showing price then drawing to the next liquidity. He stresses this is execution only — no stop-loss, take-profit or daily-bias logic yet — and assigns drilling homework: 10+ examples of each pattern. Next video: liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG entry, and sweep + BOS + FVG/OB + equilibrium.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Execution techniques as building blocks** — there are several execution strategies; you assemble building blocks (liquidity, BOS, OB, FVG, equilibrium) as you prefer. Plan for the series: (a) sweep→BOS, (b) sweep→BOS→OB, (c) sweep→BOS→FVG, (d) sweep→BOS→FVG/OB + equilibrium. [01:12], [01:46]
- **Liquidity sweep** — price takes out a prior high/low where liquidity rests (e.g., below lows). A sweep alone without BOS is not an execution. [04:36]
- **Liquidity sweep + BOS (base model)** — after liquidity is swept, wait for a break of structure in the opposite direction; that BOS is the execution point. Price then draws toward previous areas of liquidity (prior highs/lows) and imbalances. He calls this the simplest but riskiest / lowest-confluence version. [04:36], [05:10]
- **BOS via close** — in the OB section he describes the break as "we close below this low" (candle close beyond the swing). [10:20] (He doesn't restate wick-vs-body rule here; close is implied.)
- **Order block (in this model)** — the candle/leg that caused the liquidity sweep ("the candle that caused these highs to get taken out" / "the leg down that caused those orders to get filled"); i.e., the last opposing move before the BOS, the price range where orders were filled. [10:56], [11:32]
- **Draws on liquidity / targets** — building blocks are price ranges price is likely to draw back to; after execution price targets previous liquidity (old highs/lows), or e.g. the top of an imbalance (FVG). Take-profit method deferred to a later video. [05:10], [06:48], [07:24]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Model A: IF liquidity is swept AND a break of structure occurs in the opposite direction THEN that BOS is an execution point; expect price to draw to the next liquidity pools. [04:36], [09:02]
- Model B: IF liquidity sweep → BOS, THEN mark the order block (the leg/candle that produced the sweep); do NOT enter on the BOS candle — enter when price retraces into the order block. [10:56], [11:32]
- Entry inside the OB doesn't need to be exact — "doesn't matter if it's exact or not execute on this down wick" into the OB. [15:16]
- Sometimes the BOS candle itself is effectively the OB / gives the same entry. [15:50]
- An OB that never gets touched = no trade (price didn't retrace into it). [12:07]
- Execution alone is not a full strategy: must later add daily bias, stop-loss and take-profit logic; don't start placing many trades from this alone. [02:19], [05:42], [06:48]
- A sweep without a subsequent BOS → no execution (example where liquidity taken but no BOS). [04:36]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify liquidity (highs/lows) and wait for it to be swept. [04:36]
2. Wait for break of structure in the opposite direction (candle close beyond swing). [10:20]
3. (Model A) execute at BOS; (Model B) mark the OB that caused the sweep and wait for retrace into it, then execute. [10:56]
4. Expect price to draw to opposing liquidity / imbalance. [05:10]

## Confluences mentioned
- Daily bias — will filter these setups (e.g., why a long sweep→BOS fails when HTF liquidity is the draw); upcoming video. [05:42]
- High-timeframe liquidity — price can seek HTF liquidity, overriding a local BOS. [05:42]
- Imbalance (FVG) — as target ("target the top of that imbalance") and as next entry model. [06:48]
- Lower-timeframe refinement — can drop to 5m to see the OB candle on S&P. [17:02]
