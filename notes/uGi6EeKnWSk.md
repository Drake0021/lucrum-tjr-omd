## Summary
TJR teaches when NOT to trade a pair/day: when the setup is against the daily bias, when timeframes conflict (e.g., daily bearish but 4H/1H bullish with no building block or confirmation), and when price action is directionless chop. The ideal is a trending market where daily, 4H, hourly building block and LTF confirmation all align. Higher timeframes "hold higher power": a 1m/5m BOS must not change your daily bias. If not every item is checked, skip the pair or the day. The second half (~13:41 onward) is a long motivation/work-ethic tangent, ending with a reminder to review losses against what price actually did.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Morning pair selection** — ~30 minutes before NY market open he goes through all pairs with Discord and decides which look good vs not for the day. [01:10]
- **High-probability (A) trade alignment** — daily bias bullish + 4H BOS up + price takes out an hourly building block (sweeps liquidity/taps OB at NY open) + LTF confirmation (e.g., 5m BOS) in the same direction → "a trade I would want to take". [02:20], [02:53]
- **Conflicting timeframes (low probability)** — daily downtrend, 4H uptrend, 1H uptrend, price not near any building blocks, no LTF confirmation, "everything bullish on an overall bearish-bias pair" → avoid. [03:31], [04:06]
- **No sense of direction / chop** — repeated BOS that turn into liquidity sweeps, sweeps both ways, "psych" moves; a sign not to trade. Short-term scalps in chop (mark hourly liquidity, take a 1m BOS) are possible but not ideal. [04:39], [05:11], [07:01]
- **Trending markets** — "the easiest and the best and the highest probability markets": clean higher highs/higher lows (or the bearish equivalent). [06:28]
- **Higher timeframes hold higher power** — a 5m BOS can just be a 15m retrace; a 15m BOS can just be an hourly retrace. [12:00]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- First filter: the trade setup must be in line with your daily bias. If daily bias is bullish and a bearish setup appears, do not take it. [02:20], [08:40]
- IF daily bias is X but 4H is opposite and 1H is unclear/choppy THEN it is not a high-confluence/high-probability time — don't trade that pair. [08:05], [09:13]
- IF daily bearish with a 4H BOS down but the 1H/15m is chop THEN still don't trade it (example around 05:46–06:28). [05:46]
- Do not change your daily directional decision because of a 1m or 5m (or 15m) break of structure. [12:00]
- Execute on lower timeframes only to fulfill where price is going on the day/4H/1H, within the daily bias. If none of those checks are hit → don't trade this pair today, or don't trade today. [12:32], [13:06]
- If you trade only one pair and it doesn't meet the checklist → take the day off. [09:47]
- Don't trade news (red-folder) — he skips news days to increase probability. [08:40], [09:47]
- Go into the market not wanting to trade; take a trade only when the market gives you no other option. [11:27]

## Process / order of operations
1. Determine daily bias. [02:20]
2. Check 4H: has it broken structure in the daily direction? [02:53]
3. Check hourly: is price at/taking out an hourly building block (liquidity/OB)? [02:53]
4. Check LTF (e.g., 5m) confirmation BOS in the bias direction. [02:53]
5. Assess price-action quality: trending vs chop. [06:28]
6. All checked → trade; anything missing → skip pair (cycle to another) or skip the day. [09:47], [13:06]

## Risk & psychology rules
- "The best Traders take the most days off"; the more days you're in the market, the higher the statistical chance you lose. [09:47]
- Prefer a few high-probability, in-bias trades over 20 mediocre/against-bias trades (references a top trader taking ~8 trades a month). [10:21], [10:55]
- Beginners' losing trades often come from taking setups against the daily bias / in bad price action. [08:40]
- Don't chase the dopamine of trading. [11:27]
- Review losing trades against what price actually did afterwards; demo trade live and journal trade reviews — writing it down cements lessons. [24:53], [25:27]
- Work ethic: he studied 6–8 hours every morning; mediocre work → mediocre results; embrace failure (golf analogy). [14:15]–[24:18] (motivational, non-method)
