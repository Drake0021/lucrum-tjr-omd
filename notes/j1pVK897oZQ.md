## Summary
Theory-only introduction to equilibrium (EQ) after ~5 minutes of banter. EQ is the 50% level of a swing high→swing low (or low→high) range, used to split it into premium and discount; big money wants to buy in discount and sell in premium. TJR does not enter purely off EQ — he uses it as a gauge of "good price" and then needs an extra reason (order block, FVG, or a BOS while in discount). He recaps the full toolset and its order: liquidity sweep = inducement, BOS = first/primary entry, then retracement entries (order block, FVG, EQ), with "FVG within discount" called the perfect retracement combination. Chart examples are deferred to Day 28.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Equilibrium (EQ)** — a retracement tool showing where big money is likely to buy/sell again; measured from swing high to swing low (or low to high), EQ is the 50% mark of that distance. [05:16] [06:24]
- **Premium / discount** — in an uptrend (measure swing low → swing high), anything below the 50% mark is discount where big banks/market movers place more buy orders. In a downtrend (high → low), price above the 50% mark is the area to go short (premium). [06:57] [08:03]
  - Caption note: at [06:57] he literally says above 50% "is considered a discount to go short" — he means it's a good/"on sale" price for shorts, i.e. premium (standard term). (inferred)
- **Logic** — like shopping: buyers want a cheaper price, sellers want a higher price; banks avoid buying in premium. [05:50] [10:50] [11:24]
- **Toolset hierarchy (as of Day 26)** [09:10]–[09:43]:
  - Liquidity sweep = "the inducement".
  - Break of structure = "our first primary entry" (liquidity sweep + BOS = entry point; already indicates market structure shift).
  - If you miss it → first retracement entry point = order block (the move that caused the liquidity sweep); enter on a reaction off it "or even just hitting the order block".
  - Fair value gap = retracement tool: imbalance fills, reaction → enter.
  - Equilibrium = paired with an FVG — "honestly the perfect tools to combine" for a retracement.

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Do not enter purely off equilibrium; use it as a gauge that price is in a desirable zone, then require another reason: an order block, a fair value gap, or "even just a break of structure while in a discount — that's enough reason for me to get in". [08:03] [08:35]
- Long retracements should be in discount (below 50% of the swing low→high); short retracements in premium (above 50% of swing high→low). [06:57] [08:03]
- If you see an FVG within a discounted price and get a reaction → enter. [09:43]
- If the BOS entry offers bad risk-reward, don't take it; wait for a re-entry tool (OB/FVG). His recent live example: "if it hits the fair value gap and it gives us a reaction off of it I'll enter off that but if it hits the order block I'm instantly entering when that price point gets hit". [13:39] [14:13]
  - Note: this implies a distinction — FVG requires a reaction before entry; the OB was used as a limit-style entry on touch in that trade. [14:13]

## Process / order of operations
1. Liquidity sweep (inducement).
2. BOS → primary entry (if RR acceptable).
3. If missed / bad RR → wait for retracement into OB, FVG, and/or discount (for longs) / premium (for shorts) per EQ.
4. Enter on reaction / LTF BOS in the zone.

## Confluences mentioned
- EQ + FVG — "the perfect tools to combine" for a retracement. [09:43]
- EQ + OB, EQ + BOS-in-discount — sufficient. [08:35]
- EQ alone — not sufficient for him. [08:35]
