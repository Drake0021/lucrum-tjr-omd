## Summary
Foundational lesson defining the fair value gap (FVG / "imbalance") as a three-candle pattern with a gap between candle 1's and candle 3's wicks, explained via order-flow logic (lack of sell orders in a bullish FVG, lack of buy orders in a bearish FVG). FVGs are TJR's first "continuation confluence": in a trend, price retraces into the gap and continues. An FVG is invalidated (disrespected) only by a candle close beyond it (like BOS) — preview of IFVG. He gives detailed rules for stacked FVGs (all valid; once price uses any and pushes past the swing extreme, delete all of them) and for removing unused/old FVGs once the trend makes a new high/low — never "drag" old FVGs forward.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Price always moves to take liquidity or fill an imbalance** (recalled from liquidity lesson). [00:00]
- **FVG = imbalance** — same thing. [00:36]
- **Bullish FVG** — three consecutive candles; gap between candle 1's TOP wick and candle 3's BOTTOM wick, with an expansionary candle 2 (up). Only wicks matter; colors of candles 1 and 3 don't matter ("we do not see color"); only the middle (expansion) candle direction matters. [02:15] [02:49] [03:25] [03:58] [07:16]
- **Bearish FVG** — gap between candle 1's BOTTOM wick and candle 3's TOP wick, expansion candle 2 down. [07:48] [28:27]
- **Not an FVG** — if wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 overlap. [10:00] [10:33] [11:17]
- **Order-flow logic** — bullish FVG = lack of sell orders in the range; bearish FVG = lack of buy orders; when price retraces into it and fills orders, it moves easily in the trend direction. [05:03] [06:09] [08:54]
- **Continuation confluence** — FVG is used in an existing trend to identify continuation: price comes into the gap and you see buy (bull) / sell (bear) orders out of it. [06:09] [06:41]
- **Disrespected FVG** — a candle close beyond the gap (above a bearish FVG / below a bullish FVG) invalidates it — "almost just like a break of structure"; usually means trend changed (preview of IFVG). A wick through without a close does not invalidate. [15:47] [16:21] [17:30]
- **Stacked FVGs** — consecutive same-direction FVGs with no retrace (no opposite candle) between them; all are valid; the whole stack is one imbalanced price range. Price can close through upper ones and still use a lower one. [18:07] [18:40] [20:24]
- **Removing FVGs** — once price retraces (into any part of the stack, or not into the gap at all) and then pushes beyond the swing high (bull) / low (bear), the FVGs of that leg are spent/unneeded → delete them; do not extend old FVGs forward. FVGs apply "only within the current trend". [19:15] [19:49] [24:19] [24:54] [30:11] [31:54]
- **FVGs not obligatory** — like liquidity, price has no obligation to fill an FVG. [30:11]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Don't trade FVGs (or any single confluence) in isolation; need liquidity sweep → confirmation → continuation. [09:27] [15:14]
- FVG invalidation = candle body close beyond far edge; wick doesn't count. [17:30]
- Within a stack, a close through an upper gap doesn't end the trend; trend still valid while a lower gap holds and structure isn't broken. [20:24] [23:12]
- After price balances the stack and makes a new high/low, delete those gaps; later retraces into old zones are not FVG reactions (e.g. was actually a liquidity sweep of highs). [26:46] [27:21]
- Don't take trades off FVGs that have already been used. [27:21]

## Process / order of operations
1. Liquidity sweep (high/low taken). [13:36]
2. Confirmation via BOS. [13:36]
3. Continuation: retrace into an FVG created by the displacement, rejection candle out of it → entry in new direction. [14:08]
