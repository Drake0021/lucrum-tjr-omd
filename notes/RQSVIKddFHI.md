## Summary
Chart-walkthrough day for order blocks (OBs). TJR restates the definition — the whole up/down leg that swept liquidity immediately prior to the break of structure / market structure shift — and identifies many examples on forex charts (GBPUSD, GBPJPY, daily/4H/1H/15m). The core lesson is multi-timeframe: identify the HTF OB, wait for price to retrace into it, then scale down (1H → 15m) and wait for a LTF break of structure out of the OB to enter, placing the stop according to the timeframe you are actually playing and targeting HTF liquidity and imbalances. He shows one GBPUSD daily example yielding roughly 1:12 RR and stresses patience. Homework: find 5 OB examples on 3 timeframes.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Order block (OB)** — the move prior to (and that causes) the liquidity sweep, immediately before the break of structure. In a reversal down: the up-leg that swept the highs, before price broke structure down, is the bearish OB. In a reversal up: the down-leg that swept the lows, before price broke structure up, is the bullish OB. It is a price range, not necessarily one candle. [01:05] [01:37] [02:10]
- **Why OBs work** — orders were filled within that price range on the liquidity sweep, enabling the BOS; when price retraces, "they" want to fill more orders at the same range and push price in the intended direction again. [02:10] [02:43] [05:39]
- **OB = the whole leg** — "it's the whole up move prior to the move down." On a HTF it may look like one candle (precise enough sometimes), but the real OB is the entire leg, which can be a large price range; that is why you scale down. [06:47] [07:21] [07:57]
- **OB relative to MSS** — restated at the end: the move down or up prior to the market structure shift that changes the direction. [22:23]
- **OB as retracement area** — once structure breaks, he instantly identifies "the leg down that caused the move up" as the price range price will probably draw back into — his retracement area. [24:39]
- **OBs exist on all timeframes** — easier to find on 4H and lower because there are more of them. [03:49] [06:47]
- **OBs don't always get hit** — sometimes price doesn't quite reach the OB and fills "off of other things"; accept that. [18:48]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Identify the HTF BOS → identify the leg (OB) prior to the BOS that swept liquidity → wait for price to retrace into that OB. [04:24] [11:27]
- Entry: once price is in the HTF OB, scale down (1H, then 15m) and monitor the LTF swing highs (for longs) / lows (for shorts); enter on the candle close that breaks structure in the trade direction. [13:09] [20:42] [22:56]
- Each LTF high that is not broken = "nothing"; keep waiting until a high is actually broken (BOS) before entering. [13:09] [20:42]
- Stop-loss must fit the timeframe of the play: if the setup is a 4H BOS/4H OB, a tight stop from a 1H BOS will get stopped out — put the stop beyond the whole HTF OB/swing ("all the way up here"). [09:08] [09:42]
- Stop options on a LTF entry: under/above the LTF swing that formed the BOS, or beyond the OB itself "if you want to play safe"; he says "it doesn't really matter" which in one example. [20:42] [22:56]
- Targets: previous areas of liquidity on higher timeframes (TP1, TP2, TP3 laddered). [09:42] [21:17] [22:56]
- TP2 can be anywhere within an imbalance (FVG), e.g. its 50%; TP3 at HTF highs. [13:09] [13:44]
- If price completely disrespects an imbalance (pushes straight through), don't rely on it; use the next liquidity instead. [11:27] [17:03]
- Session filter: don't trade pre-market New York; mark NY open (9:30) and wait for it. For a London-session setup, wait for London open first, then for price to come into the OB. [12:35] [15:57]
- Patience over chasing: if entering on the initial liquidity sweep + BOS would put your fill far away (poor RR), wait for price to retrace into the OB for a much better RR. [25:13]
- Acceptable alternative: for a daily play, entering off the 1H BOS (less precise than 15m) is fine and "probably better" for patience/stop room. [15:25]

## Process / order of operations
GBPUSD daily example [10:54]–[14:51]:
1. Daily downtrend; identify the down-leg before the daily BOS = daily OB (wide price range).
2. Daily closes back up; 4H BOS confirms the retracement you want.
3. Look for targets inside the range (imbalance — disrespected; 4H liquidity).
4. On 1H, price takes out lows (liquidity), then 1H BOS up. Don't enter with a huge stop there.
5. Identify the 1H down-leg before that 1H BOS = 1H OB; wait for price to fill into it.
6. Scale to 15m; wait for NY open (9:30) — no pre-market trading.
7. Monitor 15m highs; enter on the 15m BOS.
8. TP1 liquidity; TP2 within the imbalance (e.g. 50%); TP3 HTF highs. ~1:12 RR.

## Confluences mentioned
- Liquidity sweep preceding the OB — the OB is defined by it; central. [05:39] [19:22]
- Fair value gap within the move — used to refine the entry: wait for price to come back into an FVG that got respected, and enter when it fills and breaks back above; an FVG that got disrespected (no reaction) is ignored. [16:30] [17:03]
- Imbalance/FVG as target (50% of it). [13:44] [21:17]
- Session timing (London open / NY open 9:30). [12:35] [15:57]
- News — one example "looks like it was probably off of news but it's still applicable". [04:58]
