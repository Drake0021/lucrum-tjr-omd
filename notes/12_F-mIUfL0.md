## Summary
Final order block (OB) lesson. After ~6 minutes of banter, TJR summarizes OBs (leg up/down prior to the BOS, where orders were filled on the liquidity sweep), then explains how he draws them: beginners should box the entire leg; he personally boxes only from the wick to the start of the body (the "base"), because price often reacts right at the base of the body. He walks through many examples (forex pairs, gold, pound pairs) showing liquidity sweep → BOS → retrace into OB → LTF BOS entry → targets at previous liquidity, and lists the retracement tools available so far: FVG and order block (equilibrium to come). He also notes OBs can be used as take-profits. (Transcript slang: "cocky blocky", "order [ __ ]" = order block.)

## Concepts & definitions
- **Order block (summary)** — the leg up or leg down prior to the break of structure to the upside/downside; the price range where orders were filled, i.e. the move that causes the liquidity sweep; then price breaks structure showing a market structure shift. [06:58] [07:34]
- **Why price returns** — market makers/banks filled orders there on the liquidity sweep; when price returns into that range, they can fill orders again and there are participants willing to take the opposite side; price then continues in the MSS direction. [08:08] [08:40]
- **Best OBs come with a liquidity sweep** — "it's almost always best when we see a liquidity sweep as well". If you see a liquidity sweep and then a leg higher, you should assume there is an OB there. [09:47] [23:20]
- **How TJR boxes an OB** — from the wick of the candle down to the start of the body (the wick portion / "base of the body"); price often taps just the wick area or "literally taps the base", where the body starts — "usually that's like the reaction point" (market-experience observation, he can't explain why). [10:20] [10:54] [11:27]
- **How beginners should box an OB** — box the entire leg down prior to the move up (or entire leg up prior to the move down) — "big ass box". [10:20] [25:42]
- **Retracement tools (as of Day 24)** — fair value gap, order block, and equilibrium (not yet taught). [16:28]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Entry: after price taps into the OB, wait for a break of structure (confirmation = BOS showing a market structure shift) in the trade direction, then enter; targets at previous areas of liquidity. [11:58] [13:06] [17:02]
- Confirmation definition: "what's our confirmation a break of structure that shows market structure shift". [17:02]
- If price doesn't break structure at the first retracement tool (e.g. FVG), that's fine — wait at the next tool (OB) for confirmation. [17:02]
- Refined entry option: after the HTF confirmation BOS, you don't have to enter on it; you can enter on a further retracement into a smaller OB or FVG off a 15m BOS within it, stop "wherever you want", targeting far HTF liquidity for large RR. [17:36]
- OBs can be used as take-profit levels. [13:39]
- Bias filter: an OB long is especially strong "if it's in line with our daily and our weekly bias". [19:58]
- On the hourly, once HTF bias is bullish (daily BOS + 4H BOS up), wait for any bullish confirmation (1H BOS up), find the 1H OB, wait for price to come in, then long on the BOS. [20:32] [21:06]
- If you're already inside the OB price range when the BOS forms, you can enter, or wait for price to go higher then retrace into it ("double confirmation"), or use an imbalance within it and find an entry on the 15m. [24:29] [25:06]
- If the OB range is very large, scale down to find a tighter entry (he notes you can but skips it in one example). [22:48] [23:20]

## Process / order of operations
Daily-bias OB example [19:58]–[21:42]:
1. Daily bias shift: daily BOS up on a big move.
2. Identify the daily OB (leg down prior to the move up); price pushes into it — "this isn't enough in itself".
3. Get 4H BOS up off it.
4. On 1H with bullish bias, wait for 1H BOS up; locate the 1H OB within the move.
5. Price comes into the 1H OB → BOS up → long.
6. Targets: liquidity levels above, progressively further.

- Base of the candle body as the reaction point. [11:27]
