## Summary
A dedicated inverse fair value gap (IFVG) lesson. TJR says IFVGs are one of the key confluences behind his seven-figure year. He defines an IFVG as a candle closing through a with-trend FVG: the trend's continuation signal has been "disrespected", which he reads as a market structure shift that often comes before the BOS. He adds a rule for stacked FVGs: consecutive same-direction FVGs with no opposite-colour candle between them count as one, and only a close beyond the far edge of the whole stack counts as the inversion. He ends by restating his whole strategy as liquidity sweep → IFVG (or BOS) → FVG fill in the new direction → entry. The chart examples are unlabeled; the instrument is likely ES/NQ (not stated).

## Concepts & definitions
- **Fair value gap (FVG)** — a three-candle pattern. A bullish FVG normally appears in an uptrend and is a **continuation confluence**: if price comes back into it and buy orders show, the trend on that timeframe continues and targets the high just made ("internal liquidity → external liquidity") [00:33–01:39].
- **Inverse FVG (IFVG)** — a with-trend FVG that is **disrespected** by a **candle close beyond it**. Bullish FVG + a candle **closes below** it = bearish IFVG (a bearish confluence). Bearish FVG + a candle **closes above** it = bullish IFVG [02:44–03:50; 04:58–05:31]. The inversion can come on "the following candle or really any candle after that" [02:44]. A close is required ("candlestick closure underneath this continuation confluence") [02:44].
- **IFVG = market structure shift** — "That's telling us hey, that's a break in market structure" [03:16]. It identifies "market structure shifts before we even can see a break of structure", so the entry comes earlier than waiting for a close under the swing low [03:50–04:23; 08:54].
- **Naming** — the IFVG is named by its new direction. A bullish FVG that gets inverted is a **bearish** IFVG [04:23].
- **Stacked FVGs** — two or more same-direction FVGs in one leg "without a different candlestick in between" (no opposite-colour/retrace candle) are "treated as one". To qualify as an inversion, price must close beyond the **far edge of the whole stack**. For bullish stacks, use the lowest gap; for bearish stacks, use the top of the highest gap [10:00–10:35; 11:39–12:45]. Reason: a close above only the lower part of a bearish stack, followed by a bearish close back out, still "maintains bearish order flow" [11:39–12:12]. A single gap in the stack can still be used as a normal continuation FVG; only the inversion needs the full stack [12:45].
- **Context requirement** — IFVGs are reversal confluences and work best **after a liquidity sweep** / at a higher-timeframe draw on liquidity. "Every single one of these examples ... has been coming off of a liquidity sweep." Don't flip trend just because an FVG failed [05:31–06:37; 13:50].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- IF price sweeps a significant HTF draw on liquidity (high or low) AND a lower-timeframe FVG in the old direction is closed through (IFVG) → expect a reversal toward the next draw on liquidity in the new direction [06:05–07:08].
- IF price comes into the FVG and respects it (closes back out in the trend direction) → continuation toward the next draw on liquidity in the trend direction [06:37; 08:18].
- An IFVG is valid only after a **candle close** beyond the gap. With stacked gaps, the close must be beyond the whole stack [02:44; 10:35; 12:12].
- Do not take an IFVG without a liquidity sweep / order-fill context [13:50].
- Where a BOS prints before the IFVG, "it would have been better to go with the break of structure" [13:18]. Use whichever shift signal comes first (inferred).
- After the shift (IFVG or BOS), look for **continuation of the new trend**: an FVG in the new direction gets filled → enter [14:24–14:56].
- Targets: the next highs/lows ("take out this high, this high, this high") [08:18; 09:27]. Example continuation: retrace into equilibrium → push past the high [12:45–13:18].
- He would "be scaling in" after the IFVG in his first example [08:54] (vague).

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify a potential order fill: a liquidity sweep of a significant high/low [14:24].
2. Confirmation the orders were filled = market structure shift: an IFVG (close through the opposing FVG, or through the whole stack) or a BOS [14:24–14:56].
3. Wait for continuation of the new trend: a new FVG in the new direction gets filled [14:56].
4. Enter; target the opposing draws on liquidity [14:56].
