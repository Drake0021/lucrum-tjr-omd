## Summary
A post-trade recap and Q&A (March 2026, during TJR's 30-day live mentorship of "Tim"). It contains the clearest 2026 statement of his whole strategy as four steps. (1) **Opportunity for orders to be filled** (a liquidity sweep). (2) **Confirmation orders were filled** (a change in order flow: BOS or IFVG). (3) **Continuation confluence** (retrace into an FVG or equilibrium). (4) **Lower-timeframe confirmation of the extension** (a 1m BOS or IFVG), then execute and target draws on liquidity. He walks the day's long through those steps: open → high swept → 5m BOS down → no proper continuation → the bearish gap was inverted, HTF lows had been swept with a bullish SMT (ES under the lows, NQ held) → waited for the retrace to EQ → 1m IFVG → long. He explains that SMT only strengthens bias and selects the index, and that a sweep can come from pre-market or the prior session. He declares order blocks and breaker blocks "completely useless" because EQ covers them. The long "oil" segment (32:05+) is sarcastic trolling of chat, not method.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Step 1 — Opportunity for orders to be filled = liquidity sweep** — "orders are resting underneath lows and above highs." Pushing past a low alone isn't a reason to go long, because "how do downtrends work? We push past lows continuously" [04:57–05:30].
- **Step 2 — Confirmation that orders were filled = change in order flow** — "either break of structure or inverse fair value gaps ... that's telling me these orders were probably filled ... because we're changing order flow on the lower time frames" [05:30–06:03].
- **Step 3 — Continuation confluence** — "I want to show proof that this new trend is going to continue ... by waiting for a continuation confluence ... fair value gaps and equilibrium" [06:36–07:09]. Not an entry by itself: "Once equilibrium gets filled? No ... this could just be a high time frame retrace" [07:09].
- **Step 4 — LTF confirmation of extension** — "scaling down to the lower time frames and seeing either a break of structure or an inverse fair value gap on the lower time frames ... And then that's when we execute ... target lower or higher draws on liquidity" [07:42–08:15].
- **"Bunk" continuation confluence** — if price pushes through the continuation FVG and inverts it, the prior direction's continuation has failed and becomes a signal the other way [09:20].
- **SMT divergence** — "SMT divergence just strengthens our bias ... It doesn't tell me to take a trade, it doesn't tell me to execute ... I use it to determine what index I should take the trade off of" [10:59–11:32]. Bullish SMT example: "ES pushed underneath these lows while Nasdaq stayed above these lows" [10:59]. Without SMT he'd demand more confluence (e.g. wait for an FVG fill) [11:32]. "An SMT divergence means nothing until we get proper confirmations" [25:59–26:32].
- **Order blocks / breaker blocks: rejected** — "No order blocks. When was the last time that you heard me use an order block? Order blocks are completely useless ... When order blocks get filled, what else is typically getting filled? Equilibrium. When breaker blocks are getting filled ... Equilibrium" [14:18–14:50].
- **Continuation trade's liquidity** — "You can't take a continuation trade without liquidity. It's just the liquidity sweep happens during pre-market or during the previous session" [35:01].
- **Course content list** — his YouTube series covers SMT, EQ, FVGs, "advanced imbalance concepts" (NDOG, NWOG, candle gaps), BOS, liquidity, time theory, daily bias, funded accounts [13:45].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Never enter right after the sweep + order-flow change: "We're already at the bottom of a new extension ... why would I want to enter right here when I could wait for proof that this trend is going to continue" [06:36].
- Entry requires all 4 steps; the trigger is a 1m BOS or 1m IFVG out of the continuation confluence (FVG/EQ) [07:42–08:15; 09:53].
- The first SMT trade (earlier) was "bad" because "we didn't get confirmation that we were extending out of the continuation confluence" [25:59].

- Index selection: the SMT decides the index. The bullish SMT here meant ES swept the lows while NQ held, and the student traded NQ (the index that held, i.e. the stronger one). (Note: the canonical framework says "trade the index that failed to make the new extreme"; here NQ failed to make the new low and NQ was traded, which is consistent. Caveat: Tim "only trades Nasdaq" [23:49], so the choice is confounded.)
- Don't short right next to a big HTF draw above: Tim's day-1 loss was shorting "when we were really close to a high ... high time frame draw on liquidity to the upside" [20:00–20:32].
- Targets: draws on liquidity (higher highs); Tim capped at a $6K profit target for prop consistency rules [01:39].
