## Summary
A Sunday motivational talk with no chart content. TJR's main trading point is to judge results over a year rather than by day or week. One losing trade or day is like one bad golf shot, and the rest of the week, month and year can make it up. He says that after a loss he stops trading for the day, journals, does his recap and walks away. The rest of the video is life advice: meditate for 30 minutes, journal your thoughts and distractions, check your screen time and learn to be comfortable alone. He also says that higher timeframes carry more weight, which parallels the long-horizon mindset.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Long-horizon performance view** — Judge performance by the year (and month and week), not by single trades or days. "Higher time frames hold a higher power" applies to the charts and to how you view your results. [01:08]
- **Golf analogy** — One bad shot can be made up on other shots, and one bad hole has 17 others. In trading, a bad day has the rest of the week, a bad week has three other weeks in the month, and a bad month has 11 other months. [01:42]–[02:14], [04:29]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- After a loss, stop trading for the day. Do not take more trades to win the money back. "When you lose in the market you just have to be comfortable with saying I'm done for today." [03:56], [08:53]
- After a win, do not keep taking trades to make even more money. [01:08]
- After a loss, still journal the trade, do a recap and work out what you could have done better, then let it go. [08:20]
- On winning days he stays on longer only because his take profits are extended and the trade is still running. On losing days his day ends early. [07:49]
- (Implied) Do not look for trades in Asian session on Sunday, such as a GU 5-minute long at the Sunday Asian open. He mocks this. [06:08]

## Process / order of operations
- Homework, not trading-specific:
  1. Meditate for 30 minutes in a dark room, sitting up with your eyes closed. [09:58]–[10:30]
  2. Journal where your thoughts went during the meditation. [17:53]
  3. List your biggest distractions and check your phone screen time. [17:53]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Do not revenge trade after a loss. You are likely to win it back on a later day, and the odds favour more winning days than losing days in a week. [03:56], [07:14]
- Accept that losses are a normal, expected part of trading. Do not judge a trader, including yourself, on a single trade. [05:01]
- Use the time freed by a loss to clear your head. [08:20]
- Remove distractions such as dopamine apps and screen time so you can focus on yourself. [18:27]
