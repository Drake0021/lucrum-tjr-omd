## Summary
A pre-market and open coaching session. TJR mentors a student (a friend on an "Evo" prop evaluation; the caption names "Marwan") on ES/NQ on a Monday after "war news". TJR teaches "order flow" thinking. The current trend holds until a close beyond the protective high/low or an inverse FVG proves otherwise. A sweep of a significant low only gives price the *opportunity* to reverse. SMT is not valid until order flow changes. Continuation trades need only two confluences: an FVG or equilibrium. Pre-market "high time frame manipulation" means he looks for lower-timeframe (5m) manipulation at the 9:30 open, and he prefers not to trade the first 10–15 minutes. Most of the live segment is music. The student's losing trade (the title's $6,000 loss, which blew the eval per chat) is not narrated. TJR mentions his own trade only afterward (stop under a low, "could have lost 20 bands").

## Concepts & definitions
- **Order flow (bearish/bullish)**: the current trend on a timeframe. It stays valid until price closes beyond the swing that protects it. "For us to change order flow on the hourly, we have to close above this high. Anything below this high is still valid for bearish order flow." [04:24]
- **Sweep = opportunity, not signal**: taking out a significant draw on liquidity (DOL) against the trend gives price "the opportunity to reverse, but we have to wait until price actually proves that it is going to reverse". Until then you stay with the current order flow. [03:21; 04:59]
- **Change in order flow / reversal confirmation**: proven by a **break of structure** (close beyond the high) **or an inverse fair value gap**. "How are orders filled? Through liquidity sweeps. How can we confirm that they're filled? Through inverse fair value gaps and break of structure." [01:38; 17:14; 22:11]
- **Continuation confirmation**: after the reversal, look for the new trend to show continuation, i.e. a retrace into equilibrium or an FVG and then displacement in the new direction. "All of those things are confirmation that the trend is continuing and then that's when I'm going to be looking to take a trade." [17:47]
- **Respect/disrespect of PD arrays**: in a downtrend, look only for bearish PD arrays (FVG/EQ) to be respected, and for bullish PD arrays to be disrespected (closed through). A bullish FVG is used only "in the opposite direction", i.e. as an IFVG. [01:05–01:38; 10:03]
- **The decisive gap**: an HTF (hourly) bullish FVG is "the one and only bullish confluence" confirming orders filled below. Staying in it and forming bullish structure = bullish. A close beneath it is "1,000% confirming" bearish continuation toward the lows. Because the hourly is slow to close, watch the 15m/5m for the same read. [19:26–20:33]
- **SMT (ES vs NQ)**: one index takes a low/high and the other doesn't. It is only a *potential* SMT until an actual change in order flow confirms it. NQ can simply lag and take the low later. Confirmed bullish SMT example: sweep lows, break structure up, respect a couple of bullish gaps, while filling an hourly FVG. [08:24–09:32; 21:06–21:38]. At the open: "Nasdaq is above this high, ES is below it. SMT? Potential." It becomes an SMT only if ES fails to push above the high and then rotates down, disrespecting bullish PD arrays. [32:32–33:03]
- **HTF manipulation vs LTF manipulation**: he always looks for a trade "off of high time frame manipulation" (e.g. a sweep of an hourly high). That happens either at the NY open or already in pre-market. If it happened in pre-market, he still expects some manipulation at the open, but on a lower timeframe. "The lowest time frame that I'll go to is like the 5-minute" (e.g. a sweep of a 5m high). [11:07–12:47]
- **High- vs low-resistance liquidity**: highs left after the HTF manipulation and displacement are "high resistance liquidity" (orders filled there, price unlikely to trade back up). The cluster of small pre-market highs/lows is "low resistance liquidity". [12:13–12:47]
- **Displacement-candle CE rebalance**: the student asks whether a retrace to the CE (50%) of a displacement candle, with no FVG, is a valid continuation entry. TJR, in a muddled answer, says "Yes" (vague). His stated preference: "literally just two forms of confluences, whether it's a fair value gap or equilibrium" for continuation trades. [14:27–16:08]
- **Power of three (AMD)**: "I want you to forget about power three." Think only about where orders are filled (sweeps), how fills are confirmed (IFVG/BOS), and continuation. [22:11]
- **New-trend leg count**: after a new trend starts, expect "one, two, three, and if we're lucky four retraces" before reaching a far draw. A new trend makes it easier to push in the trend direction than to break its order flow. [28:02–28:36]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- IF the HTF (hourly/4H) is bearish and a significant low gets swept, THEN stay bearish until an hourly close above the protective high (or an IFVG up). Do not buy the sweep. [03:52–04:59; 06:38]
- IF the 15m breaks structure up while the hourly is still bearish, THEN it is only a retrace. Look for the 15m/5m bullish PD arrays to be disrespected (BOS down or IFVG on the 5m/15m) to confirm bearish continuation. [04:24; 05:33]
- IF the hourly closes above the high (order flow flips), THEN it is not too late. Wait for a retrace into an HTF bullish PD array (FVG or EQ), then enter. [06:06–07:14]
- Continuation entries use only an FVG or EQ as confluence. [15:33]
- Draw EQ on the hourly from the swing high down to the low to find the retrace zone in a downtrend. [03:52]
- Wait for the NY open even if HTF manipulation happened in pre-market. Then look for 5m-level manipulation (a sweep of a 5m high for shorts) and confirmation. [11:41–12:47]
- "Ideally, we don't take a trade within like the first 10–15 minutes" after 9:30. The first minutes of the open are "some form of manipulation". [18:52; 34:10]
- SMT is valid only after a change in order flow. Trade it only after a BOS/IFVG in the SMT direction (inferred: the index that failed to make the new extreme is the signal side; he frames it as "ES won't push above this high"). [08:59; 33:03]
- Be reactive. A big open move can "ruin that bias as a whole", so re-evaluate rather than marry the bias. [34:42]
- Entries are on the 1-minute ("I look for entries on the 1 minute"). [30:52]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify HTF order flow (4H, hourly) and the protective swing whose close would flip it. [10:36; 04:24]
2. Note significant DOLs that were swept: these are reversal opportunities, not signals. [03:21]
3. Mark the HTF PD arrays (hourly FVG, hourly EQ) and decide which outcome (respect or disrespect) confirms which bias. [19:26–20:33]
4. Note whether HTF manipulation has already happened in pre-market (an hourly high swept, then displacement). [11:07]
5. At the 9:30 open, wait (ideally 10–15 min) for LTF (5m) manipulation, then a BOS/IFVG in the bias direction. [12:13; 18:52]
6. Look for continuation (a retrace into FVG/EQ and displacement), then enter on the 1m. [17:47; 30:52]
7. Use ES vs NQ SMT only as a potential signal until order flow confirms it. [33:03]
