## Concepts & definitions

### Mindset / what trading is
- **Trading (definition)**: "predicting where price on any asset class is going to go with a high probability on a daily basis." Making money is a *side effect* of this skill. Never go to the charts thinking "I want to make money today", because that leads to FOMO entries at the open, overleveraging and too many trades. [20:26–21:32] [24:51]
- **Skill metrics**: judge yourself by win rate and risk-to-reward (combined, these decide whether you are profitable), not by daily P&L. Use a trade journal fed with broker statistics. [34:21–35:28]
- He wants a *reason* for every confluence (orders filled, continuation of trend). He rejects support/resistance and chart patterns because they don't explain why price moves. [14:53–15:58] [2:46:26–2:47:30] [6:31:15]

### Chart setup
- **TradingView** is the only live charting platform he uses. Free plan is fine. He has premium only for more indicators. He does **not use indicators to take trades**, only to help mark confluences (e.g., a session high/low indicator). [38:49–39:55] [1:56:02]
- **Candle colors**: he avoids green/red because they psychologically link to making/losing money. He uses blue (up) and black (down) on a plain white background with no grid. [42:46–43:54]
- **Time zone must be New York (Eastern)** whatever your location. All session and open times are in ET. [44:27–45:34]
- **Candlestick anatomy**: body runs from open to close. On an up candle the bottom of the body = open and the top = close. On a down candle it is reversed. Top of the upper wick = high, bottom of the lower wick = low. Each candle = one period of the selected timeframe. Lower timeframes show the same move in more candles (e.g., 1 × 4H = 4 × 1H). [48:22–59:30]

### Highs, lows, trends
- **High** = an up candle followed by a down candle. The swing high is the highest wick of those two candles. **Low** = a down candle followed by an up candle. The swing low is the lowest wick of the two. [1:05:11–1:06:18]
- **Uptrend** = higher highs + higher lows. **Downtrend** = lower highs + lower lows. Third state = consolidation/sideways. [1:04:02] [1:06:53–1:10:13]
- Trends can break, so "we're in an uptrend, press buy" is not a strategy. [1:10:13]

### Liquidity
- **Liquidity = resting orders.** The market needs orders filled to move. Liquidity is "the goat of confluences… where everything of our strategy begins… and ends." [1:15:14–1:18:05]
- **Where it rests**: above highs and below lows. **Above highs there are two sets of buy orders**: (1) breakout traders buying above the high and (2) short sellers' stop-losses. **Below lows there are two sets of sell orders**: breakout sellers and longs' stops. [1:22:30–1:28:35]
- **Smart money logic**: large players (market makers) need opposite-side orders to fill size (e.g., 1,000 contracts need 1,000 opposing contracts). So they push price above highs to sell into the buy orders there, and below lows to buy into the sell orders there. He calls this "reverse psychology". [1:30:18–1:35:23]
- **Key caveat**: a sweep only gives the *opportunity* to reverse; it does not guarantee a reversal. Pressing sell above every high or buy below every low loses money. Other confluences must confirm. [1:40:54–1:44:13]
- **Internal vs external liquidity**: "price is always either looking to take out external liquidity which is highs and lows or fill imbalances which is internal liquidity" (FVGs and imbalances). [2:31:15–2:31:51] [2:45:16]

### Advanced liquidity / high-probability draws on liquidity (DOL)
He lists these as the "higher probability" draws, in place of any random high or low [1:47:06]:
1. **Session highs/lows** [1:48:14–2:05:36]. Sessions in ET:
   - **Asia 18:00–03:00**
   - **London 03:00–08:30** (he ends London at the NY pre-market open, even though London actually trades until 11:30 NY time [4:29:38])
   - **New York 08:30–17:00/18:00**: pre-market 08:30, cash open 09:30. NY has AM and PM sessions; PM starts around 13:00. [1:57:11] [2:05:03] [4:30:11]
   - **17:00–18:00 = "spread hour"**: no market, spreads widen at CFD/forex brokers. [4:30:44]
   - Logic: each new session's fresh money manipulates the prior session's high or low, then targets the opposite one. Typical sequence: London sweeps Asia low → runs Asia high; NY sweeps London/Asia high → runs London low. "It doesn't happen every single day." [1:51:36–1:54:21] [1:59:26]
2. **Previous day high/low (PDH/PDL)**: the highest and lowest points across all three sessions of the prior day. [2:05:36–2:08:58]
3. **Low-resistance liquidity = stacked highs/lows = "trend line liquidity" / "trend liquidity"**: a series of trending swing lows in an uptrend (or highs in a downtrend) that have not been swept. His "cookie monster" analogy: after the reversal, price keeps running through each one ("why would I stop there when there's another cookie"). These work as **targets** (exits) and also as **entries**: once a run of stacked lows has been taken, the sell-side is used up, so look to buy. [2:09:34–2:17:21] [2:19:01–2:21:14]
4. **Relative equal highs/lows (EQH/EQL)**: two highs or lows at the same or nearly the same price. Equal levels hold "four times the amount" of orders (two sets per swing). His example had lows 50 cents apart and he called that "close enough". [2:17:54–2:24:34]
5. **Data highs/lows (news candle highs/lows)**: the high and low of the candle printed on a high-impact news release (Forex Factory red folder, e.g., CPI, PPI and unemployment claims at 08:30 ET, ISM Manufacturing PMI at 10:00 ET). He uses them as draws/targets. His examples were weak and he admits it ("you're going to have to take my word for this"). [2:25:08–2:30:41]
- He says "seven" forms at the start [1:47:06] but explicitly covers the five above (session, previous day, low-resistance, relative equal, data). He describes relative equal as "pretty much the same thing as low resistance". (Count discrepancy noted.)
- All DOLs serve as **entry points** (sweep → reverse) **and exit points** (targets). Example: NY sweeps London high (entry) and targets London/session lows (exit). [2:17:21–2:17:54] [2:19:35]

### Break of structure (BOS)
- **Market structure**: uptrend = bullish structure, downtrend = bearish structure. **BOS** = the current structure breaks. [2:34:08–2:35:49]
- **Bearish BOS (in an uptrend)**: a **candle body close below the most recent swing low** (the most recent higher low). **Bullish BOS (in a downtrend)**: a **candle close above the most recent swing high**. [2:35:49–2:36:23]
- **Wicks don't count.** A body *equal* to the low does not count either; it must close beyond it. [2:38:03] [2:39:42] [2:41:57]
- In an uptrend, only monitor lows for a bearish break. A lower high does **not** break bullish structure. Each new swing low becomes the new level to monitor. [2:40:16–2:42:30]
- **Role**: BOS is a **confirmation confluence**. It confirms that orders were filled at the liquidity sweep. Typical use: HTF sweep, then drop to 5m and look for a 5m BOS. [2:43:04–2:44:44] [2:44:44–2:45:16]
- He states the model in one line here: "We look for potential for orders to be filled, confirmation that orders are filled, and then a continuation of the current trend that is now being created." [2:45:16]

### Fair value gap (FVG) / imbalance
- **Definition**: a three-candle pattern with an expansionary middle candle. **Bullish FVG** = gap between candle 1's **high (top wick)** and candle 3's **low (bottom wick)**. **Bearish FVG** = gap between candle 1's **low** and candle 3's **high**. Colors of candles 1 and 3 don't matter. Only the middle candle's direction matters. [2:46:26–2:53:08]
- **Why it works**: a bullish FVG is a range with a lack of sell orders, so when price returns and buy orders come in, it moves up easily. A bearish FVG is the mirror, with a lack of buy orders. [2:49:49–2:54:47]
- **Not an FVG**: when the candle 1 and candle 3 wicks overlap. [2:55:20–2:57:03]
- **Role**: a **continuation confluence**. In an established trend, price retraces into the FVG and you look for the trend to continue out of it. [2:51:31–2:52:35]
- **Disrespected / invalidated FVG**: a **candle close through the gap**. For a bearish FVG that means a close above it; for a bullish FVG, a close below it. A wick through it does not invalidate it. An invalidated gap usually means the trend changed (this leads into IFVGs). [2:59:16–3:02:08]
- **Stacked FVGs** (consecutive gaps in one move with **no retrace / no opposite candle in between**): all of them are valid. Price may close through the upper one and still respect a lower one. The whole stack is one imbalanced range. [3:02:41–3:07:10]
- **Delete used or ignored FVGs**:
  - Once price retraces into the imbalance and then breaks the swing high (bull case), the imbalance is "balanced". Remove all FVGs in that stack.
  - Also remove FVGs that price never retraced into before making a new high. Price showed it didn't need them.
  - Never drag old gaps forward to trade them later. FVGs are "only within the current trend." A later retrace into an old, already-balanced gap is a liquidity sweep, not an FVG reaction. [3:03:48–3:15:39]

### Advanced imbalance concepts
- **New Day Opening Gap (NDOG)**: the gap between the prior day's close (17:00 ET) and the new day's open (18:00 ET). Price tends to seek out and fill it. Use it as a target or entry, but it usually fills in Asia, so he rarely trades it. [3:16:47–3:19:34] [3:22:55–3:24:05]
- **New Week Opening Gap (NWOG)**: the gap between Friday's close and Sunday's open. Gaps are larger, and if unfilled by Monday it makes "a very very good target", or an entry inside the gap. He trades these "occasionally". [3:19:34–3:20:07] [3:24:05–3:26:58]
- NDOG/NWOG are "not required to get filled", but price "more often than not" seeks them out. Unlike plain FVGs, he treats them as strong draws on liquidity. [3:18:28–3:19:34]
- **New Candle Opening Gap**: a gap between one candle's close and the next candle's open on any timeframe. He calls it "pretty freaking useless". [3:20:42–3:22:55] [3:26:58–3:28:04]
- **BPR (balanced price range)**: an illiquid (imbalanced) range that price moved through swiftly in one direction and then swiftly back through the other way. When price re-enters an area of a rapid one-way move, expect it to move just as fast through the whole range. When price breaks the high or low bordering the range, expect a "rapid fire" move through it. Use it as a **target** (the far side of the range) or an **entry** (continuation after the range fills). [3:28:04–3:38:12]

### Inverse fair value gap (IFVG)
- **Definition**: an FVG that is **disrespected (candle body closes through it) within the current trend**. The inverted gap becomes a **confirmation confluence** of trend change, "as a break of structure". [3:43:53–3:45:00]
- **Why it matters**: it often prints **before** the BOS, which gives an earlier entry, a tighter stop and better RR. "I use this confluence almost every single day almost more than break of structure because more often than not it happens before break of structure." [3:46:41–3:50:37]
- Requires a full candle close beyond the gap, as with BOS. A wick through it or a close inside it doesn't count. [3:57:35–3:58:08]
- **Stacked FVGs rule**: for an IFVG to count, the **last gap holding up the trend must be inverted**. In a bullish stack that is the bottom gap; in a bearish stack it is the top gap (by symmetry, inferred). Closing through only the upper gap of a bullish stack "doesn't mean anything." [3:51:19–3:57:02]
- Once a bearish FVG is inverted, the new trend tends to form fresh FVGs that get respected on the way up. [3:58:08–3:58:43]

### Equilibrium (EQ)
- **Definition**: the 50% level of the current swing range. It splits the range into premium and discount. He draws it with a **Gann box**. [3:59:50–4:01:32]
- **Uptrend**: draw from the **most recent swing low to the most recent swing high**. Buy only in discount (below 50%). Don't bid above EQ. **Downtrend**: draw from the **most recent swing high to the most recent swing low**. Sell in premium (above 50%). [4:01:32–4:02:41] [4:04:20–4:07:09]
- He stresses "most recent". Do **not** anchor to an older low or high. Redraw as each new swing forms: you need the retrace to begin, e.g., the first up candle in a downtrend, before the swing low is defined. He gets angry about people marking it wrong. [4:04:20–4:06:35] [4:11:35–4:13:16]
- **Role**: a **continuation confluence**, like an FVG. "Completely and utterly useless… if it is not used with correct context." When EQ isn't reached, the FVG may be the one respected (and vice versa). Both often line up. [4:08:16–4:09:57]

### SMT divergence (ES vs NQ)
- Compares ES and NQ (correlated indexes) at the same swing points. It only matters **when a significant draw on liquidity is being swept**. Outside of that, divergences "show up all the time and will be pretty much useless." He doesn't know what SMT stands for. [4:13:52–4:18:57]
- **Bearish SMT**: one index makes a high then a **lower high** while the other makes a high then a **higher high** (sweeping the level). It is bearish for **both**. [4:18:57–4:20:05]
- **Bullish SMT**: one index makes a low then a **higher low** while the other makes a **lower low**. It is bullish for both. [4:20:40–4:21:12]
- **Leading vs lagging**: the index that *failed* to sweep is the **leading** index, already turning. **Trade the leading index** (the stronger one in your direction). Example: in a bearish SMT where NQ makes the higher high and ES the lower high, short ES. The leader also tends to expand further and faster. [4:24:09–4:24:42] [4:26:21–4:27:26]
- Used **for bias especially on HTF**, and also for execution on LTF (5m). [4:24:42]
- Doesn't apply to forex/commodities. [4:13:52]

### Time in the market (NY session for ES/NQ)
- He only trades the **New York session** for US indexes. [4:29:05]
- **08:30 pre-market**: pre-market manipulation is common. **09:30**: market open. [4:29:38–4:30:44]
- **09:30–09:50 = manipulation window**: expect some manipulation of a HTF or LTF DOL. [4:31:51]
- **09:50–10:10 = typical entry window**: "that's called the macro… typically the ideal entry time." [4:31:51–4:33:36]
- **10:30 cutoff**: "if I can't find a trade by 10:30, I'm done for the day because that's when the market tends to slow down." [4:33:36]
- **PM session ~13:00**: "Me personally, I don't trade this." [4:32:29]
- The windows are not rigid. He takes trades at 9:45 and 10:20 sometimes, and one example's manipulation finished at 9:52. [4:37:36]

### Market cycle (external ↔ internal)
- An uptrend moves high (external) → FVG/EQ (internal) → new high → FVG/EQ… A downtrend is the mirror. After a low is taken, expect a retrace to FVG/EQ. After the continuation confluence reacts, expect the next low to be taken. "It moves from external to internal." [5:23:30–5:25:09]

### Three skills
- Profitability needs **strategy + risk management + psychology**. **Psychology = discipline**: sticking to your trading plan (only trade when the strategy presents itself) and your risk plan (don't over-risk, overleverage or overtrade). [5:29:44–5:31:59] [5:50:56–5:51:29]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)

### Core model (stated as the "step-by-step TJR strategy") [6:24:36–6:27:56] [6:30:10–6:31:15]
1. **Opportunity (reversal confluence)**: price sweeps a significant high or low (a DOL), so orders have the potential to be filled.
2. **Confirmation**: a change in trend on the lower timeframe (5m) via **BOS or IFVG** confirms that orders were filled.
3. **Continuation**: price retraces into a **continuation confluence** of the new trend, either an **FVG or EQ** (premium for shorts, discount for longs).
4. **Entry trigger**: once price is inside the 5m continuation confluence, wait for proof it is reacting. Either:
   - (a) wait for the next **5m candle to close** in the trade direction, or
   - (b) **(his preferred method, "what the majority of other people do")** drop to the **1m**. On the 5m retrace the 1m usually shifts against you (BOS/IFVG in the retrace direction). Enter when the 1m shifts back in the trade direction via BOS or IFVG. [6:21:47–6:24:01]
5. **Never enter just because price touched the FVG/EQ** while the candle is still forming. Wait for the reaction or confirmation. [6:21:12–6:22:20]
6. **Stop**: above the highs of the setup for a short (beyond the swing that was swept / the retrace high, inferred from "above these highs") [6:24:01]. In the daily-bias examples he puts stops under the low of the candle/swing at the entry. [5:06:39]
7. **Targets**: exit at **liquidation points**, i.e., external liquidity where the filled orders can be offloaded: HTF highs/lows, session highs/lows (London/Asia), PDH/PDL, stacked low-resistance levels, or internal imbalances (NDOG/NWOG/BPR). He uses **multiple TPs** (TP1, TP2, TP3) at successive DOLs. [6:24:36] [6:29:35] [6:30:42]
- The step list is not meant to be rigid. He will sometimes use 1m instead of 5m, or a different confirmation, based on experience. Commenters calling that "a new strategy" is wrong in his view. He deliberately leaves room for discretion and leaves some pieces out "so that you go out and learn them yourself". [5:52:37–5:53:11] [5:59:48–6:04:49]

### Daily bias procedure [4:58:47–5:29:09] [6:05:58–6:13:54]
- No bias, no entry: "without daily bias, you don't know where the market's going to go… why are we entering in the first place." [4:59:53]
- Identify **HTF order flow on 4H and 1H** (uptrend or downtrend by BOS/IFVG). Find where price is in that trend: extending to external liquidity, or retracing into internal (FVG/EQ). [6:10:28–6:11:01]
- If price is retracing into a HTF continuation confluence (4H/1H FVG or EQ), then:
  - **respected**: the target is the external DOL in the trend direction;
  - **inverted (IFVG)**: bias flips toward the opposite DOLs. [5:01:33–5:02:40] [5:17:59–5:18:31]
- Was liquidity recently swept, or is it being swept now? A HTF sweep followed by a 1H IFVG or 1H close beyond structure gives a bias toward the opposite DOL. [5:04:23–5:07:44]
- **Mark all DOLs for the day**: PDH, PDL, Asia H/L, London H/L, hourly highs/lows. A session level that was already run with no reaction is "pretty useless". [6:09:19–6:11:35]
- If NY hasn't manipulated yet, **expect manipulation** of a nearby level (5m or hourly) before the move toward the bias target. Wait for it. [5:09:30–5:11:45]
- A bias is a working hypothesis. Let the market prove you wrong; e.g., an SMT plus a LTF BOS against the bias can be traded. "We can let the market prove us wrong and we can still make money." [6:27:56–6:28:29]
- Keep reassessing which key level must hold. If the expected lower high is broken (BOS up), drop the bearish FVG and target the highs. [5:25:51–5:27:31]

### Validity / invalidation rules
- BOS, FVG invalidation and IFVG all need a **candle body close**. Wicks don't count. [2:39:42] [3:00:24] [3:57:35]
- In a stack of FVGs, only inverting the **last** gap (the one holding the trend) confirms a reversal. [3:54:10]
- Delete FVGs once they have been balanced or bypassed. Never extend old gaps. [3:07:44–3:10:33]
- Draw EQ only from the most recent swing to the most recent swing. [4:04:55]
- SMT only counts at a significant DOL. Trade the leading index. [4:18:24–4:18:57] [4:24:09]

### Filters / timing
- Trade NY AM only: manipulation 9:30–9:50, entries 9:50–10:10, stop looking at 10:30. No PM session trading. [4:31:51–4:33:36]
- He skipped "the day before FOMC" as an example because it was "super ugly". A "high impact news day" example "isn't going to be super beneficial". [4:36:26] [5:16:14]

## Process / order of operations
1. Before the open: read **4H and 1H** order flow. Is price respecting or inverting the latest HTF FVG/EQ? Is it extending or retracing? [6:06:34–6:08:14] [6:10:28]
2. Mark DOLs: PDH/PDL, Asia H/L (18:00–03:00), London H/L (03:00–08:30), hourly highs/lows, equal/stacked levels, news-candle H/L, NDOG/NWOG. [6:08:46–6:09:54] [6:11:35]
3. Form the **daily bias** toward the most obvious DOL in line with HTF order flow. [6:09:54–6:10:28]
4. At/after 9:30, watch for **manipulation** (a sweep of a DOL) in the 9:30–9:50 window. Watch **ES vs NQ for SMT** at that sweep. [6:13:54–6:16:11]
5. On the **5m**, wait for **confirmation**: BOS or IFVG against the swept side. Use IFVG if an FVG exists in the leg; otherwise wait for BOS. [6:17:55–6:19:02]
6. Choose the instrument: the **leading index** from the SMT. [6:19:34–6:20:07]
7. Wait for the retrace into a 5m **continuation confluence** (FVG or EQ from the new swing). [6:19:02–6:21:12]
8. Drop to the **1m**. After the 1m shifts with the retrace, enter on the 1m shift back (IFVG/BOS) in the trade direction. [6:22:20–6:24:01]
9. Stop above/below the setup's swing extreme. Scale out at successive DOLs (TP1/TP2/TP3). [6:24:01–6:24:36] [6:29:35]
10. If no trade by **10:30**, stop for the day. [4:33:36]

## Trade examples
- **Liquidity illustrations** (various TFs; ES/SPX): the tariff flash crash swept multiple lows and became a "buying opportunity". COVID Feb 2020 on the monthly swept HTF lows, then rallied. 15m and 5m highs were swept, then price dumped. [1:39:13–1:40:54]

## Risk & psychology rules
- **Learn on a demo account** first. Don't use real money until you have practiced (backtest, journal). [26:31] [5:48:11–5:48:43]
- **Psychology = discipline**: only take trades when your strategy presents itself, and stick to your risk plan (no over-risking, overleveraging or overtrading). "If you want it bad enough, you will stick to your… strategy and… risk management plan." [5:31:24–5:34:13]
- Trading is not get-rich-quick. Skill comes first, then capital, then rewards. Wanting to get rich quick leads to off-plan trades and ramping up risk. [5:35:55–5:38:11]
- Analyze both wins (to replicate them) and losses (lessons) in a trade journal (e.g., Trade Zella). A strategy with a proven positive win rate and RR plus consistent risk equals profitability. [28:43–29:17] [5:34:13]
- **Position sizing (live accounts)**: he moved away from "risk 1% of balance per trade" to a **fixed number of contracts per trade** for NQ and for ES, chosen so that the largest typical stop is acceptable. The "sweet spot" is 1–3% risk per trade. A wide stop (about 35 ticks) may mean 3–4% risk on that trade, which he accepts because tight-stop trades risk less and usually have higher RR, so it evens out. [5:39:52–5:43:12]
- **Cut size in half** (a) when the stop is drastically larger than usual, or (b) when fundamentals/news (e.g., PPI) make the day choppy or uncertain. [5:43:46–5:44:20]
- **Prop firm risk**: rules differ by firm (trailing vs EOD vs static drawdown, size of drawdown, consistency rules on eval and payout). Read your account's rules and size for them. Common practice he describes: on the **evaluation**, risk near the maximum to pass in as few trades as possible. On the **funded/payout** account, scale risk back so you don't blow it on one trade. [5:44:20–5:47:39]
- Treat prop-firm fees as real money. Most people fail challenges, and you need skill first. [4:43:12–4:44:18] [4:48:14]

  - **Alpha Futures Advanced 100K**: minimum 2 trading days, 8% target ($8,000), max 10 contracts, max drawdown $3,500, $279 reset, no daily loss limit, **50% consistency rule** (so a minimum of about 3 winning trades), holding through news allowed, max withdrawal $15,000 vs an industry $1,200–$5,000.
