## Summary
A no-chart discipline video defining risk management as "how much to risk and when to risk it". TJR's rule is to risk 1–3% of the account. He first says this is per trade, then refines it to 1–3% total per day, split across the day's setups. He explains why small risk keeps you alive, warns against overtrading and over-leveraging, and says you should treat a small account as if it were $200K. Discipline matters more than the risk number itself. Position sizing is left to the viewer to work out with a position-size calculator.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Risk management** — "literally just how much to risk and when to risk it": how much you risk per trade and how often you trade. [00:35], [03:26]
- **Discipline > risk management** — Risk management is "the most important thing" only because it is hard to stick to with emotions involved. He calls discipline the real most important thing. [01:10]
- **Survival math** — At 1% risk per trade it would take 100 losses in a row to blow the account. Even losing 80% of trades at 1% leaves capital at year end. [05:07], [05:40]
- **"Living to trade another day"** — The purpose of the risk cap. There will always be setups tomorrow. [11:48]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Risk 1–3% of account size per trade. [03:26]
- Refined, preferred rule: risk 1–3% of the account per day in total. Examples: 2 trades at 1.5%, 1 trade at 1%, 1 trade at 3%, or 3 trades at 1%. [10:41], [11:14]
- Some days have no setups, so you risk 0% that day. [11:14]
- You may "go heavier" on risk (within the 1–3% daily cap) when "all the biases are aligning". He says this will be covered later. [11:14]
- Size the position from the stop distance (futures points, forex pips, options) using a position-size or lot-size calculator. He assigns this as homework and does not explain it. [04:00], [04:34]
- Set a maximum number of trades per day in advance and never exceed it. Never risk more than your set amount. [07:54]
- Treat your account as if it were large (for example $200K): no 20–30% risk per trade and no 10 trades a day. [09:00], [09:32]
- Don't put risk on before you know how to trade (learn what you're entering with before learning entries). [15:41]

## Process / order of operations
1. Decide the daily risk budget (1–3% of the account).
2. Decide the maximum number of trades per day.
3. For each setup, compute position size from stop distance and the risk allotted to that trade.
4. Stop when the budget or trade count is used up.

## Confluences mentioned
- "All the biases aligning" is mentioned as the reason to size up within the cap. Details are deferred. [11:14]

## Trade examples
- None on chart. He mentions days making up to about $60K and a $27K one-trade loss about two weeks earlier, handled without emotion. [07:21]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Expect to lose money in year 1 and probably year 2 ("college tuition"). [02:18]
- Going from a profitable demo to live includes a losing in-between period caused by emotion. [02:54]
- When emotions come in, overtrading, taking more trades than planned or risking more follows, and then "you're screwed". [07:54]
- When you make a mistake, recognise it and never repeat it (the boss's "one more chance" analogy). [08:27]
- Get rich slow. Over-leveraged "one trade to get me out of the hole" trading digs the hole deeper. [06:13], [14:35]
- Focus on the skill, not the money. [13:30]

## Changes vs. earlier teaching / contradictions
- Inside this video he moves from "1–3% per trade" [03:26] to "1–3% per day" [10:41], and the per-day version is the one he recommends ("makes it the easiest thing possible"). Treat 1–3% per day as the governing cap.
