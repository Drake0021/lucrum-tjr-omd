## Summary
Companion to the earlier "how to take a loss" day: TJR argues a win must be treated exactly like a loss — emotionlessly, in probabilities/percentages, and as something to review and journal. Winning leads to overconfidence, greed, over-leveraging and over-trading, which is why he advocates one trade a day and then stopping. He gives a model "good day" journal entry that summarizes his full top-down process (weekly/daily/LTF bias alignment → EQ/FVG/OB → MSS/BOS entry → liquidity targets → BE management). Roughly the second half (~22:00 onward) is personal story / motivation (mental-health history, why he makes the boot camp) with no trading content.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Taking a win** — treat a win the same as a loss: emotionless, just a percentage gain / probability outcome; review why you won exactly as you would review a loss. [01:42] [06:43] [15:42]
- **Thinking in probabilities/percentages, not dollars** — evaluate results by % gained, win rate, monthly/yearly performance; dollar amounts drive emotional decisions. Example: $10M account, 1% risk = $100k; at 1:1 RR you make $100k — closing early at +$50k because of the dollar figure is acting on emotion, not plan. [02:48] [03:21] [07:16]
- **"Lucky win"** — a win where you cannot explain why you won; he claims this is common for learners and is dangerous because it breeds overconfidence. [14:01]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Once in a trade, do not close it unless it hits take-profit or stop-loss (or your plan's management rule) — closing early because of floating dollar P&L is emotional. He cites holding $30k floating profit with stop at break-even rather than closing, "because I had to stick to my plan". [03:21] [03:56]
- One trade a day: take one trade, win or lose, and be done for the day. Prevents greed ("I could win more") and fear ("don't want to give profits back"). [04:29] [05:01]
- Do not increase risk after a win to "make more" — that is overconfidence. [04:29]
- (Heuristic, half-joking) If you feel the urge to screenshot a running trade, you should probably close it — he says his screenshots often marked the top. Vague; framed as an emotion check, not a mechanical rule. [05:35] [06:10]
- Don't watch the account balance; focus on the chart (TradingView), take the trade per plan and leave it; judge P&L at end of month / end of year. [11:15] [11:49] [12:56]
- When TPs are hit, move stop loss to break-even (stated as part of the model journal entry). [20:11]
- Set extended take-profits when all timeframe biases are aligned (from model journal entry). [19:38]
- Use previous areas of liquidity as take-profit targets. [19:38]
- If you cannot yet prove profitability, you should be demo trading. [07:16]

## Process / order of operations
His example of a "good day" journal entry (effectively a summary of the model as of Day 21) [19:38]–[20:11]:
1. Follow bias: check weekly timeframe, then daily timeframe — confirm they agree.
2. Scale down to a lower timeframe; confirm all three biases aligned.
3. Look for longs (in a bullish alignment).
4. Wait for price to move into a PD array: equilibrium, a fair value gap, an order block.
5. See a market structure shift, then a break of structure on the LTF → enter.
6. Set take-profits at previous areas of liquidity; because all biases align, set extended TPs.
7. Manage: when TPs are hit, move SL to break-even.
8. After the session, journal what you did right and why (and for losses, what went wrong and how to prevent it). [19:03]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Treat wins and losses identically; no celebrating, no "money spreading". [01:42] [13:29]
- Risk management: 1% risk example used. [02:48]
- One trade per day, win or loss, then stop. [05:01]
- Judge yourself on % and win rate over months/years, not daily dollars. [07:16] [11:15]
- Journal every win: why did I win, how can I repeat it; journal every loss: what went wrong, how do I prevent it — "write it down, say it out loud". [15:42] [19:03]
- Treat trading as a profession (wake early, journal), "learn the skill, not the money". [09:37] [14:35]
- Post/record P&L at end of every day including losses, not just wins. [08:27] [09:05]
