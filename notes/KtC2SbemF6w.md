## Summary
This video wraps up the liquidity series with chart examples on S&P 500 (daily), GBPUSD (4H/1H/5m) and gold. TJR defines liquidity as prominent highs and lows. Liquidity acts as a magnet for price and is where market makers fill and exit orders. Liquidity is used in two ways: (1) as the area where you wait for a sweep and then confirmation (BOS, an FVG/imbalance reaction, an order block) before entering, and (2) as the target, meaning opposing prominent highs or lows. Liquidity alone is not a strategy and you never trade blindly at a level. The homework is to find five liquidity sweeps and the confluence that followed each.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity** — Highs and lows in the market, where people enter longs and shorts and where stops sit. Market makers can fill orders there because many participants are entering and exiting. When liquidity isn't being swept or induced, price is "chippy choppy". [02:53], [03:28]
- **Prominent highs/lows** — The liquidity he cares most about. What counts as prominent depends on the timeframe; he says it is "relatively easy" to see by eye and is vague on an exact rule. His examples: a high that starts a big trend; a double top; a high or low that broke structure on the daily timeframe; the most recent swing points before a BOS. [03:28], [04:01], [04:34], [05:08]
- **Liquidity as a magnet / draw on liquidity** — Price is drawn toward liquidity because market makers want to push price there to fill their orders. [05:41], [06:16]
- **Why the opposing liquidity is the target** — Market makers who sell at highs need to close (exit) at lows, where other people are entering and exiting, so their exit doesn't move price against them. So a short entered after a high is swept targets prior lows, and a long targets prior highs. [08:28], [09:02], [10:12]
- **Liquidity sweep** — A prominent high or low gets taken out. This includes a barely-wicked high, which he counts ("barely gets wicked"). It also includes a sweep that is only visible on a lower timeframe ("you're probably saying it never got hit — exactly, look on the one hour, sweep"). [13:02], [14:11]
- **Not every high/low is used** — It is fine to mark every high and low, but not every one will be used to fill orders. That's why you wait for confluence. [19:15]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Never buy just because a low was hit or sell just because a high was hit ("you would lose way more than you win"). Wait for the liquidity to be swept, then wait for confirmation that orders have been filled. [06:16], [06:49]
- Confirmation = break of structure (BOS) plus other confluences: an FVG/imbalance fill and reaction, an order block reaction. If price doesn't react after the sweep, there is no trade. If it reacts, act on it. [06:49], [07:23], [19:15]
- BOS definition used here: a candle closes below the most recent low that produced the move up into the swept high (for shorts). "We got a closure below." [10:45], [14:11]
- Entry options after a sweep plus BOS: enter on the BOS itself, or enter in a fair value gap/imbalance that gets filled on a pullback, for example a 4H FVG. You can drop to a lower timeframe (for example 5m) and enter when an imbalance fills and confirms. [10:45], [14:11]
- Bias context: in a bear market (downtrend), wait for highs to get taken out, then look for shorts targeting lows. [07:56]
- Target = the next previous prominent liquidity on the opposite side: prior prominent lows for shorts, prior prominent highs for longs. [08:28], [11:18], [15:21]
- Once liquidity has been taken and price has pushed past several prominent highs with no reaction, the next unreacted prominent high is the "one we're looking for". [12:28]
- Liquidity is "a piece that we can plug into a strategy", not a strategy on its own. [01:42]
- A detailed take-profit section is promised for later. [13:39]

## Process / order of operations
1. Mark prominent highs and lows on the timeframe you are using (this works on every timeframe, daily down to 1m).
2. Decide on a directional context (for example, a downtrend means waiting for highs to be swept).
3. Wait for a prominent high or low to be swept. Check lower timeframes for sweeps not visible on the higher one.
4. Wait for confirmation: BOS (a candle closes beyond the most recent opposing swing), then an FVG/imbalance or order block reaction.
5. Enter on the BOS or on the retracement into the imbalance, refining on a lower timeframe if you want.
6. Target the opposite prominent liquidity (prior lows or highs).

## Confluences mentioned
- Break of structure after a sweep — the core confirmation ("pretty standard"). [10:45]
- Fair value gap / imbalance fill plus reaction — entry location. [10:45], [14:11], [18:09]
- Order block / "hidden order block" reaction. [13:39], [18:09]
- Lower-timeframe refinement (for example 5m). [14:11]

## Trade examples
- S&P 500, daily (no date): Price takes a prominent high, then gets a BOS down with a close below the most recent low. Entry options are the BOS or a 4H FVG within those candles. Price falls to the previous prominent lows. Later another high is swept, gives a BOS down, a daily candle fills an FVG, and price drops to the prior prominent low. [04:34]–[12:28]
- GBPUSD 4H/1H: The highs look unswept on 4H, but the 1H shows a sweep. BOS on a later candle, a retracement into an order block, then price capitulates, taking a prominent low and filling imbalances (several TP options). [13:02], [13:39]
- GBPUSD: Highs are barely wicked. BOS when the most recent low closes broken. Scale into the 5m, the imbalance fills, look for confirmation, go short and target the lows below. [14:11], [14:48]
- Gold: Lows are swept and quickly regained with a close above. A dip into an imbalance is the entry zone. Price draws to the previous highs, then chops. [14:48], [15:21]
