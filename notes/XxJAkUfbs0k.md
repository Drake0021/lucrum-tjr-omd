## Summary
An early (Oct 2022) lesson on liquidity and liquidity sweeps; the first ~4:30 is vlog. TJR explains liquidity as the stops and opposite-direction entries banks need so their large orders fill at the best price. Liquidity sits at the **most recent and most prominent** highs/lows and at relative equal highs/lows. He uses HTF (weekly/daily) liquidity **only for bias**: after a daily BOS against the weekly trend, ask "where's the next liquidity sweep" that will continue the weekly trend. For execution he marks the most recent, most prominent high and low before each **NYSE open** (S&P) or each session open (London/Asia/NY for GBPJPY). He waits for the session open to sweep one of them, then needs a 1m BOS, then enters on the BOS or an FVG, stop beyond the sweep, TPs at previous liquidity. A sweep with no BOS = no trade. His weekly bias for the coming week (S&P): bullish after a weekly-low sweep, targeting unswept highs and a daily CPI FVG.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity** — "liquid cash"; banks/institutions must **induce** it ("sweep liquidity in order for their orders to get filled"). A sweep takes people out (stops) and pulls people in the other direction (breakout entries), so the big orders fill at the best price without moving the market against them. [05:03]–[09:01]
- **Where liquidity lies** — "the most prominent and recent highs and lows within the market… because there's stop losses there, there's buy stops there". Also relative equal highs (two or three equal highs = "a lot of liquidity"). [14:07]; [12:24]–[12:59]; [16:20]
- **Retail-trap logic** — retail shorts the BOS, stop above the lower high; when the high is breached, shorts are stopped and breakout buyers enter → "the perfect storm for banks to switch direction". [10:11]–[11:51]; [39:56]–[40:29]
- **Liquidity on every timeframe** — retail longs on a weekly-low sweep with a LTF BOS put stops under the LTF low, which gets swept too: "see how liquidity is swept on every time frame". [20:54]
- **Session liquidity** — the most recent and most prominent high and low **prior to** the NYSE open (S&P), or prior to each session open (forex: Asia, London, NY). [25:34]; [28:28]–[30:17]
- **Low-volume sessions** — on GBPJPY, NY and Asia often produce little; London moves it: "this is a European and Asian pair… not a lot of volume" in NY; Asia "pretty low volume". Unswept Asia levels are likely London targets. [29:43]; [34:52]–[35:26]
- **"Most important thing"** — liquidity sweeps are "probably the most important thing within the market because it is what moves the market". [40:29]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- **HTF liquidity = bias only:** "that is all I use high time frame liquidity for, it's for a bias… I don't take swing trades." Trades last ~1–3 hours or less. [14:42]
- **Daily BOS against weekly trend:** "whenever I see a break of structure on the daily time frame I instantly think where's the liquidity sweep going to be" (the prominent high/equal highs) that continues the weekly trend. [15:46]–[18:39]
- **Execution model:** mark the most recent, most prominent high and low before the NYSE open → wait for the open to sweep one → drop to the 1m → BOS (close beyond the most recent opposite swing) → enter on the BOS close or in the FVG → stop beyond the sweep → TPs at previous liquidity levels. [24:23]–[27:19]; [31:24]–[32:31]
- **Sweep without BOS = no trade:** "we get a liquidity sweep, do we get a break of structure though? No… no trade… doesn't always mean that's where market wants to go." [33:42]–[34:18]
- **Minimum model:** "look for a liquidity sweep, look for a break of structure and honestly that's all you need. I use the fair value gap just so I can hone in my entries" for better RR. [38:47]–[39:21]
- **Stop:** above (below) the liquidity sweep. [31:58]; [35:59]; [38:12]
- **Targets:** previous areas of liquidity on the 1m (multiple TPs), 1:3 example. [31:58]–[32:31]; [36:33]
- **No-trade day:** Saturday no charts. [03:20]

## Process / order of operations
1. Weekly/daily: find recent prominent highs/lows and equal highs/lows; after a daily BOS, ask where the next sweep will occur; set the weekly bias and HTF targets (unswept highs, daily FVG). [15:46]–[23:42]
2. Before the session open: mark the most recent, prominent high and low prior to the open. [24:56]–[25:34]
3. At the open: wait for one side to be swept. [25:34]
4. 1m: wait for a BOS opposite the sweep. [31:24]
5. Enter on the BOS close or the FVG; stop beyond the sweep; TPs at previous liquidity. [31:58]; [38:12]
6. No BOS → no trade. [34:18]

## Changes vs. earlier teaching / contradictions
- **Timeline placement:** Oct 2022, pre-Boot Camp. The sweep → BOS → (FVG) model is the early version; it already contains the core of the 2026 canonical flow (sweep of significant high/low → BOS → FVG retrace → targets at liquidity).

- Session highs/lows before the NY open as the swept liquidity — consistent with the 2026 canonical "sweep of significant high/low" and session H/L draws. Answers OPEN #5 partly: the LTF sweep for ES is the most recent, most prominent high/low prior to the NYSE open. [25:34]
- Sweep without BOS = no trade, consistent with the Boot Camp.
- No news, SMT, risk size, time window beyond "the open", or contracts mentioned.
