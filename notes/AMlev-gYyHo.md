## Summary
A lesson on liquidity and liquidity sweeps. Liquidity is resting orders: buy orders/buy stops above highs and sell orders/sell stops below lows. Market makers fill their large orders there to get the best price, so highs and lows are places where price can reverse. A sweep is when those resting orders are filled and a new trend starts. His execution: mark NY open (9:30 ET), mark 4H and 1H highs/lows (pre-market and earlier) as draws on liquidity, and wait for price to run one. A run alone is not a trade; he needs a visible reaction candle off the level. Then he drops to the 1m for a BOS and a retrace into an FVG (or breaker) and targets the next draw on liquidity. Examples are on NQ (inferred) and ES at the NY open, plus a forex London-open example.

## Concepts & definitions
- **High** — a move up then a move down; "a two candlestick pattern": an up candle then a down candle. Continuous up candles with no down candle give no high. [01:06]
- **Low** — a move down then a move up (down candle followed by up candle). [01:41]
- **Liquidity** — "resting orders": resting buy orders above highs and resting sell orders below lows. Above highs sit with-trend breakout buyers plus stop losses of shorts (forced to buy back). Below lows sit breakout sellers plus stops of longs. [02:49]–[05:02] [10:33] [12:57]
- **Why the market reverses at liquidity** — market makers/"the algorithm" need opposing orders to fill large size. Filling sells inside an uptrend would push price against them ("they're greedy"). Above highs there are many buy orders to sell into, which gives them the best price, and then price reverses. [05:37]–[08:23]
- **Why we use liquidity** — price "has the potential to reverse at highs and lows" because of the resting orders. It is only potential; liquidity alone is not an entry. [11:10] [12:19]
- **Liquidity sweep** — "when those resting orders actually get filled and start a new trend, either up or down". [12:57] [13:33]
- **Buy-side / sell-side liquidity** — highs = buy-side liquidity (where buy orders get filled); lows = sell-side liquidity. [23:10]
- **Fractal** — liquidity exists on every timeframe (monthly to 1m); he shows monthly, 4H, 1H and 15m sweeps. [14:38] [15:12] [19:13]
  - Caption/slip: he says "lower time frames hold higher power and obviously have smaller moves". He means higher timeframes hold higher power (his usual phrase); lower-TF sweeps make smaller moves. (inferred) [19:13]
- **London session fake-out** — his old forex strategy: London open would stop him out, then run his way. He now explains it as liquidity being swept at the session open. [08:56]–[09:29]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Never sell just because a high is taken, or buy just because a low is taken ("pressing sell at highs and buy at lows — worst strategy ever"). Wait for confirmation that liquidity was swept. [08:56] [11:10] [14:38] [25:24]
- Identify your session. He trades only the NY session; at this time he trades only S&P 500 and NASDAQ. Asian session has too little volatility. [21:27]
- Mark the session open: NY = 9:30 (ET). [22:01]
- HTF draws on liquidity: go to the 4H and 1H and mark highs and lows (including those made during pre-market). Remove levels already taken out during pre-market. [22:35] [23:10] [29:32]
- After marking 4H/1H levels, "that's typically all that I really need from the high time frames"; then scale to the low timeframes. [23:45]
- After the open, as price runs each marked level, ask: "do we get a reaction?" If no reaction, liquidity "has not been swept" and price may keep going to the next level. [24:50]–[25:58] [30:06]
- Reaction = a strong candle against the run (e.g. a "big" down candle off the highs; a "huge" up candle off the lows). Then "liquidity has potentially been swept". The candle has no numeric size definition (vague). [26:35] [30:42]
- After the reaction: scale down to the 1m → BOS → retrace into an imbalance/FVG → orders coming out of it (reaction) → enter. [26:35] [31:16]
- Stop: above the swept highs (short example). [27:10]
- Targets: the next draws on liquidity. Liquidity is used "not only for entries but for exits", e.g. hourly lows below for a short, or the highs above for a long. [27:10] [27:46] [31:48]
- Entry confluences he lists as his own: break of structure, inverse fair value gap, fair value gaps, order blocks, breaker blocks. [30:42]
- Forex alternative confirmation: "we can literally just wait for like a five minute break of structure" after the reaction. [32:57]

## Process / order of operations
1. Pick your session (his: NY) and mark the open (9:30 ET). [21:27] [22:01]
2. Mark 4H highs/lows, then 1H highs/lows (buy-side above, sell-side below). Drop levels already taken in pre-market. [22:35]–[23:10] [29:32]
3. After the open, watch each level as it is run. If there is no reaction, wait. [24:50]
4. When there is a reaction candle off a level, treat the liquidity as potentially swept. [26:35]
5. Drop to the 1m: BOS → retrace into FVG (or breaker block) → reaction → enter. [26:35] [31:16] [34:04]
6. Stop beyond the swept extreme; target the next draw on liquidity (hourly H/L). [27:10] [27:46]
