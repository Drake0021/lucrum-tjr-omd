## Summary
A short lesson on "imbalances" in general: fair value gaps, inverse FVGs (IFVG), new week opening gaps (NWOG), new day opening gaps (NDOG) and candle gaps. The idea is that all imbalances are price ranges that price "wants to balance out", so it tends to draw back into them. They show up on every timeframe. FVGs are confluences that only work with bias and context. IFVGs are used as **confirmation, similar to a BOS** (an early sign that order flow has flipped), not as confluences. Opening gaps and candle gaps are presented as educational and rarely traded. No live trade is taken; the chart examples are illustrative and undated.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Imbalance** — "price isn't balanced out"; price is likely to want to come back and fill the imbalanced range. FVG, candle gap, NDOG, NWOG and IFVG are all imbalances that price "is likely going to draw back in towards" [01:08–01:41].
- **Fair value gap (FVG)** — a three-candle pattern. The middle candle is the "extension candle" that makes the gap [01:41]. Bearish: the first candle's wick does not overlap the third candle's top wick. The range between them lacks buy orders because candle 2 moved through it swiftly. Price returns to fill more sell orders and then continues lower [02:14–02:46]. Bullish: from the **top wick of candle 1 to the bottom wick of candle 3**. If the wicks overlap, it is not an FVG [03:18–03:52].
- **FVGs on all timeframes** — "these show up on every single time frame" (4H, daily, 15m, 5m, 1m) [03:52–04:24].
- **FVG still valid after a wick through** — "a fair value gap is still valid if we wick to the bottom of it and wick past it." Only a **candle close** beyond the far side invalidates it and turns it into an IFVG [07:40].
- **Inverse FVG (IFVG)** — an FVG that is invalidated: e.g. a bullish FVG where price later **closes underneath** it (not necessarily on the next candle; it can be several candles later) [06:02–06:34]. Mirror: a bearish FVG that price closes above [07:07; 08:45].
- **IFVG use = confirmation, like a BOS** — "I'm not necessarily looking to use these as confluences, I like to call inverse fair value gaps more of like confirmation ... super similar to breaks of structure." It can tell him "before a break of structure even happens" that order flow is being disrupted, and it changes his bias of the trend [06:34–07:07]. If there is no BOS, the IFVG close can be treated "as if it was a break of structure ... as confirmation" [07:40].
- **New week opening gap (NWOG)** — the range between the prior week's close and the new week's open. It is treated like an FVG / draw on liquidity, and price tends to fill it "damn near immediately". Easy to see on the weekly chart via the wick [09:51–11:29].
- **New day opening gap (NDOG)** — the same thing for the daily close vs the next day's open; price typically wicks back to fill it [11:29–12:36].
- **Candle gap** — the same as NWOG/NDOG on smaller timeframes (often seen on the 1m): candle close ≠ next candle open, and price fills it [12:36–13:43].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- FVGs are never traded on their own. "Our confluences are nothing without context and bias." Do not short every bearish FVG; you need daily bias and an execution model first [04:57–05:29].
- An FVG is invalidated only by a **candle close** through it, not a wick [07:40].
- A close through an FVG against its direction (IFVG) = confirmation of a trend change, treated like a BOS [06:34–07:40; 08:45].
- Sequence shown in the example: sweep of liquidity → BOS → IFVG confirmation → price seeks out imbalances and draws on liquidity [08:45–09:18].
- NWOG/NDOG/candle gaps are not used for trades often: "are we likely going to be taking trades off of this, no" [12:36–13:10].

## Process / order of operations
1. Have daily bias and context first [05:29].
2. Example (bullish day, undated): the bias is bullish with a draw on liquidity above. On the lower timeframe price sweeps liquidity, then retraces, leaving bullish FVGs (possible trades). A bearish FVG inside the retrace is **closed above** (IFVG), so the retrace is over and price continues higher [08:13–08:45].
