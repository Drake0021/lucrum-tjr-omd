## Summary
TJR recaps two "TJR forex strategy" longs. The gold long swept the prior New York session lows, waited for the FVG to fill, and then took a 5m BOS at the NYSE open. TP1 was the prior NY session highs, for about +$7.7k, before he was wicked out at break even; TP2 and TP3 were the Asian and London highs. The GU long came off the prior NY lows plus a HTF order block and a 5m BOS at the NYSE open, and was live and in drawdown. He states the forex strategy plainly: wait for session lows or highs to be taken out, then a 5m BOS, with no need for a third confluence. He marks session highs and lows from the candle body ("base") to the wick. The psychology segment covers not panicking at under 50% drawdown, treating trading as a focused two-hour job with a morning routine, and not trading when emotionally compromised.

## Concepts & definitions
- **TJR forex strategy** — "Wait for these session lows to get taken out and then wait for a five minute break of structure." The forex strategy "doesn't necessarily need to wait for a third confluence". [03:32], [02:23], [05:09]
- **Session high/low zone marking** — He marks session highs and lows "from the base up to the wick", meaning from the candle body to the wick, giving a zone. TP levels can be placed at the base (body) and at the wick. [02:23]–[03:32], [04:36]
- **Previous New York (Stock Exchange) session lows/highs** — The liquidity targeted for the sweep (prior NY session lows) and TP1 (prior NY session highs). [01:15], [02:23]
- **NYSE open timing** — He references the NYSE opening at 14:30 on his chart (UTC; 9:30 ET), with the entry BOS taken "when New York was actually open", not in pre-market. [02:23], [05:09]
- **Post-BOS dip** — After a 5m BOS there is "usually an instant reaction" against it "to kind of scare people out". Entering on that dip (even off the body) gives a better entry and stop. [04:04]–[04:36]
- **HTF order block** — A quick move down followed by a big expansion up, used as confluence for the GU long. [05:41]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Forex strategy entry: (1) price sweeps the previous session low (NY session lows here); (2) wait for a 5m BOS in the opposite direction (here, up); (3) enter on the BOS candle close. The stop goes beneath the swept lows. [02:23], [03:32], [05:09]
- Optional refinement: if the BOS leaves a FVG unfilled, he prefers to see most or all of the FVG filled first. A weak BOS "wasn't enough". [01:49]
- Optional refinement: after the BOS, wait for the small counter-dip and enter there. [04:04]–[04:36]
- Timing: he took entries at or after the NYSE open, not pre-market. [02:23], [05:09]
- TP1 is the previous NY session highs. TP2 and TP3 are the combined Asian and London session highs, at the body and at the wick. An extended TP is beyond. [02:23]–[03:32]
- Management: after TP1, the stop goes to break even. He was wicked out at BE after TP1. [02:58]
- Holding: remain unconcerned while the 15m and 5m bullish structure is intact. [06:14]
- GU stop was 24 pips; the drawdown reached 11 pips, under 50% of risk. Do not exit for that. [08:25]–[08:59]
- Possible improvement on GU: wait for the NY lows to be fully taken out and pay closer attention to the daily bias or trend. [06:14]–[06:48]

## Process / order of operations
1. Mark the previous New York session highs and lows, plus the Asian and London highs and lows, as body-to-wick zones. [02:23]–[03:32]
2. Wait for price to sweep the previous session low (or high). [03:32]
3. At or after the NYSE open, wait for a 5m BOS in the reversal direction. [05:09]
4. Enter on the BOS, or on the subsequent small dip. The stop goes beyond the swept extreme. [04:36]
5. TP1 is the opposing previous session extreme. Then move the stop to BE and target the Asian/London highs. [02:58]

## Risk & psychology rules
- If price moving less than 50% of your stop distance makes you exit or second-guess, de-risk, go to demo, or reconsider trading. [09:32]–[10:04]
- If you get emotional on live, you should be on demo until you prove yourself. [10:04]
- Treat trading as a full-time, high-performance job: be fully locked in for the roughly 2 hours of trading. No joking or memes in that window. [10:38]–[11:11], [13:57]
- Morning routine: he wakes about 4 hours before the open, takes pre-workout and coffee, and works out before trading to clear his mind. Do not roll out of bed into the open. [13:23]
- Build a routine to get into a good mental state: meditation, reading about 10 pages, etc. [15:04]
- Do not trade on days of major emotional stress such as a breakup or bereavement. Take the day off. [16:10]–[16:43]
- Ask yourself whether you are fit to trade or are seeking a dopamine or mood boost. The latter leads to revenge trading. [18:24]
- Once TP1 is hit on one trade, he is comfortable for the day even if another trade stops out. [11:45]
- Mood shows up in results: his bad mood caused a small losing week. [17:18]
