## Summary
A psychology "breather" before teaching BOS. TJR lists what unprofitable traders do: several strategies, several mentors, overthinking and overcomplicating, trying to get rich quick, giving up quickly, short attention span, and overlearning while under-practising. He inverts that into a profitable mindset to write out daily. He mentions that his 2024 Mastermind strategy uses **four timeframes and three confluences** as a step-by-step checklist that won't give a trade every day. He says beginners should trade demo, not live.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Unprofitable mindset** — (1) several strategies, (2) several mentors, (3) overthink/overcomplicate, (4) get-rich-quick, (5) give up too quickly, (6) short attention span, (7) overlearn and under-practise. [07:25–23:47]
- **Profitable mindset (to write out daily)** — One strategy; one mentor; simplify everything; get rich over a long period; stay strong/consistent for a long time ("think in years not days, in decades not months"); over-practise and learn one step at a time. [25:28–28:53]
- **His Mastermind strategy (2024)** — "only looking at four time frames and it's three confluences off of those four time frames." It is a checklist: "step one step two understand what you're looking for step three step four execute ... hands off." It won't produce a trade every day. [10:51–11:58] The four timeframes are not named here. A later day-23 note suggests 4H/1H bias and 5m/15m execution (inferred from series context).
- **Mastermind error example** — Students took trades "off one minute liquidity sweeps" and looked at the weekly, which was not part of the four-timeframe strategy. [22:39–23:14]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- If the setup isn't there, don't trade: "you won't be able to take a trade every day but that doesn't matter ... if the shot's not there ... just don't take it." [17:34]
- Don't use 1m liquidity sweeps or the weekly in the beginner four-TF strategy (implied by his critique). [22:39–23:14]
- Beginners: demo first; don't put live capital (e.g. $5,000) at risk without experience. [15:23–15:55]
