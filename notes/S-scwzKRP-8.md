## Summary
On a CPI day when he did not trade, TJR does hindsight "aimbot" backtesting with Discord on S&P, GBPJPY, gold and GBPUSD. He explains why he avoids CPI (pre-market news makes most of the move; the post-open move is low-volume and small), shows that the only S&P short setup was against a fully bullish HTF bias and was just an hourly retrace, and restates the key structural rule: a lower-timeframe BOS against the next-higher timeframe's trend is usually that higher timeframe's retracement. He also shows the Forex session-levels approach (Asian/London highs/lows) on GJ, gold and GU, and skips trades with conflicting timeframes or no FVG/OB/equilibrium.

## Concepts & definitions
- **LTF BOS against HTF trend = HTF retrace** — If 15m breaks structure down while the hourly is bullish, it's probably an hourly retrace; if 5m breaks down while 15m is bullish, probably a 15m retrace; an hourly BOS down is probably a 4H retrace; a daily BOS down was a weekly retrace on S&P. "Not every single time" — exceptions are liquidity sweeps and change of market structure/direction. [04:31], [05:05], [05:37]
- **Why CPI/pre-market news is avoided** — the news candle makes the majority of the move (S&P: ~27-point CPI candle vs ~4-point move from an entry, or ~12 points from the imbalance afterward); price after the open is low volume and barely moves. [02:19], [02:51], [03:24]
- **Session levels (Forex strategy)** — mark Asian session high/low and London session high/low; NY pushing into a London high then BOS down = short trigger; London lows pushed into then BOS up = long. [07:36], [08:10], [11:35]
- **Weekly retracement tools** — fair value gap and equilibrium on the weekly as draws during a weekly retrace. [06:31], [07:04]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Avoid trading CPI days (especially when news releases pre-market). [02:19], [03:24], [15:09]
- Do not take a LTF setup against the full HTF stack: daily, 4H, 1H and 15m all bullish → a 5m BOS down into an order block is a counter-bias trade; skip it ("perfect example of a time that we wouldn't want to trade"). [03:24], [03:58]
- IF daily, 4H, 1H bullish and price falls after the open, breaks structure (5m) down, fills an OB, rallies into an imbalance, then 5m BOS up THEN a long is valid (the trade he'd have taken, if any). [02:19], [04:31]
- IF weekly bearish, daily bearish, 4H & 1H not contradicting, and in NY price pushes into the London session high then BOS down THEN short on the BOS or wait for reaction from the order block (the candle that made the high, inferred); stop above that; targets = HTF draws (unhit OB, liquidity, daily low / start of FVG). [07:36]–[08:43]
- IF timeframes conflict (weekly bearish, daily bearish, 4H bullish, 1H bearish) THEN avoid. [11:00], [11:35]
- IF 4H bullish, 1H bearish → only look for a 4H retrace, and only at a FVG/OB/equilibrium; if none present, not a good spot. [14:35]
- A setup during CPI release time is not tradable even if the pattern (sweep London low → BOS) works. [11:35]
- Trading too late into the session is a reason to avoid. [15:09]

## Process / order of operations
1. Mark market open line and session levels (Asian high/low, London high/low). [00:35], [07:36]
2. Weekly → daily → 4H → 1H bias; note draws on liquidity (e.g., 15m highs) and imbalances. [01:10]
3. Check news. [01:10]
4. Identify setups at the open; filter by HTF alignment and presence of FVG/OB/equilibrium. [03:58], [14:35]
