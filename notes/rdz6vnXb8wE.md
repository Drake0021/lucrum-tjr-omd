## Summary
Part 2 of the liquidity series: where liquidity rests. In an uptrend, buy-stop entries (breakout buyers) and stop losses of counter-trend shorts sit just above swing highs; in a downtrend, sell stops/sell entries and stop losses of counter-trend longs sit just below swing lows. When those highs/lows are taken, institutions fill orders and price often reverses — "sweep → fall" or "sweep → rally". TJR shows this on the S&P 500 daily, a monthly/yearly low sweep (instrument unclear, likely a USD pair), GU in Asian session/15m, and says it happens on every timeframe. He stresses liquidity alone (even with BOS) is not enough for a bias or high-probability trade; execution is promised for Part 3. Homework: find 5 liquidity sweeps on each of 3 timeframes.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Why liquidity matters (recap)** — orders are filled there; banks need millions of opposite-side orders (people exiting + people entering against them) to fill and move the market. [01:06]-[01:38]
- **Buy-side liquidity above highs (uptrend)** — retail trend-followers place buy stops (pending orders) just above the high expecting a higher high; counter-trend shorts have stop losses just above the high. When the high is pushed above, both sets of orders trigger → liquidity for institutions to fill and change direction ("usually to the downside"). [02:12]-[05:31]
- **Sell-side liquidity below lows (downtrend)** — sell stops / sell entries below lows from trend-continuation sellers, plus stop losses of traders buying the small up-moves. [05:31]-[06:38]
- **"Nobody won"** — breakout buyers above the high and stopped-out shorts are both liquidated as price reverses; stops get hit by "a couple pips" and then price moves in the stopped trader's favor. [03:51]-[04:24]
- **Order block (preview)** — the move that causes the liquidation and allows the market to fill orders "would be essentially an order block"; to be defined later. [06:38]-[07:10]
- **Liquidity sweep** — a high (or low) within a trend gets taken out and price then reverses: "uptrend, sweep of the highs, collapse"; "boom sweep fall", "boom sweep rally". [07:10]-[07:47], [10:33]-[11:08]
- **Fractal** — sweeps occur on every timeframe (monthly/yearly down to 15m/1m). [07:47], [09:26], [12:14]
- **Market needs liquidity to move** — "the market is unable to actually move in the direction that it wants to without taking out liquidity to fill the orders". [11:08]
- **Trading reversals at trend ends** — because trends don't last forever, using liquidity lets him enter at the beginning of the new trend (he says "catch the top of a downtrend or the bottom of an uptrend" — a slip; meaning the top of an uptrend / bottom of a downtrend), catching the big moves. [05:31]-[06:04]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Mark the most obvious swing highs in uptrends (buy-side liquidity) and swing lows in downtrends (sell-side liquidity) as targets for institutional sweeps. [04:56]-[06:38]
- After a sweep, look for a break of structure in the opposite direction as confirmation (GU example: sweep lows → BOS up → price pushes higher). [09:57]
- Do not trade from liquidity alone: "it's not just liquidity you need to know a lot more than just liquidity and break of structure to be able to form an actual bias and ... take an actual high probability trade". [13:54]
- Breakout entries (buy stops above highs) and stops placed just beyond obvious highs/lows are the retail behaviour being exploited — avoid being that liquidity (inferred from his framing). [02:47]-[04:56]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify trend on the chosen timeframe. [02:12]
2. Mark the swing highs (uptrend) / swing lows (downtrend) where stops and breakout orders rest. [02:47]-[06:38]
3. Watch for price to take the level (sweep). [07:10]
4. Look for reversal confirmation (BOS opposite direction) — execution details in Part 3. [09:57], [13:54]

## Risk & psychology rules
