## Summary
TJR's take-profit method: take profits at building blocks (liquidity, order blocks, fair value gaps/imbalances, liquidity voids) because they are "price magnets". TP1 = the first draw on liquidity in the trade direction on one timeframe higher than the execution timeframe (e.g., 5m execution → 15m draw), ideally at least 1:1 R:R; subsequent TPs = further building blocks. He usually sets 3–4 TPs (number is up to your trading plan). He prefers TPs based on the execution timeframe (and one higher), not 4H levels, for LTF trades. He ranks importance: daily bias > execution > stop loss > take profit. Includes a real ES/S&P short from Friday closed at breakeven.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Take profits at building blocks** — order block (top or bottom of it), liquidity (highs/lows), fair value gap (inside or at the far side), liquidity voids — price "magnets" because orders are filled there. [01:49], [02:56], [07:56]
- **TP1 = one-higher-timeframe draw on liquidity** — "the one higher time frame draw on liquidity on whatever execution time frame I'm on": 5m short → scale to 15m, find first draw on liquidity in trade direction. [03:29]
- **TPs based on execution timeframe** — even for LTF entries on HTF setups (15m entry for 4H trade), he personally sets TPs on the execution timeframe (and one higher), not necessarily 4H liquidity, unless you want to stay in that long and genuinely think it gets there. [04:35], [05:10]
- **Don't base TPs on 1-minute confluences** — with a 5m BOS and 1m entry, target the 5m OB, not 1m levels ("higher time frame holds higher power"). [09:06]
- **Order block definition used** — move that swept liquidity and then rallied (the sweep leg). [02:56], [09:39]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- TP1: first draw on liquidity in trade direction on one timeframe above the execution timeframe. [03:29]
- TP1 should be minimum ~1:1 R:R ("most of the time my first take profit is going to be minimum one to one ... that's kind of the goal"); find the building block that matches ~1:1. [06:15], [07:23]
- Further TPs: next building blocks (FVG, OB, next liquidity), possibly from one timeframe up (15m FVG) or down (1H OB on a 4H trade). Skip an OB that "envelops" a high already used as a TP. [04:03], [06:49], [07:23], [09:39]
- Usually 3–4 TPs; how many is your trading-plan choice. [04:03]
- Typical first-TP R:R is 1:1 to 1:5, not 1:10. [05:42]
- Entry noted: on candle close of BOS (example), or FVG entry with stop below the swing low. [02:24], [06:15]
- Don't take counter-trend shorts when in an uptrend on multiple timeframes (example explicitly called a "stupid"/"terrible" trade, used only to illustrate TPs). [02:24]
- Close at breakeven if price is taking "forever" (Friday ES trade) — management example, not formalized. [08:30]

## Process / order of operations
1. Enter per execution model. 2. Scale up one timeframe from execution TF; find first draw on liquidity in trade direction → TP1 (~≥1:1). 3. Mark subsequent building blocks (FVG, OB, liquidity) → TP2–TP4. [03:29], [06:15]
- Priority ranking of skills: daily bias > execution > stop loss > take profit. [05:10]

## Confluences mentioned
- Liquidity (highs/lows) — primary TP1. [03:29]
- Order blocks (top/bottom), FVGs, liquidity voids — later TPs. [02:56], [07:56]
