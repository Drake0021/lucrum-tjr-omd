## Summary
Final episode of the Jan 2026 "Path to Profitability" series giving TJR's current strategy as a thought process rather than a rigid checklist (he says his strategy has changed since older strategy videos and he deliberately leaves room for discretion, e.g. sometimes dropping to 1-min). Core model: (1) potential for orders to be filled = sweep of a significant high/low (draw on liquidity), (2) confirmation orders were filled = LTF change in structure via BOS or IFVG, (3) continuation = retrace into FVG or equilibrium of the new leg, with entry on 1-min confirmation (BOS/IFVG back in trade direction) inside that 5-min continuation confluence, (4) exit at draws on liquidity (session highs/lows, HTF highs/lows) where filled orders can be liquidated. He walks a Friday (Jan 16 2026) ES short in replay: bullish HTF bias was overridden by a bearish SMT (NQ swept London high, ES didn't), 5-min BOS down, retrace to 5-min equilibrium, 1-min IFVG entry, stop above highs, TP1-TP3 at London/Asia session lows and hourly lows.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Three skill sets** — strategy, risk management, psychology; all required. [00:33]
- **Strategy as thought process** — "We go from orders being filled to change in order flow to continuation of the new trend and I'm looking to enter and I'm looking to target other draws on liquidity or internal draws on liquidity." [10:36]
- **Confluences as a toolbox** — not every confluence used every day; market differs daily. [13:54] [14:26]
- **Draws on liquidity used as entries and targets** — areas where price can fill orders and reverse. [24:18]
- **Useless draw** — a session high already traded through with no reaction (e.g. Asia high taken by London) is "pretty useless". [22:04]
- **Entry confluence** — price pushes into the 5-min continuation confluence (FVG/EQ); on the 1-min the retrace is bullish (for a short), then wait for a 1-min BOS or IFVG back down → entry. [33:55] [34:28]
- **Liquidation points / exits** — HTF highs/lows, previous session highs/lows where filled orders can be liquidated. [41:14]
- **Support & resistance** — he is "not the biggest fan"; not part of his model. [41:46] [42:19]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
1. Bias: identify 4H and 1H order flow and where price is in the HTF trend (e.g. filled 4H FVG/equilibrium and showing extension → expect external draw). [20:57] [21:31]
2. Mark draws on liquidity for the day: previous day high/low, Asia session high/low, London session high/low, hourly highs/lows. [19:19] [19:50] [22:04] [22:39]
3. Potential for orders to fill: sweep of a significant high/low. SMT divergence at the sweep strengthens (or can override) bias. [24:54] [25:28] [35:36] [38:31]
4. Confirmation: LTF (5-min) change in order flow via BOS or IFVG. If no FVG in the leg to invert, wait for BOS. A bearish SMT alone is not enough — "I want to see a change in order flow". [27:45] [28:19] [29:27]
5. Continuation: wait for retrace into a 5-min FVG or equilibrium (of the new leg high→low, needs an opposite candle to define it). [30:00] [31:08]
6. Do NOT enter merely on touching the FVG/EQ (candle still forming could run you out). Either wait for the next 5-min candle to close in trade direction, or (his preference) drop to 1-min and enter on a 1-min BOS/IFVG back in trade direction. [31:40] [32:13] [32:48] [33:19]
7. Trade the leading index of an SMT (the one that failed to sweep, e.g. ES when NQ swept the high). [30:35]
8. Stop above the highs (swing high of the retrace) for shorts. [35:03]
9. Targets: multiple TPs at draws on liquidity (London session low, Asia session low, hourly lows). [35:03] [40:08]
10. Bias can be wrong; let the market prove you wrong and trade what it shows. [39:03]
- Discretion: sometimes 1-min instead of 5-min, or different confirmation, based on experience (e.g., scale down when HTF confirmation won't form before draws are hit). [01:41] [02:15] [09:28]

## Process / order of operations
1. Pre-open: 4H/1H trend and position in trend (which gaps inverted/respected, EQ). [17:35] [18:10] [18:45]
2. Mark PDH/PDL, Asia H/L, London H/L, hourly highs/lows; discard already-used ones. [19:50] [22:04]
3. Form bias toward the external draws. [23:44]
4. At open, watch for sweeps and SMT between ES and NQ. [24:54]
5. 5-min confirmation (BOS/IFVG). [28:54]
6. 5-min continuation retrace (FVG/EQ) → 1-min confirmation entry. [33:55]
7. Stop beyond retrace high/low; scale out at successive draws (TP1/TP2/TP3). [35:03] [40:08]

## Changes vs. earlier teaching / contradictions
- He explicitly says older strategy videos are "pretty dated" and "my strategy has changed since then". [01:06]
- Current model: liquidity sweep → BOS **or IFVG** → FVG **or equilibrium** continuation → 1-min BOS/IFVG entry. IFVG, equilibrium as entry zone, and SMT divergence are integrated; 2023 Boot Camp model relied on BOS + FVG/OB (verify against framework). [36:11] [38:31]
- Refuses a strict step-by-step; acknowledges discretionary timeframe choice (5-min vs 1-min) — a bot needs a fixed default: 5-min confirmation/continuation, 1-min entry trigger. [12:15] [33:19]
- Uses ES/NQ SMT to select which index to trade (leading index). [30:35]
- Rejects support/resistance. [42:19]
- vs kb/framework_v1.md §4.E (BC2 canonical: HTF block hit → sweep → 5m BOS → area of accumulation/FVG/EQ/OB → 1m BOS or 5m reaction): 2026 skeleton is the same 3-step logic but: (a) confirmation = BOS OR IFVG; (b) continuation = FVG OR EQ only (no OB / accumulation mentioned); (c) 1-min entry trigger can be IFVG; (d) SMT divergence used for bias/instrument choice; (e) explicit requirement that the HTF block be hit is softened — the sweep of a session/PD level is the starting point.
- vs framework §1/§5 (TP1 ~1:1, close 50%, move to BE): this video shows TP1/TP2/TP3 at draws on liquidity but does not mention 50% partial or BE — management unchanged/unstated.
- vs framework §3 (no counter-bias trades): here he shorted against his bullish HTF bias after SMT + 5m BOS, saying 'the market proved me wrong' [39:03]. UPDATE: LTF sweep+SMT+confirmation can override pre-market bias.
