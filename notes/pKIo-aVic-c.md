## Summary
A liquidity lesson from December 2025. TJR says he uses liquidity sweeps in "literally every single trade". He defines highs and lows as two-candle patterns and says liquidity rests above highs and below lows: breakout traders' entries plus the stops of traders on the other side. Market makers use those resting orders to fill size at good prices. He calls himself a **reversal trader**, not a trend trader. A sweep is valid only when price **reacts** and a lower-timeframe change of trend/structure follows. His operational routine: mark **4H and 1H highs/lows** before the 9:30 ET NY open. If one was already swept in the prior session, pre-market or London and price is reacting, that is the day's sweep; otherwise expect the NY open to sweep one. Liquidity is also the target, i.e. draws on liquidity are the exits.

## Concepts & definitions
- **High / low** — "A high is two candlestick pattern. A move up then a move down. A low is a move down then a move up." The level is the highest (lowest) point of the two candles. "It's not a move down then a move down" [00:32; 11:00–11:33].
- **Liquidity** — resting orders above highs (breakout buyers + short-sellers' stops = buy orders) and below lows (breakout sellers + longs' stops = sell orders) [03:52–04:26; 08:51–09:23].
- **Why sweeps reverse** — market makers need large opposite-side volume to fill size at one price without moving the market against themselves. They push above a high, where buy orders flood in, and sell into it [05:33–07:44]. "Liquidity is just using manipulation" of both sides [09:57].
- **Opportunity, not a signal** — pushing above a high gives the market "the opportunity" to reverse, nothing more. Don't sell every time a high is taken [05:00; 08:18; 10:28].
- **Valid liquidity sweep** — the level is taken out **and price reacts**. "If price comes down and takes out a low and keeps going down, is it a liquidity sweep? No. Because it's not reacting to it." [16:26–16:58]. Proper-sweep confirmation = "we see a change in trend. We see a change in market structure on the lower time frames" [18:03].
- **Stacked lows** — several hourly lows close together = "a ton of sell orders"; the sweep takes all of them out [16:58; 17:31].
- **Draw on liquidity as exit** — after shorts fill above highs, they are offloaded under lows, where sellers appear. Longs are exited above highs [20:13–21:53; 23:34–24:40].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Trade **reversals off liquidity sweeps**, not trend continuation; don't buy breakouts above highs [02:13–02:46].
- Liquidity timeframes for his NY-open trading: **4H and 1H** highs/lows [15:20–15:54].
- Before the 9:30 ET open, mark 4H highs/lows (and hourly) that could be swept or **have been swept** in the previous session, pre-market or London [18:36].
  - IF liquidity has not been swept before the open → expect NY to open and sweep it during the session, then trade off it [18:36].
  - IF liquidity was already swept in pre-market AND price is already reacting → "this was the liquidity sweep for the day". Trade reactively off it; don't wait for a new NY-open sweep [19:08].
- A sweep needs a reaction + an LTF change of trend/structure before acting [16:26–18:03].
- If price pushes through highs and keeps going, there is no short; "we wait for reaction" [16:58].
- Targets/exits = the opposing draws on liquidity (highs for longs, lows for shorts). In one example the targets were the 4H highs that started the prior down move [21:53; 23:34]. "I use draws on liquidity not only as entry points, but also as exit points" [21:18–21:53].

## Process / order of operations
1. Before the open, mark 4H and 1H highs and lows [15:54; 18:36].
2. Check whether any were swept in the previous session, London or pre-market, with a reaction [18:36–19:08].
3. If not swept, wait for the NY open (9:30 ET) to sweep one [18:36].
4. Confirm a reaction + an LTF change in trend / market structure [18:03].
5. Enter (details deferred to his strategy videos) [17:31].
6. Target the opposing draw on liquidity [21:53].
