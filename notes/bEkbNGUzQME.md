## Summary
Foundational lesson on reading candlesticks (shown on the S&P 500 5-minute chart and gold daily). TJR defines timeframe, open, close, body and wicks, then says the only candlestick "patterns" he cares about are single-candle ones (dojis, long-legged/cross/inverse-cross/dragonfly dojis, hammers) because they show where price rejected. He stresses that candle reads are context only, never a standalone trade reason, and that decisions are made only on closed candles. Homework: find 10+ examples of these candles on your own instrument and note how price reacted.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Timeframe** — the time it takes for one candle to close; on the 5m chart each candle = 5 minutes of price action. [02:48]-[03:22]
- **Reading lower-TF structure from HTF candles** — with skill, one or two 5m candles let him "pretty much guess" what 1m structure did. [03:58]
- **Bullish (green) candle** — opens at the bottom of the body, closes at the top of the body at the end of the period. [04:32]
- **Bearish (red) candle** — opens at the top of the body, closes at the bottom. [06:47]
- **Wick** — shows where price traded during the period but did not close; top of upper wick = period high, bottom of lower wick = period low. [05:05]-[06:13]
- **Large upper wick on a bullish candle** — "strong bulls but not strong enough to close" up there; shows weakness / inability to close above whatever was above, "more than likely it was taking this liquidity". [09:44]-[10:18]
- **Doji** — small body with long wick(s); shows indecision, and "indecision with a bias in one direction". [10:51]-[11:25]
- **Loss of momentum candle** — after a big sell-off, a candle with a long lower wick and no full-bodied close lower = losing bearish momentum; price then consolidated. [11:59]-[12:35]
- **Strong close** — essentially a normal full-bodied candle with small wick against the direction of the close; a bullish candle with a massive lower wick and tiny upper wick (closing near its high) is "very very strong bullish" and tells where price wants to go. [15:01]-[17:49]
- **Exhaustion** — dojis with long rejection wicks after several candles of one-directional movement can show exhaustion. [19:29]
- **"Wicks don't lie"** — wicks and candlesticks tell the whole story of price action. [20:35]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Only use single-candle patterns: dojis (incl. long-legged, cross, inverse cross, dragonfly) and hammers. Ignore all multi-candle patterns (more than two candles), "mountain tops", W's, head & shoulders. [07:21]-[09:07]
- Never take a trade purely off a candlestick pattern — it is "only one confluence". [11:25], [14:25]
- Do not let a single candle read override bias from structure: e.g. a bullish doji with strong lower rejection inside a downtrend that keeps breaking structure down is not a reason to go long. [14:25]
- Make bias/decisions only on candles that have formed (closed), never on a candle that is still forming. [16:43]
- Always wait for the candle close for a break of structure. [16:43]-[17:14]

## Process / order of operations
1. Know which timeframe you are on (what one candle represents). [02:48]
2. Read each closed candle: open, close, body size, wick sizes and direction. [04:32]-[06:47]
3. Interpret rejection/indecision/strength in the context of the existing trend/structure. [14:25]
4. Homework: on your own instrument, find at least 10 examples of these candles (dojis/rejection wicks), screenshot and label them, and record how price reacted afterward. [20:03]-[22:19]

## Confluences mentioned
- Single-candle rejection/doji reads — minor confluence; "very very minimal in what you need to trade" but good to know. [14:25]-[15:01]
- Combining two candles (full bullish candle + doji with big lower wick) "in confluence together" to read exhaustion. [19:29]
