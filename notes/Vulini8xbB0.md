## Summary
A full liquidity lesson (Jul 2026). Covered:
- Liquidity = resting orders above highs and below lows (two-candle swing definition).
- Retail vs. smart-money liquidity: above a high sit breakout buys plus short stop-losses, and market makers fill their sells against them.
- A sweep only gives the *opportunity* to reverse; confirmation = a new trend forming (BOS).
- **Significant draws on liquidity**: HTF highs/lows (for him, 1H/4H), session highs/lows with exact ET session clocks, and "data highs/lows" from news candles.
- **Low-resistance liquidity**: stacked, unswept highs/lows ("trend-line liquidity"); trade toward it.
- **High-resistance liquidity**: a just-swept level that price is reversing away from; trade away from it.
He states he trades US indexes only during the New York open and says his strategy is liquidity plus "two other concepts". He lists BOS, FVG and IFVG as the other confluences.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity** — "just resting orders on a chart" ("cookies"). "Every single trade that I take is based off of liquidity. The market cannot move without liquidity" [01:07].
- **Liquidity sweep** — the liquidity is taken out, which activates the resting orders [01:07–01:41].
- **High / low** — two-candle pattern. High = up candle then down candle; its price = the highest wick of the two. Low = down candle then up candle; its price = the lowest wick of the two [05:00–05:33].
- **Retail liquidity** — above highs in an uptrend: (1) breakout buyers and (2) stop-losses of shorts = "two sets of buy orders". Below lows: breakdown sellers + long stop-losses = two sets of sell orders [07:14–10:35].
- **Smart-money logic** — to reverse an uptrend, market makers push above a significant high so retail buys fill their sells. Mirror for lows [12:47–15:31].
- **Opportunity, not certainty** — a push past a high/low gives "the opportunity to reverse". "Other confluences" decide whether it was taken [16:04; 20:28–21:34].
- **Confirmation** — "we just look for an uptrend to form out of the liquidity sweep": low, high, higher low, higher high, entry on the higher low (his simplified version) [23:12–24:19].
- **Significant draws on liquidity** — (a) **HTF highs/lows**. For his 5m/1m execution, "high-time-frame liquidity for me is like 1-hour lows or 4-hour lows". (b) **Session highs/lows** (NY, London, Asia). (c) **Data highs/lows**: the extremes of big news candles; "advanced advanced", not used often [27:36–28:45].
- **Session clocks (ET)** — **Asia 18:00–03:00**. **London 03:00–08:30** (technically to 11:00, but he ends it at 08:30 when NY pre-market starts). **NY pre-market from 08:30, NY session 09:30–17:00**. 17:00–18:00 = untradable "spread hour" [31:02–32:09].
- **Low-resistance liquidity ("trend-line liquidity")** — several stacked highs (or lows) close together, none swept yet. Price "actively" seeks it; it serves as both a reversal spot and a target [39:27–41:38; 42:12].
- **High-resistance liquidity** — "the most recent ... liquidity sweep that price is currently trading away from". Price is unlikely to return to it soon [44:55–46:01].
- **DOL as target** — market makers exit their positions at opposing liquidity, so stacked highs are where longs get liquidated [42:47–44:22].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Never buy just because a low was swept, or sell because a high was swept. Wait for confirmation (a trend forming / BOS) [22:39; 37:45].
- IF executing on 5m/1m, THEN take trades off **HTF (1H/4H) liquidity sweeps**, not 5m sweeps. A move after a 5m sweep is "probably not" long-lived; "higher time frames hold higher power" [29:18–29:52].
- At the NY open, mark the Asia and London session highs and lows (plus previous day high, 4H/1H highs and lows). NY frequently sweeps London's low or high and then makes the day's move [36:03–38:18].
- Only trade US indexes during the New York open: "It would be stupid for me to be trading a US index during Asia session or during London session" [35:28–36:03].
- Trade **toward** low-resistance liquidity and **away from** high-resistance liquidity [46:37; 47:11].
- After a sweep of highs and a reversal, those highs become high-resistance. Lows must be taken first "in order for us to even consider looking for longs again" [46:37].
- Targets: opposing session high and prior-day high (e.g., "Asia session high, previous day high ... trade completed") [36:36].

## Process / order of operations
1. Before NY open: mark HTF (1H/4H) highs/lows, Asia and London session H/L, previous-day H/L; note stacked (low-resistance) clusters [36:03; 39:27].
2. Wait for NY (from pre-market 08:30 / open 09:30) to sweep a significant DOL, often the London low or high [37:10].
3. Confirm on the LTF: a new trend forms (BOS / higher low, plus his other confluences: BOS, FVG, IFVG) [38:18; 48:16].
4. Enter; target low-resistance liquidity and opposing session/HTF levels; trade away from the swept (high-resistance) level [47:44].
