## Summary
A brief conceptual intro to order blocks (identification is deferred to pt. 2). An order block is a price range where large orders were filled and caused the market to change direction / break structure. If price revisits it **and shows a rejection** (buying or selling power out of it), more orders were likely filled there, and price likely continues in the direction of the original orders. He says the series will lay out each tool separately before combining them, unlike the Boot Camp.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Order block (OB)** — "a price range where large orders were able to be filled and caused price or market to change direction, break structure." [02:49–03:55]
- **Bearish vs. bullish OB** — Named by the direction of the orders ("whatever type of order block it is bearish or bullish, up or down"). [06:58]
- **Why it works** — "If price revisits that area ... because there were so many orders that were able to get filled before it's likely that there will be availability for us to fill even more orders in the same direction." [06:23–06:58]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- A revisit alone is not enough. Require a **rejection** out of the OB ("buying power out of it or selling power out of it") before assuming continuation in the OB's direction. [06:58–08:06]
- Bearish OB example: on a revisit + selling power/rejection → "price is going to continue lower". [08:06]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify where large orders caused a change of direction / BOS (method in pt. 2; not in this batch).
2. Wait for price to revisit the range.
3. Look for a rejection out of it.
4. Expect continuation in the OB's direction. [07:34–08:06]

## Changes vs. earlier teaching / contradictions
- The definition is broader than BC1's "the move up that causes the liquidity sweep" [qhuFg_OXdmE@03:10]. It is closer to BC2's "an area where orders were filled prior" [5v4G4xJVKIQ@06:06]: any range where large orders flipped direction. A sweep is not required here.
- Requires a rejection before acting, consistent with the BC2 resolution (reaction or LTF BOS required; framework §11 item 5).
