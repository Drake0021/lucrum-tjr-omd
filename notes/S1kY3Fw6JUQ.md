## Summary
This video teaches how to identify fair value gaps on a chart. An FVG is a three-candle pattern where the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap, and candle 2 is the large displacement candle that leaves the gap. Bullish FVG zone: from candle 1's high (top of wick) to candle 3's low (bottom of wick). Bearish FVG zone: from candle 1's low to candle 3's high. FVGs are retracement and continuation tools, never reversal tools. Only use FVGs in the direction of the confirmed bias (after a BOS). Never enter just because price touched an FVG: wait for a reaction or a lower-timeframe BOS or change of character. Instrument is not stated (chart examples, inferred forex or index).

## Concepts & definitions
- **FVG / imbalance / liquidity void (recap)** — An area with no resting orders opposite to where price wants to go. Price draws back into it and reacts. [01:46], [02:53]
- **FVG = three-candle pattern** — Candle 1, candle 2 (the large "super full" candle that creates the imbalance) and candle 3. [04:01]
- **Bullish FVG boundaries** — From the top of candle 1's wick (its high) to the bottom of candle 3's wick (its low). Draw a box over that range. [05:09], [10:49]
- **Bearish FVG boundaries** — From the bottom of candle 1's wick (its low) to the top of candle 3's wick (its high). [06:49], [11:20]
- **Validity test** — The wicks of candles 1 and 3 must not fill (overlap into) candle 2's body leaving no gap. If candle 1's and candle 3's wicks overlap, it is NOT an FVG, because the wicks show resting or hindering orders and the void has already been filled. [04:36], [09:37], [10:16], [10:49]
- **How to spot one quickly** — Look for areas of a big candle "that do not have wicks covering" them. [06:14]
- **FVGs are not reversal tools** — "fair value gaps are not used for reversals". FVGs (and equilibrium) are for retracements and continuations. [02:53], [04:01]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Use only FVGs aligned with the trend or bias. Bullish trend (HH/HL, BOS up) means look for retracements into bullish FVGs. Bearish (BOS down) means bearish FVGs. Don't look for opposing FVGs to turn price around. [03:29], [05:42], [06:49]
- Bias confirmation comes first: "we get a break of structure, we're confirmed bullish now", and then the FVG is used. [05:42]
- Never execute purely because an FVG is touched. Wait for a reaction (a bullish or bearish candle in your direction), OR scale to a lower timeframe and wait for a BOS / change of character / market structure shift. [07:57], [08:30]
- Not every FVG works. They are everywhere on the chart, so be patient and require confirmation. "It isn't necessarily as valuable as you may think." [07:24], [08:30]
- Hypothetical trade template (bearish): BOS down confirms bias, then price fills the bearish FVG and closes bearish, then enter. Stop above the previous high. Target the previous lows. [09:04]
- Alternative entry: price rallies into the bearish FVG, drop to a lower timeframe, BOS, then execute. [11:53]

## Process / order of operations
1. Confirm direction with a break of structure.
2. Find an FVG in the bias direction (three-candle rule, non-overlapping wicks).
3. Box it: bullish from C1 high to C3 low, bearish from C1 low to C3 high.
4. Wait for price to retrace into it.
5. Wait for a reaction candle or a lower-timeframe BOS/CHoCH.
6. Enter. Stop beyond the previous swing high/low. Target opposing liquidity (prior lows or highs).
