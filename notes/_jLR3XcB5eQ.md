## Summary
A no-chart discipline video on how to take a loss. TJR says losses are a normal part of a probability game, so a trader should welcome them as chances to learn rather than get upset. After a loss you should study why the market moved against you, not regret taking the trade. He says to learn and practise on a demo account before trading live, to follow a trading plan "robotically", never to revenge trade or overtrade after a loss, and to judge results over a year rather than a single day.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Trading as a probability / percentage game** — No consistently profitable trader has a 100% win rate. "The market is built to beat you." The same setup with the same confluences can win one day and lose the next, because every setup is "one of one" (market conditions differ every day). [02:18], [04:31], [05:06]
- **Market as algorithm** — "it's an algorithm", constantly reacting; different prices and different reactions every day. [05:06]
- **Yearly percentage-gain framing** — Judge performance by annual percentage gain, not day to day, week to week or month to month. [15:41]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- After a loss, do not revenge trade, do not overtrade and do not over-leverage. Stick to your preset risk amount and stop. [01:07], [12:21]
- After a loss, study why the market moved against you ("why didn't it go in my direction"), not "why did I take it". [04:31]
- Getting upset or frustrated after a loss is itself a sign you are doing something wrong. Recognise it, step back and keep the reaction short and minimal. [08:26], [14:35]
- Learn the skill on demo/paper trading first. Go live only once you are profitable on demo, and expect roughly a week of emotional losses after the switch. [10:06], [11:15]
- Follow your trading plan step by step and "be robotic": "you are the algo / EA / trading bot." [11:48]
- Consider taking days off when your probability of winning is low: news days, being up late, or being emotionally compromised (his example is a breakup). [13:28], [14:02]
- Do not blame the market or news for a stop-out. Take responsibility. If news stopped you out, the lesson may be "maybe I shouldn't trade before news." [16:48], [17:22]
- Don't take too many shots (avoid overtrading). [05:38]

## Process / order of operations
1. Accept beforehand that losses will happen.
2. When a loss happens, keep any emotional reaction minimal.
3. Ask "how can I learn from this?" and study the price action after the loss to see why it moved that way.
4. Do not revenge trade, overtrade or increase risk. Stop and wait for another day or setup ("there's always another setup").

## Trade examples
- He mentions that he lost about $9,000–10,000 on a GBPUSD ("GU") trade that morning. He kept his preset risk, did not revenge trade or over-leverage, and stopped trading for the rest of the day. No setup details are given. [01:07], [12:54]
- He recalls a past story of flipping $10K into $112K and losing it all the next day, which was what pushed him to learn risk management. [05:38]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Losing is expected and should be welcomed during the learning phase. "The person that fails a hundred times will have way more knowledge." [03:25]
- A lot of emotion after a loss probably means you over-risked or took too many trades (for example, "your fifth trade that day"). [02:52]
- Don't try to get rich quick. [09:33]
- Replace "I can't" with "I will". [10:40]
- There is always another day and another setup. "These markets aren't going anywhere." [13:28]
