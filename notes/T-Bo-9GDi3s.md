## Summary
Jan 2023 (pre-Boot Camp) "full strategy explained" on the S&P 500 (SPX500 CFD chart, London time), combining his building blocks: liquidity sweep → BOS (primary entry), with order block as the "first safety net" and FVG/EQ as the "last chance" later entries. Bias here comes from pre-market 15m structure (a 15m BOS/sweep+BOS pre-market = bullish), with 15m/5m highs/lows as liquidity and a 15m OB (marked wick to open/body) as POI. He bar-replays every S&P day trade of the prior week (all wins; CPI day skipped), showing a 4-TP management scheme (50% off at TP1 + BE; 50% of remainder at TP2; trail stop under 5m structure only after a candle *close* beyond the high). Then a GJ London example (forex: everything off at TP1) and a BTC daily/4H example. The last ~10 minutes are psychology/risk: 1% risk, one trade a day, no news/bank-holiday trading, make trading boring.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Entry hierarchy** — (1) liquidity sweep + BOS = ideal entry, "catching the top or the bottom"; (2) order block = "first safety net", still near the extreme; (3) FVG and equilibrium = "last chance / final hope", later entries. Use 2/3 when the BOS entry has bad RR or you missed it. [03:18] [04:26] [04:59]
- **Why sweep first** — a sweep marks the start of a new move; "right at New York Stock Exchange open there's almost always a liquidity sweep", either in pre-market or right at the open. [01:41] [02:13]
- **BOS** — confirms liquidity was swept and orders filled; must be a candle body close beyond the wick of the swing ("if we put this right on the wick it did not break actual structure"). [02:45] [25:43]
- **Order block drawing** — from the wick to the open/body of the candle (he highlights the wick, not just the body); if no wick on that side, use the body. Price either dips into the wick and goes, or "just tap[s] the body and then take[s] off". [09:27] [16:40] [26:16] [35:21]
- **Liquidity already taken** — a high violated by a later high no longer holds liquidity; don't mark it. [39:16]
- **Session times (London time)** — 13:00 = NY pre-market open ("1300 is New York Stock Exchange open" in his wording), 08:00 London, 00:00 Asia; indices trade off the actual NYSE open, forex off the 13:00 pre-market open. [05:32] [06:04]
- **Chart** — SPX500 CFD (FX broker feed, 24h) so pre-market and Asia/London are visible. [06:40]
- **"Use your brain" liquidity filter** — a far-away high/low on a Friday with no news is unlikely to be swept. [08:21] [08:53]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Bias: a 15m BOS in pre-market (or a pre-market sweep + 15m BOS) sets the day's direction ("this should already indicate we are bullish"). [08:21] [08:53] [17:12] [33:09]
- Mark 15m and 5m highs/lows as liquidity, plus the 15m OB created by the pre-market BOS (and 5m OBs). [10:00] [32:04] [37:37]
- A 1m BOS after a sweep of only 5m liquidity, against a pre-market 15m BOS, is NOT a trade ("higher time frames hold higher power"); expect the open "fake-out" into the 15m OB instead. [11:05] [11:39]
- Entry options: (a) on the BOS candle close after a sweep in bias direction; (b) on a reaction off the 15m/5m OB ("that reaction off the order block is enough for me to go long"); (c) at EQ/FVG of the BOS leg when the BOS is too large for good RR. [12:14] [20:33] [29:41] [33:41]
- Stop: under the liquidity sweep low / the OB low (longs), above the high (shorts). [12:14] [20:33]
- TPs at "high confluence areas", not Fibonacci extensions/Bollinger: TP1 nearest 5m high (liquidity), TP2 EQ of an HTF swing, TP3 next 5m high, TP4 1H order block; or the 50% of a big gap between TP1 and TP2 so profits are taken along the way. [12:48] [13:21] [34:48]
- Management (S&P): TP1 → close 50%, stop to BE ("I've won already on the day"); TP2 → close 50% of the remainder (25% left), may move stop under 5m structure — but only after a candle **closes** above the high (a wick doesn't count); final TP → fully out. [14:26] [15:00] [22:15] [22:48] [36:31]
- Management (forex): take everything off at TP1. [39:49] [41:28]
- No trading on CPI days ("we are not trading on news days"). [15:34]
- No trading on bank holidays (didn't trade MLK day). [48:48]
- Crypto: no session opens, so trade higher timeframes (daily sweep + BOS → daily OB → 4H BOS entry). [38:11] [42:36]
- Forex (GJ London): sweep + BOS only, "not even risking waiting for it to go into the order block"; 15m/5m liquidity as exits. [40:21] [42:00]

## Process / order of operations
1. Pre-market (from 13:00 London / ~8:00 ET): mark 15m highs/lows; note any 15m BOS or sweep+BOS → bias. [07:48] [08:21]
2. Mark the resulting 15m OB (wick→open); drop to 5m for more highs/lows and 5m OBs. [09:27] [10:00]
3. At the open, wait for liquidity against bias to be swept (or the OB to be tapped). [10:33]
4. Drop to 1m; ignore 1m BOS that only reflects 5m liquidity against the 15m. [11:05]
5. Enter on BOS close / OB reaction / EQ-FVG retrace. [12:14]
6. Stop beyond the sweep/OB; set 3–4 TPs at liquidity, EQ, OBs. [12:48]
7. Scale out 50% / 50% of remainder; BE then trail under 5m structure on closes. [14:26] [22:48]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Risk 1% per trade and take one trade a day ("call it a day"). [44:54] [45:27]
- Overtrading = over-leveraging: 10 trades at 1% losing = 10%. [45:27]
- Don't trade news days or bank holidays; only "optimal trading conditions"; no entry → no trade. [48:48] [49:21]
- Make trading boring/emotionless; wins and losses both expected. [47:08] [48:14]
- Patience (waiting for OB retrace after a BOS rip) is the hard part; dopamine hit comes on clicking the button. [46:00] [46:33]
- Trades ~30 minutes in the morning. [51:31]
