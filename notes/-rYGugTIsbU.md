## Summary
TJR does a pre-market daily bias across ES (S&P), gold, GU, GJ and other pairs. He then recaps a GU short closed for about +$200, effectively break even. The main lesson is about conflicting timeframes. When daily and weekly point one way and 4H/1H point the other, he mostly sits out. He follows the daily trend over the weekly, and he flips bias when the 1H closes above a key high. He admits the GU trade broke his own plan: it went against the daily bias and he took it at a tenth of normal size. He closed it when 15m structure broke against him late in the day. The better setup was GJ, where the daily was bearish and price tapped a daily order block. He says this daily-bias-plus-recap format will be the course format going forward.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Daily bias from multi-timeframe trend** — He states the trend on the weekly, daily, 4H and 1H for each instrument and looks for trades in the direction of the daily trend. [00:00], [02:13]
- **Daily trend over weekly trend** — When the weekly is down and the daily is up, he looks for buys and follows the daily. You can still trade weekly retraces. The weekly downtrend only counts again once daily structure breaks back down. [02:13]–[03:51]
- **Draw on liquidity (DOL)** — The next target. On S&P the draw is the monthly highs. [00:00]
- **Daily equilibrium** — The 50% of the daily range. With the daily up and the 4H down, he wants price to retrace into imbalances or daily equilibrium and then look for longs. He does not short the retrace. [00:33]–[01:41]
- **Daily imbalance** — A price range on the daily that price is "filling". On gold he does not trade while it is filling. [03:19]
- **Hourly liquidity** — Highs on the 1H that get swept, such as Asian session highs. [13:28]
- **Prior session highs as liquidity** — London session high, Asian session high, and the prior week's New York session highs. [07:11], [16:13]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- IF the daily and weekly are in an uptrend AND the 4H and 1H are in a downtrend, THEN do not take a trade against the HTF. Wait for the retrace to reach a daily level and a confirmation in the HTF direction. "You don't want to be trading down when you have all those high time frames saying up." [05:34]–[06:08]
- IF the timeframes are mixed (for example weekly down, daily up, 4H and 1H down), THEN sit out. [03:51], [06:08]
- IF the daily is bullish, THEN look for longs. Shorts into daily equilibrium are "not necessarily shorts that we want to take". [01:08]
- Bias flip: once the 1H closed above a lower high, he flipped S&P bias to long ("Cool, we can look for longs now"). Price never gave an entry. [18:25]
- Entry model (GU example): highs (hourly liquidity) are swept, then there is a break of structure to the downside, and he enters on that BOS. [13:28]
- Stop: above the swept Asian session highs, about 15 pips on GU. He notes this was larger than his usual GU stops. [14:02], [12:53]
- Management: if price breaks 15m structure against the position, especially when the daily bias also opposes the trade, exit. [15:08], [16:46]–[17:19]
- Time exit: he will not hold a stagnant trade late in the session. At about 2pm ET, after 2 to 2.5 hours of consolidation, he closed. [15:08], [17:19]
- A low-quality, counter-daily trade can be taken only at severely reduced risk. He used 15 lots instead of his usual 100, about a tenth of normal size. Better still is not to take it ("How could I improve? Don't take this trade"). [12:53], [15:41]
- News: Empire Manufacturing Index came out bearish for S&P. He does not let it change his bias. [00:00]

## Process / order of operations
1. Check news for the day. [00:00]
2. For each instrument, note the weekly, daily, 4H and 1H trend. [00:00], [05:02]
3. Identify the draw on liquidity and HTF levels: monthly highs, daily equilibrium, daily order blocks and imbalances. [00:00]
4. Choose the instrument with the most aligned bias plus an HTF POI tap. That was GJ: daily bearish, daily order block tapped, 1H BOS down. Camp on that instrument. [06:39]–[08:17]
5. Mark liquidity such as London and Asian highs. Wait for a sweep, then a BOS for entry. [06:39], [13:28]

## Confluences mentioned
- Daily order block tap combined with a 1H break of structure in the same direction (GJ). He considered this the best setup. [06:39], [15:41]
- 4H break of structure. He would ideally want this on GJ in addition to the 1H BOS. [06:39]
- A large fair value gap or liquidity void as the draw. [07:11]
- Sweep of London or Asian highs before the reversal. [16:13]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Being stuck in profit while price consolidates is better than being stuck in drawdown. [14:02]
- If a trade is not one you wanted to take in the first place, being out at break even is a good result. [15:08]
- Keep yourself safe. Do not take the risk of holding late in the day. [17:19]
