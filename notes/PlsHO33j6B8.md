## Summary
Covers imbalances other than standard FVGs: new day opening gaps (NDOG), new week opening gaps (NWOG), new candle opening gaps, and BPRs (balanced price ranges, which TJR defines loosely as an illiquid price range traversed by a swift move one way and a swift move back). NDOG/NWOG are treated as strong draws on liquidity / entry areas but are rarely traded (NDOG fills usually happen in Asia; NWOG more useful into Monday). New candle opening gaps are "useless". BPRs/illiquid ranges: once price re-enters the range it tends to move rapidly through to the other side — usable as targets or for entries; he shorted NQ off one on a Tuesday at 10:41.

## Concepts & definitions
- **New day opening gap (NDOG)** — gap between the prior day's close (16:59/17:00 ET candle) and the new day's open (18:00) after the one-hour break. Price "actively going to want to seek out" and balance it before continuing. [01:10] [02:18] [07:17]
- **Imbalances vs draws on liquidity** — FVGs, NDOGs and NWOGs are not required to be filled, but NDOG/NWOG have "a very high probability" of being sought, so he treats them as strong draws on liquidity. [02:51] [03:24]
- **Two uses** — (a) entry: trade from within the gap expecting continuation after it's filled; (b) target: if price is well away from the gap, target it. [03:24] [04:30]
- **New week opening gap (NWOG)** — gap between Friday's close and Sunday's open; usually larger; if unfilled by Monday open it's "a very, very good target". [03:57] [08:24] [10:43]
- **New candle opening gap** — gap between one candle's close and the next's open on any timeframe while the market is open; very rare and tiny; "pretty freaking useless" for trading. [04:30] [05:02] [05:36] [11:49]
- **BPR (balanced price range) / illiquid price range** — TJR's definition: an imbalanced price range with a swift move up through it followed by a swift move back down through it (or vice versa); the whole range is then treated as imbalanced. When price re-enters an illiquid range created by a swift move, it tends to move "just as swiftly" through it to the other end, then often continue. [12:24] [14:43] [15:53] [16:26]
- **Cause** — swift one-directional moves create ranges with "a lack of orders" (e.g. lack of sell orders in a bullish rip). [14:09]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- He does not take Asia-session NDOG trades ("really never looking for these trades"). [07:51]
- NWOG: unlikely to trade Sunday; Monday NY can trade toward an unfilled NWOG (target) or from it (entry). [08:24] [10:08] [10:43]
- BPR/illiquid range as target: after bearish confirmation at the top of an illiquid range, target the bottom of the range (price will traverse it quickly). [15:16] [17:00]
- BPR as entry: once the range is refilled, look for a continuation out of it (e.g. BOS then retrace then continue). [17:00]
- Trigger often = price cracking above highs / below lows bordering the illiquid range, then rapid traverse. [18:08] [20:23]
