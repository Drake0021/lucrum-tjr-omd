## Summary
TJR lays out the strategy behind his April–October 2025 results. The pieces are: liquidity sweeps of 1H/4H/session highs and lows; sessions (Asia, London, NY); four 5m "confirmation confluences" (BOS, IFVG, SMT, 79% extension close); and two "continuation confluences" (EQ, FVG). He explicitly says he removed order blocks and breaker blocks. He introduces "2B": when the HTF sweep happens pre-market or in the previous session, wait for an additional 5m manipulation after the NY open, after which SMT also becomes a valid continuation confluence. He backtests two days he did not trade, Oct 2 (short) and Oct 1, 2025 (long), both on ES, choosing the "leading" index by SMT. He shows how the 2B rule would have avoided a stop-out.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity sweep** — price takes a high or low. Breakout traders buy above highs in uptrends and sell below lows in downtrends, which lets market makers fill opposite orders and reverse price [02:48–03:22].
- **Sessions** — Asia open, London open, NY Stock Exchange open. He trades only US indices (S&P 500, NASDAQ) at the NY open. Prior session highs and lows are "very strong draws on liquidity" [03:54]. New sessions bring manipulation at the open [04:27–05:33]. NY pre-market starts at **8:30** ET [09:55].
- **Confirmation confluences (5m, and the same set on the 1m)**, any one suffices:
  - **BOS** — close beneath the most recent low in an uptrend (bearish) [12:07].
  - **IFVG** — inversion of "the lowest gap on this stack of gaps" (for a bearish change, the lowest bullish FVG of a stacked series gets closed through) [12:07].
  - **SMT divergence** — "one index makes a higher high and the other index makes a lower high, or ... one makes a lower low and the other makes a higher low". He compares the timestamps of the swing highs on ES vs NQ, e.g. 9:00 vs 9:15 [12:40; 13:50–14:23].
  - **79% extension closure** — draw the Fibonacci from the low to the high of the sweep leg (for a short); a candle **closing beyond the 79% level** confirms [14:56; 32:51].
  [05:33; 11:00; 42:27]
- **Continuation confluences** — limited to **EQ and FVG**. SMT is added only when 2B is active [06:05; 21:06–22:46].
- **2B rule** — "If the liquidity sweep happens during pre-market or the previous session, we wait for a five-minute manipulation" after the open. Reason: new money at the open must manipulate price. In 2B the 5m manipulation is a sweep of a 5m high (for shorts) or low (for longs) **without** changing the 5m trend set by the confirmation, i.e. no close above the relevant lower high. Because price pushes beyond a high, no EQ or FVG retrace exists, so **SMT is the only continuation confluence available** in that case [16:03–17:44; 19:25–22:46].
- **Leading vs. lagging index** — in an SMT, trade the index "leading in my favor": the one that did NOT make the new extreme, which is closer to the draws on liquidity. Bearish SMT: trade the index that made the lower high. Bullish: the one that made the higher low. "We don't want to take trades on the lagging index" [23:20–24:25; 26:40; 32:51–33:24].
- **Invalidated FVG** — an FVG that gets filled but price trades and closes through it "is no longer valid" [33:58–34:30].
- **Irrelevant levels** — a session high already taken without a reaction is removed from the marked levels [09:23–09:55].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Step 1: a liquidity sweep of 1H H/L, 4H H/L or session H/L is required every time [10:27].
- Step 2: a 5m confirmation confluence (any one of BOS / IFVG / SMT / 79% ext close) [11:00; 15:30].
- Step 2B: IF the sweep occurred pre-market (after 8:30) or in the previous session, THEN wait for a 5m manipulation (a sweep of a 5m high or low in the original direction) after 9:30 while the 5m trend from step 2 stays intact. "Every single time we get a pre-market sweep ... we'll get big legs" that stop out early entries [16:03–19:25].
- Step 3: a 5m continuation confluence — EQ or FVG; SMT too, only if 2B happened [15:30; 21:06].
- Step 4: drop to the 1m and wait for a 1m confirmation confluence back in the trade direction. The 1m will usually be trending against you during the 5m retrace, especially on the lagging index [25:32–26:07; 27:13].
- Step 5: enter, with the stop above the retrace highs (short) or below the lows (long) [27:47; 40:10; 44:06].
- Step 6: target the "previous draws on liquidity in our direction": session lows/highs, hourly lows, 5m highs, previous-day highs [26:07; 28:20; 40:10].
- TP1 is about 1:1. He would set TP1 at the nearest hourly lows "because it's a one to one right there". Multiple TPs with partials; the remainder is stopped at BE [28:20].
- If price makes a scary deep counter-move (a candle wick far below a not-closed-above EQ), you may "redo step two into step three": wait for a fresh 5m BOS and a new continuation confluence, provided the 5m trend has not been broken by a close [34:30–36:48].
- If the patient EQ entry never fills but the 1m breaks structure in your direction, with draws still available and RR still good, "we should still be taking that trade" [39:02–39:35].
- Avoids trading during the government shutdown week (Oct 2025) [00:00–00:32]. He checks high-impact news for the day before backtesting it ("Wasn't horrible") [30:35].

## Process / order of operations
1. Mark the session H/L (Asia, London; he uses his own session indicator), previous-day highs, 1H/4H H/L, on both ES and NQ. Remove levels already taken without a reaction [08:16–09:55; 13:50].
2. Note whether any sweep happened in pre-market (8:30–9:30) or the previous session, which decides whether 2B applies [31:42].
3. Wait for the sweep, then a 5m confirmation confluence.
4. If 2B applies, wait for a 5m manipulation after the open, and use SMT/EQ/FVG as the continuation.
5. Choose the leading index via SMT.
6. Drop to the 1m and enter on a 1m BOS/IFVG/SMT/79% ext.
7. Stop beyond the retrace extreme. TP ladder at draws on liquidity. Partials, then BE.
