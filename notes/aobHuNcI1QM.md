## Concepts & definitions

### Part 1: Mindset primer (00:00–1:24:44)
- **Goal of trading.** "Day trading is getting into the markets on a daily basis and being able to accurately predict with a high probability where price wants to go." Money is the side effect. Thinking "how do I make money today" leads to overtrading and overleveraging. [04:54] [05:26] [2:18:52]
- **Trading is a skill built by repetition.** He uses a basketball analogy and a construction-worker analogy: practice roughly as much as you consume, and never change the blueprint. [09:17]–[13:43] [15:24]–[22:02]
- **Adding a new concept.** Never add it in live trading. Add it "in back testing ... in private" until it's "overwhelmingly obvious". [25:52]
- **"TJR's psychology book".**
  - Learn a strategy and never change it.
  - Manage risk ("live to trade another day").
  - Don't overtrade. Don't overleverage.
  - Have one mentor only.
  - Don't overthink.
  - Execute.
  - Summary: "Do what you're supposed to do and don't do what you're not supposed to do." [28:36]–[33:42] [39:55]
- **His playbook style.** "This happens, then this happens, then this happens, and you press buy or sell ... and you don't do anything until stop-loss or take profits get hit." [33:42]
- **Mentorship students' setup.** They look at only **four timeframes** and **three confluences** from those four timeframes. He calls it "the step-by-step four time frame strategy." Students who took 1m liquidity sweeps or went to the weekly were "not listening." [58:12] [1:08:51]
- **Unprofitable mindset list.** Several strategies, several mentors, overthinking/overcomplicating, get-rich-quick, giving up quickly, short attention span, over-learning and under-practicing. [53:02]–[1:09:25]
- **Profitable mindset list.** One strategy, one mentor, simplify, get rich over a long period, stay consistent, over-practice and learn one step at a time. [1:11:46]–[1:14:36]
- **The tool kit he will "lay out".** BOS, liquidity, order blocks, FVGs, equilibrium, breaker blocks. If a tool doesn't make sense to you after months of trying with good risk and psychology, drop it. [1:17:30] [1:21:57]

### Part 2: Fundamentals (1:24:44–2:09:16)
- **Candle anatomy.**
  - Body = open and close (green: open at the bottom, close at the top; red: the inverse).
  - Wicks = the high and low reached during the period ("snail trail").
  - The timeframe = the time each candle covers. [1:30:21]–[1:34:27] [1:40:50]
  - He uses blue/black candles. Bitcoin is used for the demos. [1:29:13] [1:34:27]
- **High (swing high).**
  - "A two candlestick pattern. A move up, then a move down. A green candle, then a red candle." Take the **highest point of those two candles**.
  - The high exists only once a down candle follows. [37:07] [38:48] [1:45:24]–[1:47:05]
- **Low (swing low).** "A move down then a move up. Red candle, green candle." Take the **lowest point of the two candles**. [37:07] [1:45:59]
- **Edge cases for highs and lows.**
  - Two consecutive same-colour candles don't form a high or low. [1:59:41]
  - If the next candle's wick goes lower but it is still the red→green pair, the swing low is still at that pair: "I don't give a [ __ ] ... This is the low." [1:48:51] [1:59:41]
  - A candle so flat you can't see its colour (doji, open≈close) is ignored. [1:54:28] [1:56:10]
  - "I don't care how anybody else teaches it." [1:48:18]
- **Trends.**
  - Uptrend = higher highs and higher lows. Downtrend = lower highs and lower lows. Consolidation = sideways, "no sense of direction."
  - Trends exist on every timeframe. The easiest read is "look at where it's going." [1:50:33]–[1:52:15] [1:53:21] [1:57:16]
- **Break of structure (BOS).**
  - In an uptrend, watch the **most recent low**. A candle that **closes** below it (the body close, "not a wick") = BOS down, "now we're in a downtrend."
  - In a downtrend, a candle close above the most recent high = BOS up, now an uptrend. [2:00:53]–[2:03:08] [2:07:38]–[2:08:43]
  - A candle that closes **equal** to the high is not a break. [2:05:22]
  - In an uptrend, a lower high doesn't matter if no low is closed below: "as long as the lows within an uptrend are not closed underneath, then we're not going to break structure." [2:04:16]
  - "You ... will never ever be able to turn profitable just by identifying break of structure." [2:05:55]
  - No "prominent swing" filter is stated in this video.

### Part 3: Confluences / tools (2:09:16–4:44:57)
- **Liquidity.**
  - "Resting orders": buy/sell limits, stop losses, take profits.
  - It exists on every timeframe and "is required for the market to move / change direction / break structure / start a new trend / continue a trend." [2:14:16]–[2:15:28]
  - It is the magnet: "it determines where the market is drawn towards." [2:16:04]–[2:17:46]
  - He calls "draws on liquidity" "probably the most important confluence." [2:10:23]
- **Where liquidity lies.**
  - Above highs: breakout buyers plus stopped-out shorts, which means resting buy orders.
  - Below lows: long stops plus breakdown sellers.
  - Market makers fill their opposite orders there, so price "is drawn towards highs and ... lows." [2:23:51]–[2:27:47] [2:39:08]
  - It is a probability, not every high: if every high reversed, price would be "a constantly expanding consolidation." [2:28:19]–[2:30:36]
  - Examples: an SPX weekly sweep of highs before the 2022 bear market; the COVID monthly sweep of lows in 2020. [2:33:30]–[2:36:53]
  - Don't buy breakouts of highs or sell breakdowns of lows. Be on the market-maker side. [2:38:02]–[2:38:35]
- **Order block (OB).**
  - Definition: a "price range where large orders were able to be filled and caused the market to change direction / break structure." [2:41:28]–[2:42:04]
  - **Bearish OB** = "the leg up that sweeps liquidity that causes the break of structure to the downside." **Bullish OB** = the leg down that swept the lows, confirmed as a sweep by the following BOS up. [2:49:17]–[2:50:24]
  - "It's just the leg down. It's not any of the move up." For a bearish OB, only the move up counts. "How do we know it's a sweep? Because it breaks structure right after." [2:50:24]–[2:51:29] [2:52:02]
  - The OB is the whole move from the swing low to the swing high ("from this low all the way up to this high"). [2:54:18]
  - He skips an example "because the sweep is on an up candle." [2:55:27]
  - Use: if price revisits the OB and shows selling or buying power out of it, expect continuation. [2:45:19] [2:47:36]
  - It is a **continuation confluence**. It doesn't have to be revisited. [3:06:14]
  - Others call every retracement an OB. TJR counts only the initial sweep leg. [4:17:10]
- **Fair value gap (FVG) = imbalance.**
  - He uses the two names interchangeably. [2:58:47]
  - Definition: "an imbalance of market orders"; "a price range that lacks orders in the opposite direction of market sentiment." [2:59:54]–[3:00:29]
  - Identification: a three-candle pattern. Candle 2 creates the gap. It is easiest to spot as a big move, but a gap can also be tiny. [3:07:56]–[3:09:38]
  - **Bullish FVG** = top of candle 1's wick to the bottom of candle 3's wick.
  - **Bearish FVG** = bottom of candle 1's wick to the top of candle 3's wick.
  - If the wicks overlap, there is no FVG. [3:09:38]–[3:15:15] [3:22:02]
  - Use: if price revisits the FVG and buying or selling power comes out "with the same market sentiment," expect continuation. [3:02:13]–[3:03:56]
  - Not every FVG gets filled. "If you're only taking trades off of just fair value gaps ... you are going to be the worst trader of all time." [3:16:55] [3:19:42] [3:21:27]
  - For BPR he says "we're looking for the dick-sized candlesticks," not tiny gaps. [3:46:13]
- **Liquidity vs. continuation confluences.**
  - Liquidity is the **draw** or magnet: price seeks it to fill orders.
  - OBs, FVGs, EQ and breakers are **continuation confluences**: "they don't necessarily have to get hit." [3:04:30]–[3:05:06] [4:01:13] [4:13:48]
- **High-probability retracement areas.**
  - "Order blocks, breaker blocks, fair value gaps, and equilibrium." Wait for confluence or a reaction out of them.
  - A tiny pullback with none of these is not an entry. [4:14:24]–[4:14:57]
- **FVG invalidation ("my take on a inverse fair value gap").**
  - "I don't really believe in inverse fair value gaps, but I do believe in a fair value gap getting invalidated as a confluence." [3:22:34]
  - Use 1: an FVG in your direction that gets a candle **close** through it is invalidated. Stop using it, and the trade idea off it is dead. [3:23:44]–[3:24:53]
  - Use 2: an FVG **against** your bias that gets a close through it is a confluence, or even an entry. Example: bias bullish and BOS already in, then a close above a bearish FVG means "that can be a reason to go long on this candle," with stops under the old bearish FVG. [3:25:27]–[3:27:42]
  - He prefers taking it on the close rather than waiting for a retest: "I'm not that big of a fan of ... inverse fair value gaps where you have to wait for it to come back down in and ... get tapped into." A retest gives a better entry, but there are usually other confluences by then. [3:25:27] [3:38:56]
  - "In my eyes an inverse fair value gap is a pretty made-up concept." [3:26:34]
  - It never overrides an obvious opposite setup. Example: sweep, BOS, OB and breaker still pointing down, price still in premium. Closing above a bearish FVG there does not make it bullish. [3:28:19]–[3:29:25]
  - Summary rule: an FVG with your bias that is invalidated → drop it. An FVG against your bias that is invalidated → use it as a confluence. He uses it "occasionally ... as a supporting confluence and also ... as an entry confluence." [3:41:11]–[3:41:42]
- **BPR (ICT "balanced price range").**
  - Setup: a bullish imbalance is formed, then disrespected, then a bearish imbalance forms overlapping it (or the mirror).
  - The **overlap** is "a super solid ... imbalanced price range" that price will want to come back and balance. Expect continuation away from it. [3:42:16]–[3:45:39] [3:46:49]–[3:47:25]
  - He shrinks the box to only the overlap. [3:49:40] [3:53:34]
  - Usage: "most of the time ... during entries," on the 15m–5m execution TF, with the bias already set and after the sweep and BOS. It is "not ... make or break ... extra confluence." It helps pick which of several FVGs price will go to. [3:48:00]–[3:48:33] [3:54:43]–[3:55:49]
  - He says he discovered it himself before learning it was ICT's. [3:42:16]
- **Equilibrium (EQ), premium/discount.**
  - Definition: the 50% ("halfway point") of swing low→swing high in an uptrend, or swing high→swing low in a downtrend.
  - Above 50% = premium. Below 50% = discount (for buying in an uptrend). [3:58:06]–[3:59:15] [4:05:21]–[4:06:30]
  - Swing high/low = "literally just the higher high and the higher low within the uptrend." [4:04:47]
  - Tool: Gann box (he shows his settings, which the transcript can't show). [4:05:21] [4:08:12]
  - Grocery analogy: banks buy at 50% off. [3:59:51]–[4:00:24]
  - Continuation confluence: it doesn't predict reversals, and price doesn't have to reach it. Shallow pullbacks that miss EQ happen, "and you just have to accept that." [4:01:13] [4:09:54]–[4:10:29]
  - Caption/terminology slip: in a downtrend he calls the area above 50% the "discounted price range" when he means premium (the short zone). [4:08:47]–[4:09:20]
- **Breaker block.**
  - Others call it a "failed order block" because they call every retracement an OB. [4:16:36]–[4:17:45]
  - TJR's definition: "a retracement followed by a break of structure opposite of the current trend, which then makes that [retracement] price range lack buy/sell orders." [4:21:10]–[4:22:18]
  - Use: if price revisits it and shows pressure in the new direction, expect continuation "due to the lack of orders from the previous trend." [4:22:51]–[4:23:57]
  - **Bullish breaker** = the move **up** (retracement) prior to the BOS to the upside. **Bearish breaker** = the move **down** prior to the BOS to the downside. His verbal summary at [4:26:18] is garbled; the chart examples are consistent. [4:24:34]–[4:26:54] [4:27:29] [4:28:36] [4:30:17] [4:32:33]
  - When there are two candidate legs, either can be marked. He notes the leg before the sweep "offered a better entry." [4:28:02]
  - "Paired with a fair value gap is a pretty awesome confluence." [4:25:44]
  - If an FVG gets invalidated but a breaker remains, the idea stays alive: "we still have this breaker block that it could be drawing towards." [4:32:33]–[4:33:06]
  - "I'm cherry-picking these." [4:33:40]
- **SMT divergence.**
  - Definition: "one index forecasting what another index is going to do." [4:34:13]
  - **Bullish SMT:** at the same time, one index makes a higher low while the other makes a lower low. **Bearish SMT:** one makes a lower high while the other makes a higher high.
  - Use **only lows** for bullish SMT and **only highs** for bearish SMT. [4:35:52] [4:39:18] [4:43:16]–[4:43:50]
  - Requirements:
    - "First find your bias, because if you don't have a bias you'll be finding SMT divergences that are conflicting." [4:36:28]
    - The divergence should occur at a high-timeframe level or HTF liquidity sweep. [4:38:40]–[4:39:18]
    - "I do not like to use SMT divergence on the one minute. Typically my SMT divergence is going to be on the 5-minute or the 15-minute. And it also works really well for high time frames." [4:39:54]
  - Which index to trade: if NQ makes the higher low and ES the lower low, "S&P is lagging behind NASDAQ ... so that means there's going to be more opportunity on ES." [4:43:16]–[4:43:50] His live example later went the other way; see Trade examples [8:43:50].
  - He mentions that a trader (Darius) won't take trades without SMT. For TJR it is "just another confluence to add to your list of tools." [4:37:33] [4:44:25]
  - Later he describes an SMT at a 15m sweep as something that "kind of signifies a ... valid sweep." [8:44:23]

### Part 4: Systemizing (4:44:57–4:59:57)
- Confluences "standing alone are little to nothing." Put them together "in congruence." [4:45:29]
- **Systemize.** You need a step-by-step entry plan, so that journaling can show which confluence loses. Example: "80% of the time when I enter off of fair value gaps, I suck ... I'm no longer going to do that." [4:49:23]–[4:50:28] [4:57:43]–[4:58:17]
- Simplify the variables: **trade one pair** and **one session**. [4:51:02]
- Don't look at seven timeframes, and don't use a different TF set each day. Use a fixed rotation: TF1 gives input → go to TF2 → decision → LTF entry. He gives an illustrative example (daily→4H bias, 15m HTF sweep, BOS, entry confluence) and says "that's not my strategy ... don't use that." [4:51:35]–[4:54:56]
- Once the steps are complete, "it's brainless work. You press buy or you press sell. You set your stop-loss. You set your take profits." [4:56:03]–[4:56:37]

### Part 5: Strategy Creation (4:59:57–6:38:26), his systemized ES/NQ model
- **Trade horizon.** "I like to catch one to three hour moves ... ideally I'm in the market for 1 to 3 hours." He stays away from the daily TF ("where are the next couple days going to go? I don't really give a [ __ ] about that"). He treats the **4H and 1H as "our daily bias."** [5:02:18]–[5:03:27]
- **Bias = the 4H trend.** "Higher time frames hold higher power."
  - 4H uptrend (HH/HL, BOS up) = bullish. 4H downtrend = bearish.
  - "We're not trying to predict reversals ... Too bad, so sad if we take a loss because the 4 hour break structure." [5:03:59]–[5:05:21]
- **The 1H trend picks the execution TF.**
  - 1H in line with 4H → execute on the **5m**.
  - 1H opposite the 4H → still trade the 4H bias, but execute on the **15m** ("we need higher time frame execution points, higher time frame confirmation"). [5:05:21]–[5:08:15] [5:49:03]
- **HTF draws on liquidity.**
  - Mark **1H and 4H lows** if bullish, **1H and 4H highs** if bearish, during pre-market.
  - "Draws of liquidity doesn't change based on the [1H] trend." Use HTF sweeps only: "Not on the 15-minute, not on the 5-minute." [5:09:27]–[5:11:48] [5:24:12]
- **"Hands in our ass"** until a marked HTF level is hit. If it isn't hit, nothing happens. [5:10:37] [5:13:29]
- **Third confluence (on the execution TF).** One of four: order block, fair value gap, breaker block, equilibrium. [5:12:20]–[5:14:36]
- **Reaction (buying/selling pressure).**
  - You need a candle out of the confluence, not just a touch: "if we just enter right when they get hit, how ... do we know if they actually held?"
  - Long = "a green candle up and out of the order block / FVG / breaker, a green candle closing above equilibrium." Short = the red-candle mirror. [5:14:36]–[5:15:44]
- **LTF entry refinement (part 3).**
  - After price enters the third confluence on the 5m, drop to the **1m** and enter on a **1m BOS** "instead of having to wait for a full 5-minute candle to close."
  - On the 15m path, drop to the **5m** and enter on a **5m BOS**. [5:51:21]–[5:53:36]
- **Pre-market-sweep addition (part 4).**
  - If the 1H (or 4H) sweep and the 1H BOS already happened in pre-market or London, use the 1H confluences that move created (OB, breaker, FVG, EQ) "as our high time frame sweeps," and trade the continuation. [6:21:21]–[6:24:45]
  - "Our best and most awesome confluence is going to be liquidity sweep." But when price is "so far gone," don't wait for an unrealistic sweep. [6:26:25]–[6:27:01]
- **HTF-confluence addition (part 5).** Step 3 becomes "HTF confluence hit," which includes HTF liquidity sweeps plus HTF FVG/OB/breaker/EQ:
  - **4H and 1H opposed** → use **4H** confluences (and 1H/4H liquidity) → execute on the 15m.
  - **4H and 1H aligned** → use **1H** confluences (and liquidity) → execute on the 5m. [6:31:38]–[6:36:11]
- **Stop-loss purpose.** It forces risk management and marks "a specific price where our trade idea is invalidated." [5:54:43]–[5:56:24]
- **Take-profit philosophy.**
  - Use "probable areas where ... price could potentially reverse." No fixed-tick TPs or stops.
  - Use multiple TPs "so I can let my runners run." [6:08:13]–[6:09:55]
  - TPs must fit the trading horizon: you can't set 5m-entry TPs at daily levels. [6:05:58]–[6:07:38]

### Part 6: Forex session strategy (6:38:26–7:31:39)
- **Status.** An old FX strategy (GBPJPY was his favourite pair). "I've never used it on indexes and I honestly don't think it would apply that well because this utilizes every single session." His futures strategy also works on FX, and mastermind students use it there. [6:38:26]–[6:39:02] [6:53:43]–[6:54:16]
- **Session opens (New York time).** Ignore Australia. **Asia 20:00 (8 pm), London 03:00, New York 08:00.** [6:40:41]–[6:41:49] [6:53:43]
- **Session high/low boxes.**
  - On the **30m** chart, box each session's high and low **from the base (body) of the extreme candle to the wick**. ("This just came with market experience.")
  - These are different from marking every high and low as liquidity. [6:42:21]–[6:43:25] [6:52:02] [6:54:48]
  - He credits a book by "William ..." (vague) for the idea that session ranges get respected. He calls it something like support/resistance, though "I'm not a support and resistance trader." [6:44:34] [6:55:56]–[6:56:31]
- **Session boundary.** A candle that belongs to the next session is not part of the previous session's high or low. [7:09:29]–[7:10:38]
- **Flip.** If price closes above a session high on the 30m, treat that high as support. Look for a retest followed by a 5m BOS up. [7:07:18]–[7:07:51] [7:15:19]–[7:15:52]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)

**Structure / definitions**
- A swing high requires an up candle followed by a down candle. Its level = the max high of the two. A swing low = a down candle then an up candle; level = the min low of the two. Ignore flat dojis. [1:45:59] [1:54:28]
- BOS requires a **candle body close** beyond the most recent opposing swing. A wick doesn't count; a close exactly equal doesn't count. [2:02:35]–[2:03:08] [2:05:22]
- A BOS flips the trend state on that TF. [2:07:38]–[2:08:43]

**Bias & TF selection (AM model)**
- IF the 4H trend is up THEN look only for longs; IF down THEN only shorts. Don't trade 4H reversals. [5:04:34]–[5:05:21]
- IF the 1H trend = the 4H trend THEN execute on the 5m (entry trigger on the 1m). ELSE execute on the 15m (trigger on the 5m), still in the 4H direction. [5:05:55]–[5:08:15] [5:53:03]

**Execution sequence (on the 5m or 15m)**
1. After the POI is hit, wait for a BOS in the bias direction on the execution TF. [5:13:29]
2. Then wait for price to retrace into a **third confluence** on that TF: OB, FVG, breaker or EQ. Mark all of them after the BOS and see which one gets hit. [5:14:36] [5:29:20]–[5:29:54]
3. Then wait for a reaction:
   - a candle on the execution TF that **closes out of** the confluence in the trade direction (for EQ, a close back above it for longs); or
   - a BOS on the next lower TF (1m for the 5m path, 5m for the 15m path). [5:15:10]–[5:15:44] [5:52:30]–[5:53:36] [6:31:02]

- Trade the **New York session only** for indices. [5:21:20]

**PM session (1:30–3:00 pm ET)** [8:21:05]–[8:21:43]

2. Wait for a 5m or 15m liquidity sweep ("don't be going on the 1 minute").

## Process / order of operations

**AM-session model (ES/NQ), as finalized by Strategy Creation part 5** [6:29:49]–[6:31:38]
1. Pre-market: read the **4H trend** = bias (long only / short only). [5:03:27]
2. Read the **1H trend**: aligned → execution TF 5m (trigger 1m); opposed → execution TF 15m (trigger 5m). [5:05:55]
3. Mark HTF POIs in the bias direction:
   - 1H and 4H highs/lows (liquidity).
   - Aligned case: 1H FVG/OB/breaker/EQ. Opposed case: 4H FVG/OB/breaker/EQ. [5:24:12] [6:33:54]–[6:35:34]
4. Wait for the NY open (9:30 ET). Prefer the action in **9:50–10:10 ET**; from 9:30 to then price usually runs to the draw. [5:21:20] [8:24:34] [8:33:34]
5. When a POI is hit (sweep or tap), go to the execution TF and wait for a **BOS** in the bias direction. [5:13:29]
6. Mark that TF's OB, breaker, FVG(s) and EQ (Gann box from the swing to the swing) created by the BOS leg. Wait for price to retrace into one. [5:29:20]
7. Trigger: a candle closing out of the confluence on the execution TF, or a BOS on the next lower TF. Enter on that close. [5:15:44] [5:52:30]
8. Stop: beyond the sweep (preferred), else beyond the confluence, else beyond the swing inside the confluence. Check the RR to TP1; skip if poor. [6:04:18]
9. TPs: opposing 1H/4H liquidity and confluences. Scale out over 2–3 TPs. Never move SL or TP. [6:15:00] [6:10:28]
10. If no POI is hit, no BOS forms, or no third confluence is retested → no trade. [5:37:08]

**Mentorship instruction** [5:10:37]: "Find the 4-hour trend. If we're bullish, cool, we highlight the high time frame lows. We get our hands and we shove them into our ass and we don't do anything until those prices are hit."

## Confluences mentioned
- **Liquidity / draws on liquidity** — "probably the most important confluence" [2:10:23]. "Our best and most awesome confluence is going to be liquidity sweep" [6:26:25]. It is the required HTF trigger in the base model. The stop goes beyond it.
- **BOS** — required on the execution TF after the POI. Also the LTF entry trigger. [5:13:29] [5:52:30]
- **Order block** — one of the four third confluences and an HTF POI. A continuation confluence. [5:12:53]
- **FVG** — a third confluence and HTF POI. In the walkthrough it was the most frequently used entry. A continuation confluence. [5:25:19] [5:29:54]
- **Breaker block** — a third confluence and HTF POI. "Paired with a fair value gap is a pretty awesome confluence." [4:25:44]
- **Equilibrium** — a third confluence and HTF POI. It needs a candle **close** above (long) or below (short) EQ as the reaction. [5:15:44]
- **FVG invalidation (his IFVG take)** — an occasional supporting/entry confluence. Not required. [3:41:11]
- **BPR** — "not ... make or break"; extra confluence for choosing an entry zone. [3:55:16]
- **SMT divergence** — "just another confluence." Use on 5m/15m at HTF levels with a bias. [4:44:25] [4:39:54]
- **OB + breaker + FVG overlapping on the 5m** — he calls it "unicorn ... whatever you want to call it." [8:19:23] [8:08:06]
- **Session highs/lows (forex)** — "by far ... the best confluence to use" for forex. [7:30:29]
- **Time (kill zones)** — 9:50–10:10 and 13:50–14:10 ET are when setups typically happen. Not strict. [8:28:34]
- **Fibonacci** — only as a last resort for TPs when "literally nothing to play with" (forex Asian-session example). [7:08:55]
- **Support/resistance, Bollinger Bands** — mocked when students mix them into his strategy. "I don't even use that." [32:33]

## Risk & psychology rules
- **Demo first.** Don't trade a live account as a beginner ("get on demo and get some reps in first"). [1:01:31]–[1:02:04]
- **Don't overtrade or overleverage.** "If you're doing either of those, you have an issue bigger than trading." [40:28]
- **Risk management is more than "1% per trade".** "There's a lot more to risk management ... than just, oh, risk 1% per trade and stick your stop loss and stick your take profit." Study your win rate and RR. (This is the only % mentioned; no explicit risk % is prescribed in this video.) [7:49:31]–[7:50:03]
- **Never full-port, never full-port on news.** Always use a stop and a TP. [7:52:50]
- **Never move or extend stops or TPs.** A loss is a learning experience. [6:10:28]
- **Losses.** Being angry about a loss should be about trade quality, not money. Go back to the chart. [7:54:31]–[7:55:04]
- **Trade frequency.**
  - You won't find a trade every day: "If the shot's not there ... just don't take it." [57:04] [1:03:41]
  - "If you take 10 trades in a single day ... you're doing it wrong." [5:17:54]
  - He quotes the common advice "only take one to two trades a day." [13:11]
- **Strategy discipline.**
  - One strategy, one mentor, one pair, one session.
  - Don't switch strategies. A strategy should be judged over **months**, not days or weeks.
  - Add concepts only via backtesting. [1:21:57] [4:51:02] [25:52]
- **Treat it like a job.** Work hard and consistently on psychology, risk management and strategy (10,000 hours). [7:46:06]–[7:51:43]
- **Long-term horizon.** Expect to lose for the first 1–2 years. Think in 5-year spans. [7:43:55] [7:40:03]
