## Summary
TJR's in-depth SMT divergence lesson (ES vs NQ). SMT = the two correlated indexes disagree at a swing point inside a "highly executional price range" (an HTF liquidity sweep, HTF FVG, EQ/discount). In this video, the index that keeps the old trend (makes the higher high / lower low) is called the **"lagging index"** and is the one he **executes on**. The other index has already broken structure and "tells the future". SMT requires daily bias (only take SMT in the bias direction). He prefers 5m/15m SMT, not 1m. It typically forms in the 9:50–10:10 macro. After the SMT he drops to the LTF for BOS / IFVG / FVG / OB / breaker / EQ entries and targets the draw on liquidity that the leading index is heading for. He says most of his trade setups are based on it.

## Concepts & definitions
- **SMT divergence** — using two correlated indexes (his: ES and NQ) and finding the "lagging index". Also usable on EURUSD/GBPUSD (less backtested); BTC/ETH "sometimes", not recommended. [00:00]–[00:34]
- **Bearish SMT** — near a draw on liquidity / liquidity sweep, one index makes a high then a **higher high** (sweeping), while the other makes a high then a **lower high** and starts moving down / breaking structure. [01:08]–[02:50] [09:30]
- **Bullish SMT** — one index makes a low then a **higher low** (already moving up, has broken structure up), while the other makes a low then a **lower low**. [04:30]–[05:02]
- **"Lagging index"** (his definition in this video) — the index still continuing the old trend, i.e. the one that made the higher high (bearish case) or the lower low (bullish case). "ES has already made its move down and it's pretty much telling us the future of what NASDAQ is going to do". [02:16]–[03:22] [05:02]
- **Highly executional price range** — the HTF area where you expect a reversal: a draw on liquidity / sweep, an HTF FVG, equilibrium / a discounted range. The SMT must form there. [04:30] [14:32]
- **Fractal** — SMT happens on every timeframe; he prefers to look for it on the 5m (and 15m), not the 1m. [02:16] [08:26] [14:32]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- "SMT divergence is nothing without bias." With a bullish daily bias, only bullish SMT counts; ignore bearish SMT. Never enter on an SMT alone (e.g. a 15-second SMT with no bias). [06:41]–[07:49]
- The SMT must occur inside a highly executional price range (HTF liquidity sweep, HTF FVG, EQ/discount). [04:30] [14:32]
- **Execute on the "lagging index"**: the one that made the new extreme (higher high in the bearish case, lower low in the bullish case). Bearish: NQ made HH and ES made LH → short NQ. Bullish: NQ made HL and ES made LL → long ES. In the live example ES made the higher high → short ES. [03:22] [05:02]–[05:35] [10:37] [12:51]
- SMT timeframe: 5m (or 15m) preferred; he does not like going to the 1m for SMT ("I prefer to keep it on the 5 minute for entry"). He admits one example was a 1m SMT, which "breaks my rule". [08:26] [12:18] [14:32]
- Compare swings at matching timestamps on both indexes (e.g. lows at 10:45, 11:00, 11:15 on both). If both make the same structure, there is no SMT, so no SMT trade that day. [08:26]
- Timing: SMTs "typically ... happen within those macros, so from like 9:50 to 10:10, that's typically a hot spot". [08:58]
- After the SMT: scale down and use the entry model (BOS → FVG retrace respected; or IFVG; OB, breaker, EQ). Possibly enter "purely off the break of structure". [03:22] [05:35] [10:37] [11:11]
- Target: the same draw on liquidity the leading index went for or is heading toward ("price seeks out that same draw on liquidity that ES wanted to seek out"). [03:22] [11:11] [13:24]
- Stop: above the high made before the entry (short example: "toss stop loss above here"). [13:59]
- Many traders refuse to trade without SMT: "it's a pretty smart idea". [06:09] [14:32]

## Process / order of operations
1. Determine daily bias (see his daily bias videos). [06:41]
2. Identify the highly executional price range / HTF liquidity in the bias direction (e.g. relative equal highs, pre-market highs). [09:30] [14:32]
3. At the NY open (9:50–10:10 macro), compare ES and NQ swings on the 5m/15m at that range. [08:58]
4. If one index made the new extreme and the other failed, the one that made the extreme is the "lagging index" → execute there. [10:37]
5. LTF: BOS → FVG/IFVG retrace and rejection (or OB/breaker/EQ) → enter. [10:37] [13:59]
6. Stop beyond the extreme; target the draw on liquidity the leading index is seeking. [11:11] [13:59]
