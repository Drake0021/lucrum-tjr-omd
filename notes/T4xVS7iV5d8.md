## Summary
A discipline and homework video with no charts. TJR has viewers write down their three biggest trading problems in a notebook. These must be psychology, risk or emotion problems, not strategy problems. For each one, ask why you do it and which emotion drives it (greed, fear, overconfidence, revenge, FOMO), then fix the root cause rather than the symptom. Worked examples are overtrading (root: you need money, so get a job) and exiting trades early (root: fear or under-confidence, so risk less or go to demo). He says he feels greed in every trade but does not act on it.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Root-cause "pyramid" problem solving** — Problem, then why I do it, then what emotion drives it, then a solution aimed at the emotion. Cut the root, not the leaves (his "fungus under the lawn" analogy). He credits his football coach for this method. [04:31], [09:31], [12:19], [15:06]
- **Emotions behind bad habits** — greed, fear, overconfidence, revenge after a loss, FOMO ("one-of-one trade that'll hit a 1:10"), under-confidence, confusion, lack of bias, lack of knowledge. [03:25], [05:04], [07:47]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- A revenge trade or FOMO trade, especially a second trade after losing the first that day, will "probably" lose and won't hit TP. (He states this as his opinion.) [03:25]
- Overtrading (for example 5 trades a day when the plan says 1) is usually rooted in needing money. The solution is to get income outside trading so trading is not your only income. [05:36], [06:09], [07:15]
- Cutting trades before SL or TP comes from fear or under-confidence. Know your risk, risk less or go back to demo. [07:47], [08:22], [08:55]
- Once a problem is identified and fixed, never repeat it (the boss's "one more chance" analogy). [10:38]
- Don't act on greed when floating in profit. Let the trade run to the planned TP or SL. [16:12], [16:45]

## Process / order of operations
1. Dedicate a notebook page to "my biggest problems with trading".
2. Write the 3 biggest non-strategy problems.
3. For each, ask why you do it and which emotion causes it, and keep drilling down.
4. Find a solution for the root emotion, not for the surface behaviour.
5. Never repeat the fixed mistake.

## Risk & psychology rules
- Risking 10% per trade is not risk management (contrast with the 1% norm). [02:15]
- Every loss is your fault, not the market's. "The market does not give no [care] about you." [05:04]
- If trading is your only income and you are not profitable, you are trading from fear. Get a job. [06:09]
- Sell luxury items if greed or fear is driving your trading, and reward yourself only after the work is done. [14:00]
- Everyone has the same emotions. The difference is not acting on them. [15:39], [16:45]
