## Summary
"Putting the pieces together pt.1" — a no-chart, text-slide video that defines the five building blocks (liquidity sweep, break of structure, order block, fair value gap, equilibrium), states what each tells us about the market, and lists the four entry models built from them, ranked by risk. Models: (1) liquidity sweep + BOS; (2) liquidity sweep + BOS + order block retracement; (3) liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG fill; (4) liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG or OB + equilibrium (discount/premium) = "ultimate confluence", the foolproof beginner model. Viewers must choose which model(s) go into their trading/execution plan. Part 2 (chart examples of each model) follows.

## Concepts & definitions
- **The five building blocks** — liquidity sweep, break of structure, order block, fair value gap, equilibrium. [01:06]
- **Liquidity sweep** — a high or low getting taken out; shows the *potential* for orders to get filled; not yet confirmed. [01:06]
- **Break of structure** — confirmation that orders were filled and that market structure shifted; e.g. in an uptrend, liquidity sweep then BOS down → smart money orders got filled and price will go the opposite direction. [01:38]
- **Order block** — the price range where orders were filled: the move (e.g. up-move) that caused the liquidity sweep; price then drops because of it. [01:38] [02:11]
- **Fair value gap** — price range with a lack of liquidity: nobody going in the opposite direction; usually the big imbalance candle (up or down) with no counteracting orders. [02:11]
- **Equilibrium** — finding the discounted price between highs and lows. [02:11] [02:44]
- **Bullish FVG logic** — when bullish, a bullish FVG getting filled means price is revisiting a range with a lack of liquidity to the downside (against our bias); wait for it to be hit and react, or just to be hit, to enter. [04:58]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- **Entry model 1 — Liquidity sweep + BOS**: sweep shows potential fills, BOS confirms them → enter on the BOS, "because that's going to be the top of the move". [03:18] [03:51]
- **Entry model 2 — Liquidity sweep + BOS + order block**: if model 1's RR is bad, be patient and wait for price to retrace to the OB (the range where orders were filled) for an optimal entry; "we enter at the order block, stops above the highs" (short example). [03:51] [04:25]
- **Entry model 3 — Liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG fill**: steps need not be simultaneous — e.g. sweep, BOS, OB entry missed, price trends further, then you enter off an FVG. Enter on a reaction from the FVG "or just for it to get hit". [04:25] [04:58]
- **Entry model 4 — Liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG or OB + equilibrium** (price in discount for longs / premium for shorts) = "ultimate confluence", "a foolproof plan to enter if you are a beginner". [05:31] [06:05] [06:37]
- Risk ranking of models: "the higher up you get on this list the more risky"; models 2 and 3 "are kind of equal"; one is "most safe", one "most risky". Captions are garbled; interpretation: model 1 (sweep + BOS) = most risky, models 2 & 3 ≈ equal, model 4 (with EQ) = most safe. (inferred) [05:31] [06:05]
- Choose which entry model(s) you will use and write them into the execution part of your trading plan. [06:37] [07:10]
- Trade only on understood market logic (liquidity/orders), not "bouncing off the floor" or EMA crosses. [07:10]

## Process / order of operations
1. Liquidity sweep of a high/low (potential order fill).
2. BOS / market structure shift in the opposite direction (confirmation).
3. Enter immediately on BOS if RR is good (model 1); otherwise
4. Wait for a retracement into the OB (model 2) or FVG (model 3), ideally also in discount/premium per EQ (model 4).
5. Stop beyond the swing high/low (short: above the highs). [04:25]
