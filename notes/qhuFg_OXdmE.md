## Summary
This is the conceptual introduction to order blocks (part 1 of 3), with only rough sketches. TJR defines an order block as the price leg that runs into and causes the liquidity sweep: the move up that takes the high (for bearish setups) or the move down that takes the low (for bullish setups). Market makers filled orders across that range, so after the BOS price tends to retrace into it to fill more orders in the new direction. He ranks entries as follows. Liquidity sweep plus BOS is the primary "top of the move" entry. If you miss it, use a retracement play in order of preference: order block first, then FVG, then equilibrium. There is only one order block per trend, but order blocks exist on every timeframe. He treats "order block" and "accumulation area" as the same thing. At the end he shows GBPJPY moving only 34 pips on a PPI/FOMC day as a reason not to trade.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Order block** — "that move up that causes the liquidity sweep". For a bearish setup it is the leg up that takes out the high, before the BOS down. For a bullish setup it is the leg down that takes the low. The whole price range of that leg is where orders were filled. [02:36], [03:10], [03:42], [09:47]
- **Why price returns to it** — Market makers filled orders in that range. When price comes back, more resting orders are waiting there to be filled in the new direction, so they push price back into the OB, fill and send price further. This is similar logic to FVGs. [04:16], [04:48], [05:20], [05:53]
- **One order block per trend** — It forms from the liquidity sweep plus the start of a new trend. Within that trend there are FVGs and equilibrium fills, but no other OBs. The next OB forms at the next trend shift. [07:34], [08:07], [08:39], [09:13]
- **Every timeframe** — Order blocks exist on all timeframes, down to 1m, and "appear literally every single day... every hour". [09:13], [10:19]
- **Order block = accumulation area** — He uses the terms interchangeably. [10:52]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Entry hierarchy: liquidity sweep plus BOS is the start of the move and the top entry. If missed, retracement re-entry options in priority order: (1) order block, "first option every single time", (2) fair value gap, (3) equilibrium. [05:53], [06:28], [07:01], [07:34]
- Why the OB comes first: it is still at the top of the move, and it often gives a better entry than the sweep/BOS entry, because the swept extreme sits at the very beginning of the OB range and price can fill within it. [07:01], [07:34]
- FVG, OB and equilibrium are all retracement tools. [05:53]
- How to spot OBs is deferred to part 2, and trading them to part 3. [03:42], [10:19]
- (News filter applied) PPI/FOMC day: GBPJPY moved only 34 pips on the 15m since the US open, so don't trade. [11:24], [11:59], [12:33]

## Process / order of operations
1. A liquidity sweep happens. The leg that caused it is the order block.
2. BOS in the opposite direction confirms the shift.
3. If the sweep/BOS entry was missed, wait for a retracement into the order block (first choice), else the FVG, else equilibrium.
4. (Confirmation details are given in later videos.)
