## Summary
TJR defines the three skill sets of a profitable trader: strategy, risk management, and psychology — where psychology is simply the discipline to stick to the strategy and the risk plan (only trade when the strategy presents, don't over-risk, over-leverage, or overtrade). For risk, he has moved away from "risk 1% of balance" calculations to a fixed contract count per trade (per instrument), sized so the largest typical stop is acceptable (roughly 1-3% sweet spot, up to 3-4% on wide stops). He halves size when the stop is drastically larger than usual or on news days (e.g. PPI). For prop accounts, risk depends on each firm's drawdown type/consistency rules; many traders risk more in evaluations and scale down once funded.

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Only take a trade when your strategy presents itself; don't over-risk, over-leverage, or overtrade. [01:39] [04:28]
- Use a fixed contract size; choose it so the risk at your largest typical stop (his ES stops ~16, 28, 34-35 ticks) is acceptable; typical sweet spot 1-3% of account; he accepts 3-4% on wide-stop trades. [11:45] [12:20] [12:52]
- Fixed size evens out: wide stop → lower R:R (~1:1, ~3% gain/loss); tight stop → higher R:R (e.g. 1:4.81 full TP that day). [12:52] [13:25]
- Cut contract size in half if the stop is drastically larger than usual. [13:59]
- Cut contract size in half on news days with uncertain/choppy conditions (e.g. PPI). [13:59] [14:33]
- Prop accounts: tailor risk to the firm's drawdown type (trailing equity, trailing EOD, static), drawdown size, and consistency rules (both eval and payout). [15:38] [16:14] [16:47]
- Common practice (described, not mandated): higher risk in evaluation to pass fast, lower risk once funded/payout-eligible. [17:19] [17:52]
