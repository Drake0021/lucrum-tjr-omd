## Summary
Lesson on identifying and trading with the trend, shown mostly on gold (daily / 4h / 30m / 15m). TJR defines uptrends (higher highs and higher lows), downtrends (lower highs and lower lows) and consolidation, and says markets move on momentum so trends tend to continue. His core rule: higher timeframes "hold higher power" and set direction; lower timeframes are used only for an optimized entry in the HTF direction (e.g., 30m liquidity sweep + 5m break of structure when 4h/daily are bearish). He warns against taking every LTF sweep + BOS without HTF context, says you must know where price wants to go for the day, and suggests a line chart to simplify direction reading. Homework: weekly/daily/today directional forecasts on one or two instruments.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Why trend matters** — it dictates where the market is going; markets move on momentum, so once trending, odds favor continuation (basketball rolling analogy). A "market structure shift" starts a trend. [01:07]-[01:39]
- **Uptrend** — higher highs and higher lows: price pushes past the prior high to make a new higher high, and makes a low higher than the previous low; continues until the trend breaks. [02:11]-[02:42]
- **Downtrend** — lower highs and lower lows (mirror of uptrend). [02:42]-[03:16]
- **Trend break / change** — in an uptrend, when a new lower low is made ("we just surpassed this" low), structure is broken and odds favor a flip to the opposite direction "for a little bit". [04:26]-[05:00]
- **Extension in a trend** — retracement depth varies (small or big lows), but "the extension always goes past the high" in an uptrend. [06:39]-[07:12]
- **Consolidation** — the third way the market moves (besides up/down trends): price static/stagnant, not continuing higher even if a marginal higher high/higher low prints; ended by a break of the low ("break the floor") = market structure change. [12:14]-[13:22]
- **Impulse vs retracement size** — in a downtrend the moves higher are always smaller than the moves lower (and vice versa in an uptrend). [20:34]-[21:06]
- **Higher timeframes hold higher power** — an LTF (e.g., 15m) structure break against the 4h/daily/weekly trend is not to be followed; LTF is used in confluence with the HTF trend. [08:19]
- **Premium / equilibrium** — mentioned in passing: on a downtrend he is "looking for it to hit equilibrium" and notes "we're in a premium right now" before looking for an LTF trend shift down (not yet defined in the course). [19:30]
- **Draw on liquidity** — mentioned as a future topic ("where is the draw of liquidity"); not defined here. [25:01]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Trade in the direction of the HTF trend; never against it. "The trend is your friend." [05:33]-[06:39], [09:58]
- Do not try to catch retracements (counter-trend moves) — you don't know how deep they go; trade momentum. [06:39], [09:58]
- Do not enter simply when a high gets pushed above (trend-continuation break) — that can get you stopped out; the LTF is used to optimize the entry. [05:00]-[05:33]
- If HTF (4h/daily/weekly) is bearish and the 15m breaks structure up, ignore it and wait for the 15m to break structure back down, then trade with the HTF. [08:19]-[08:52]
- Liquidity sweeps and trend changes are traded on LOWER timeframes only, in line with HTF: e.g., liquidity sweep on the 30m + break of structure down on the 5m while 4h and daily are bearish = "perfect", catching the top of the next leg down. [10:34]
- Do not take every 1m/5m liquidity sweep + BOS ("ticky-tack" setups) without HTF confluence — not high probability. Use at least the 1h, and know where price wants to go on the 4h or daily. [16:10]-[17:16]
- For intraday trades you must know where price wants to go for the day, or "you're going to lose". [17:16]-[17:48]
- When HTF says down but price is currently going up, that is the opportunity to find an area to go short. [26:07]
- Trading the basic trend alone can be profitable; his more refined model exists for optimized entries → tighter stops → higher RR. [13:56]-[14:29]
- Focus on one or two instruments only during the boot camp. [27:13]

## Process / order of operations
1. Zoom out to HTF (weekly/daily/4h); identify the trend via HH/HL or LH/LL (optionally on a line chart). [04:26], [18:24]-[19:30], [27:48]
2. Decide where price wants to go for the week. [24:27]
3. Scale to the daily: where will price go in the next couple of days. [25:01]
4. Determine where price wants to go today. [25:33]
5. Drop to LTF (30m/15m/5m) and wait for a trend shift/structure break in the HTF direction (after a pullback into premium for shorts — inferred from his gold example) for an optimized entry. [08:52], [19:30]-[20:01]
6. Volume/direction for the day: wait through Asia and London, "once New York session kicks in we'll be able to understand where price wants to go". [20:01]

## Risk & psychology rules
- His own biggest struggle: trading against trend/bias on LTF ("this could be a little liquidity sweep and then a retracement"); his losing week came from focusing on small timeframes and ignoring HTF. [09:25], [21:39]
- The market won't move for you; don't hope ("please go higher"). [23:21]
- Focus on one or two instruments; losing on many → narrow down. [27:13]
- Journal the weekly/daily forecasts. [24:27]
