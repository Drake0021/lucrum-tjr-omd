## Summary
TJR classifies his confluences into three roles — liquidity (reversal opportunity / "potential to fill orders"), confirmation (BOS, IFVG: orders were filled, trend changed), continuation (FVG, equilibrium: new trend will continue) — and defines the inverse fair value gap (IFVG): an FVG within the current trend that gets a full candle-body close through it, flipping it from a continuation confluence into a confirmation of trend change. IFVGs often appear before BOS, giving earlier entries, tighter stops and better R:R; he says he uses IFVG almost every day, more than BOS. With stacked FVGs (consecutive gaps with no retrace candle between), only a close through the LAST (outermost — bottom for bullish stack) gap counts as the inversion.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity = reversal confluence (step 1)** — pushing above a high/below a low gives price the opportunity (not obligation) to fill orders and reverse. He prefers selling tops/buying bottoms for better R:R and odds. [00:35] [01:08] [01:41]
- **Confirmation confluence (step 2)** — confirms orders were actually filled and trend changed: BOS and IFVG. [01:41] [02:17] [22:19]
- **Continuation confluence (step 3)** — shows the new trend will continue: FVG (and equilibrium, next lesson). Required because a sweep + BOS alone "could just be a higher time frame retrace". [02:50] [03:23] [03:57]
- **Inverse fair value gap (IFVG)** — "a fair value gap that gets disrespected within the current trend that it's in"; e.g. in an uptrend a bullish FVG gets a candle close below its bottom → bearish confirmation. Used "as a break of structure or as a confirmation confluence". [05:03] [05:36] [06:10]
- **Invalidation/inversion trigger** — a full candle-body close beyond the far edge of the gap (below bottom of bullish FVG; above top of bearish FVG). A candle closing inside the gap or a wick through it is NOT an inversion. [09:11] [20:40]
- **Stacked FVGs** — two or more FVGs back-to-back with no retrace (no opposite-color candle) between them form one imbalanced price range. Price may fill the upper one and continue, or close through it and fill the lower one. [13:44] [14:18] [14:53]
- **Which gap to invert in a stack** — for a bearish inversion, the BOTTOM (last) FVG of the bullish stack must be closed below ("the last fair value gap that is holding up the trend"); closing below the upper gap "doesn't mean anything". Mirror for bullish (top of a bearish stack, inferred). [15:27] [16:34] [18:17]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Strategy skeleton: opportunity to fill orders (liquidity sweep) → confirmation orders filled (BOS or IFVG) → continuation (FVG / equilibrium) → target opposing draws on liquidity. [03:57] [04:31]
- IFVG triggers on candle close only (same as BOS). [20:40]
- In a stack, require close beyond the outermost gap before flipping bias. [16:34]
- Don't trade IFVGs "willy-nilly" in isolation; needs the full context. [10:55]
- Prefer IFVG entry when it precedes BOS: tighter stop, better R:R. [09:44] [11:28]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify trend (closing beyond most recent swing). [08:36]
2. Note the most recent FVG supporting that trend (and the bottom/top of any stack). [08:36]
3. After a liquidity sweep, watch for a candle close through that FVG = IFVG confirmation. [09:11]
4. After inversion, look for continuation in new direction (price respects new-direction FVGs). [21:13]
