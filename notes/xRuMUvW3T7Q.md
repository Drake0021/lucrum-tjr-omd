## Summary
TJR states his full late-2025 model as a six-step "cheat sheet". Step 1 is a sweep of HTF liquidity (1H/4H highs or lows, session highs or lows). Step 2 is a 5m "confirmation confluence": BOS, IFVG, 79% extension close or 5m SMT. Step 2B adds a 5m liquidity sweep at the NY open, required only when step 1 happened before the open. Step 3 is a 5m "continuation confluence": EQ or FVG, or SMT if 2B was used. Then a 1m confirmation confluence is the entry, and targets are the opposing draws on liquidity. He backtests it on two random days in October 2025, covering three setups (two wins, one loss on ES that won on NQ), on ES vs NQ. He uses SMT to choose the index. The video closes with Mark Douglas probability psychology: losses are part of the strategy.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Draws on liquidity / key levels** — "highs and lows within the market". The HTF draws are 1H highs and lows, 4H highs and lows, and session highs and lows. He also marks previous-day H/L, London and Asia H/L, and 30-minute lows [01:41; 10:22–11:30].
- **Confirmation confluence (5m or 1m)** — any ONE of four: break of structure, inverse FVG, "79% extension closure" (a close beyond the 79% Fibonacci extension level), or SMT divergence. "We don't need every single one of these to happen. We just need one because each one of these confluences signifies the same thing." It proves the sweep was valid and order flow changed [02:14–02:46; 03:18; 08:12].
- **Continuation confluence (5m)** — EQ or FVG. A 5m SMT counts too, but **only if 2B happened** [07:07; 15:21; 22:02].
- **Step 2B, NY-open 5m manipulation** — if the HTF sweep (step 1) happens before the NY open (London or pre-market), you must also wait for a 5m liquidity sweep after the open. Reason: "when New York market opens, new money is coming into the market ... there's almost always going to be some form of manipulation." If step 1 happens at or after the NY open, 2B is skipped because the open's manipulation was the HTF sweep [03:51–06:35].
- **Logic of the steps** — (1) fill orders by taking liquidity; (2) confirm the orders were filled through a change in order flow; (3) confirm the trend will continue by pushing into a continuation confluence; then a 1m confirmation; then enter [06:35–07:40].
- **1m behaviour during a 5m retrace** — while the 5m retraces into EQ/FVG, the 1m will "more often than not" break structure against your trade direction. That is expected; you wait for the 1m to change back in your direction [15:53–16:27].
- **SMT divergence (ES vs NQ)** — one index makes a higher high over a 5m high while the other makes a lower high, which is bearish SMT. Example: NQ 955 above its 935 high, ES a lower high [13:42].
- **Lagging index / index choice** — trade the index that did NOT make the new extreme. On a bearish SMT where NQ made the higher high, NQ "is the lagging index ... more bullish than the S&P", so short ES [14:15–14:48].
- **Invalidation of step 3** — on a short, if price closes above EQ during the continuation retrace, "that invalidates step number three". Re-evaluate the steps for the other direction [22:35].
- **Swept levels that don't react** — highs taken in pre-market with no reaction are removed from the level list [10:55; 19:46].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Every trade must come from an HTF liquidity sweep (1H/4H/session H/L). The sweep can occur in London, pre-market or NY [01:08–01:41].
- Never buy or sell just because a key level was hit ("that would be stupid and we would lose all of our money"). A confirmation confluence is required [02:14; 03:18].
- IF the HTF sweep plus the 5m confirmation occurred before 9:30 ET, THEN after the open also require a 5m liquidity sweep (2B) in the same direction as the trade [03:51–05:28].
- IF the HTF sweep occurred at the NY open, THEN skip 2B [06:00].
- After step 2 (and 2B if needed), wait for price to retrace into a 5m EQ or FVG (or a 5m SMT if 2B applied) [07:07; 09:49].
- When price is inside the 5m continuation confluence, drop to the 1m and enter on the first 1m confirmation confluence (BOS, IFVG, 79% extension close or SMT) back in your direction [08:12; 17:35].
- Stop: above the highs that formed the retrace (short) or under the lows (long) [10:22; 17:35; 24:47]. No point size is stated.
- Targets: the opposing draws on liquidity in your direction: London lows, 30m lows, previous-day lows, 1H/4H lows, Asia lows [08:45; 18:07].
- Management: at TP1, take partials and move the stop to break-even; then TP2, TP3 and a final TP [18:07].
- When ES and NQ diverge at the sweep, trade the index that failed to make the new extreme (the one "more" in your direction) [14:15].
- If a close beyond the 5m EQ invalidates the short-side step 3, re-check whether the opposite side's checklist (including 2B) is now satisfied [22:35–24:15].
- No setup that day means no trade, which "is a drop in the probability bucket" [29:40].

## Process / order of operations
1. Before the open, mark the draws on liquidity on both ES and NQ: 4H H/L, 1H H/L, session H/L (Asia, London), previous-day H/L and 30m H/L. Drop pre-market-swept levels that showed no reaction [10:22–11:30; 19:11–20:22].
2. Sit on the 5m chart [11:30].
3. Step 1: an HTF level is swept.
4. Step 2: a 5m confirmation confluence (BOS / IFVG / 79% ext / SMT).
5. Step 2B (only if 1 happened pre-open): a 5m liquidity sweep after 9:30. It can come together with step 2, e.g. a 5m sweep that forms a 5m SMT [13:42].
6. Step 3: price retraces into a 5m EQ or FVG (or SMT if 2B).
7. Step 4/5: drop to the 1m and enter on a 1m confirmation confluence.
8. Step 6: target the opposing draws on liquidity. Partial at TP1 and move to BE.

## Risk & psychology rules
- The strategy gives a probability edge, not a 100% win rate. Losses are "required ... part of the strategy". Don't abandon a strategy after a few losses (small sample) [25:20; 26:58; 31:21–32:27].
- From Mark Douglas, *Trading in the Zone*: execute without hesitation when the strategy says so, don't trade when it gives nothing, and don't get upset at losses [29:40–30:14].
- The strategy is primarily capital preservation: "a safety net ... to only be able to execute when price is moving in our favor" [30:48].
