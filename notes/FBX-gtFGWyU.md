## Summary
TJR redefines "daily bias" as the direction price will take **during his trading window** (the first 1h–1h30m after the open), not the daily candle. He sets that bias with draws on liquidity (DOL, his "cookies"). He asks four questions: has a DOL been swept pre-market or at the open, and is price reacting away from it? Where are the opposing DOLs, how many are there and which is closest? Is price printing a BOS toward them? Is there a continuation entry? He walks through that day's ES short (pre-market high swept → BOS → bearish FVG retrace entry → TP at 5m and London lows; 2 TPs hit, rest stopped at BE). He also shows a hypothetical NQ long (no sweep, more/closer upside DOLs, retrace to EQ → IFVG long) and a higher-timeframe swing example (15m BOS/IFVG). Timeframes he uses: 4H, 1H, 5m, 1m.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Daily bias** — "where price is going to go during just the entire day or during your specific time frame". He trades the first 1h–1h30m, so his bias is for that window only: "I don't necessarily have to be correct about what the bias is for the entire day" [00:00–01:06]. A trader executing on the 15m would use roughly a 4-hour window instead [01:38].
- **His timeframes** — 4-hour, 1-hour, 5-minute and 1-minute [00:32].
- **Instruments** — S&P 500 and Nasdaq [01:38–02:12].
- **Draw on liquidity (DOL) / "cookies"** — liquidity price is drawn to: highs/lows (5m lows, London session lows, 4H highs, hourly highs, market-open high) and the **new week opening gap** ("another strong draw") [03:18; 09:58; 17:09]. "Price moves towards draws on liquidity. That's the only way that price moves" [14:56].
- **Valid swept DOL** — a DOL that was taken (e.g., a pre-market high) and that price is reacting away from: "this was a valid draw on liquidity, it was swept, and now we're reacting off of it" [02:45–03:18].
- **Why price targets opposing liquidity** — market makers who filled sells at the swept high must buy them back lower, so price must push under lows where sellers are [03:51–04:24].
- **Cookie-monster heuristic** — choose the side with **more** DOLs that are **closer** ("which cookies are closer and which has more cookies") [07:45–08:50; 09:58].
- **His trade template** — "manipulation, break of structure, this fill of this fair value gap, and then the breakdown was the continuation. That's pretty much the majority of every single trade that I take" [10:32].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Bias step 1: IF a DOL was swept recently (pre-market or at the open) AND price is reacting away from it, THEN bias = away from it, toward the opposing DOLs [02:45–03:51].
- Bias step 2: mark all opposing DOLs. Price "doesn't necessarily need to go all the way down to the bottom low" but should reach at least some of them [04:24].
- Bias step 3: compare sides. Favor the side with more DOLs that are closer. At the open, if price is very close to one cluster, "it would be stupid for us to want to target these highs before targeting these lows" [08:17–08:50].
- Bias step 4 (required): price action must confirm, i.e. a BOS toward the DOL, then a continuation. "Having draws on liquidity is one thing ... does that mean price has to go seek it out? No" [13:50–14:22]. If yes to all, "you've established your daily bias" [14:22].
- No-trade filter: "If we don't have an area that price wants to go towards, then you should not be taking that trade at all" [14:56].
- Top-down search for DOLs: start on the 4H. If there is little nearby, drop to the 1H, then to lower timeframes until DOLs appear [06:07–06:39].
- A move that takes **no** DOL leaves the bias intact. NQ dumped at the open without taking any downside DOL, so the bullish bias "is still valid"; he read the drop as a HTF retrace or gap fill [07:12].
- Entry needs a confluence. An impulsive one-way move "doesn't give us much of an entry at all" ("massive amounts of candles to the downside ... no sort of entry confluence"), so wait for structure and a retrace [09:24–09:58].
- Don't enter on the first push. Wait for the retrace, which also confirms or rejects the direction [11:38–12:11].
- Long trigger (NQ example): after the retrace into EQ, price **inverses a bearish FVG** that had been respected twice before. "The second that I see this gap get inverse, that's where I'm going to want to long" [12:11–12:45].
- Stop: under the lows (the retrace low) [12:45]. In the HTF example, "either under this low or underneath the second low" [18:14].
- Targets: successive DOLs (highs, market-open high, 4H highs, gap fill). In the NQ example the market-open high ≈ 1:1.5 R and the next high ≈ 1:1.7 R [12:45–13:18].
- Management (ES trade): multiple TPs; after TP1/TP2 the remainder was stopped at break-even [04:57].
- If price trades with the first market and a second one sets up later, he prefers the one that sets up first ("I just preferred the trade on the S&P 500 because it happened first") [06:07].

## Process / order of operations
1. Define your bias window from your execution timeframe (his: first 60–90 min after the open; 4H/1H/5m/1m) [00:32–01:06].
2. Check whether a DOL was swept in pre-market or at the open, and whether price is reacting away from it [02:45].
3. Mark opposing DOLs top-down (4H → 1H → LTF): highs/lows, session H/L, new week opening gap [06:07; 17:09].
4. Weigh the sides: more and closer DOLs win [08:17].
5. Wait for manipulation → BOS toward the DOL → retrace (FVG/EQ) → continuation trigger (FVG respected/broken away, or IFVG) [10:32; 12:11].
6. Enter. Stop beyond the retrace or manipulation low/high. TPs at successive DOLs; BE after TPs [04:57; 12:45].

## Confluences mentioned
- Draws on liquidity — the basis of bias; "without draws on liquidity ... your trade is essentially nothing" [14:56].
- Liquidity sweep / manipulation — first step of nearly every trade [10:32].
- BOS — required price-action confirmation toward the DOL [14:22].
- Bearish/bullish FVG — retrace entry zone (ES short) [04:57].
- Equilibrium / "fib levels" — retrace zone (NQ example) [11:05–11:38].
- Inverse FVG — long trigger (NQ, HTF example) [12:45; 17:42].
- New week opening gap — counted as a strong DOL [17:09].

## Changes vs. earlier teaching / contradictions
- Timeline: Oct 2026, CANONICAL era. It confirms sweep → BOS → retrace into FVG/EQ → trigger (IFVG) → targets at DOLs (session lows, 5m/hourly/4H H/L).
- **Bias redefined.** The Boot Camp used daily market structure (latest daily BOS). Here bias = the direction within his 60–90 min window, from swept vs. remaining DOLs plus LTF price action. The daily candle direction is not required. This differs from §2.18 / §3 of the framework.
- **Time window: "first hour to hour 30 minutes of price action"** [00:00]. This partially **answers OPEN 7** (entry cutoff ≈ 10:30–11:00 ET).
- **Pre-market sweeps count.** A pre-market sweep of a high can set the day's bias [02:45]. This partially **answers OPEN 5** (which liquidity counts for ES): pre-market highs, 5m lows, London session lows, hourly/4H highs, market-open high, new week opening gap.
- New-week opening gap is introduced as a DOL (new vs. the Boot Camp).
- IFVG used as the entry trigger (NQ, HTF example). Order blocks not mentioned.
- Index choice: he trades both ES and NQ and takes whichever sets up first. **No SMT** reasoning is used here.
- TP: successive DOLs with BE on the runner after TPs (matches earlier practice). TP R-multiples ~1.5–1.7 in the NQ example.
- Not mentioned: news days, contracts/risk, daily limits, stop size in points.
