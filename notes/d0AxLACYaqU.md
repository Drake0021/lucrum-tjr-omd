## Summary
This is a conceptual introduction to fair value gaps (FVG), which TJR also calls imbalances or liquidity voids. There are no charts. An FVG is a price range with no opposing orders, so price moved through it in one big candle without hesitation. Retracements in a trend tend to draw back into these voids, where market makers can execute again. TJR uses FVGs mainly to locate where a retracement will go and for secondary or continuation entries, not as the primary entry signal. The primary signal is the liquidity sweep. The setup is: a higher-timeframe FVG is filled during a retracement in a trend, then a lower-timeframe break of structure triggers the entry.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Fair value gap = imbalance = liquidity void** — He treats these as "pretty much the same thing" and prefers "liquidity void". [01:40]
- **Liquidity void definition** — "a price range where there are no contradicting orders". A big move up means no sell orders in that range. A big drop with no hesitation means no resting buy orders. If there were resting orders, price would pull back a bit along the way. It usually shows as a large candle. [02:13], [02:47], [05:31]
- **Why price returns** — In a trend (higher highs and higher lows), retracements and higher lows draw into areas where liquidity was missing before. There, market makers can execute because they know price will move in their direction. [03:19]
- **Role of FVGs in his model** — "Fair value gaps are used for retracements." He does not use them for entries necessarily. Liquidity sweeps tell him where the market is going, and FVGs show where price may draw to on a retracement or give a "secondary entry". [03:54]
- **Opposite of liquidity** — Liquidity is where orders rest. An FVG is "quite literally the opposite". [02:13]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- In an uptrend, when price retraces into a higher-timeframe FVG/imbalance and then prints a break of structure (market structure break) on a lower timeframe, enter in the trend direction. (The mirror image applies to downtrends, inferred.) [04:28], [04:59]
- Continuation process: in a retracement, find the imbalance, scale down to a lower timeframe, find a break of structure in the continuation direction, and enter. [06:38]
- Reversal building block: liquidity taken out plus a break of structure means execute. [06:38]
- Full sequence: liquidity sweep, then BOS, then the trend starts to form, then an FVG forms, the FVG gets filled, and a smaller-timeframe BOS gives the entry. [06:04]
- He prefers order-flow-based confluence over support and resistance ("hit a ceiling"), which he does not use. [01:08], [01:40]
- How to spot FVGs on charts is deferred to the next trading video. [03:19], [08:16]

## Process / order of operations
1. Liquidity sweep, then BOS, which establishes direction.
2. Identify the FVG/imbalance left by the impulsive move.
3. Wait for price to retrace into the FVG.
4. Drop to a lower timeframe and wait for a BOS in the trend direction.
5. Enter.

## Confluences mentioned
- FVG / liquidity void — for retracement targets and secondary entries. [03:54]
- Lower-timeframe BOS after a higher-timeframe FVG fill — the confirmation needed to enter. [04:28]
- Liquidity sweep — the primary directional signal. [03:54]
- Support/resistance — "don't use" (not order-based). [01:40]
- He says the concepts apply to any timeframe, any pair and any market (scalps or swings). [06:38], [07:10]
