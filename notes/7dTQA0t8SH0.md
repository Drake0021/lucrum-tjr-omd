## Summary
Lesson defining SMT divergence between the S&P 500 (ES) and Nasdaq (NQ). A divergence is when one index makes a lower high (or higher low) while the other makes a higher high (or lower low) at the same time; it signals a trend change, potentially before a break of structure or inverse FVG appears. It is only meaningful when it forms while sweeping a significant (HTF) draw on liquidity. He always trades the "leading" index — the one that already shows the new direction (the lower high in a bearish SMT, the higher low in a bullish SMT). Examples on 4H (bias) and 5-min (execution confluence).

## Concepts & definitions
- **SMT divergence** — comparing highs and lows of two correlated instruments (ES and NQ) at the same time; a divergence between them helps identify that the trend will change "before even being able to see a break of structure or an inverse fair value gap". He does not know what SMT stands for and doesn't care. [00:00] [00:34] [01:07]
- **Bearish SMT** — one index makes a high then a lower high while the other makes a high then a higher high (at the same points in time). Bearish confluence for both indexes. Either index can be the one making the lower high. [05:00] [05:34] [06:08]
- **Bullish SMT** — one index makes a low then a lower low while the other makes a low then a higher low. Bullish for both. [06:43] [07:18]
- **Leading index** — the one already showing the new trend (lower high in bearish SMT / higher low in bullish SMT); it "was able to fill probably more orders and is able to move a little bit quicker". **Lagging index** — the one still continuing the old trend (the higher high / lower low). [05:34] [07:18]
- **Context requirement** — SMT is useful specifically while actively sweeping draws on liquidity (where orders can be filled); outside of that "these things will show up all the time and will be pretty much like useless". [04:26] [05:00]
- Works on all time frames; on HTF used mainly for bias, on LTF usable for execution/confluence. [10:41] [13:25]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Only count an SMT divergence if it forms while sweeping a significant (HTF) draw on liquidity. [05:00] [15:03]
- Take the trade on the leading index: bearish SMT → short the index that made the lower high (not the one that swept/made a higher high); bullish SMT → buy the index that made the higher low. [10:41] [13:25]
- Rationale: leading index gives bigger/faster expansion (example: ES cleared highs NQ never reached). [14:31]
- HTF SMT (e.g. 4H) → bias; LTF SMT (5-min) → execution confluence. [11:13]

## Process / order of operations
1. Open ES and NQ side by side (split screen optional). [08:25] [09:00]
2. Identify price sweeping a HTF draw on liquidity. [13:57]
3. Compare the swing points formed at the same time on both indexes; look for higher high vs lower high (or lower low vs higher low). [13:57]
4. Bias in reversal direction; execute on the leading index. [13:57]
