## Summary
TJR ranks the five confluences he says are "the only confluences that I use on a day-to-day basis" (Oct 2026). In order: (1) liquidity, (2) inverse fair value gap, (3) fair value gap, (4) equilibrium (Gann box 50%), (5) break of structure. He classes each as a reversal or continuation confluence. Liquidity sweeps and IFVGs/BOS signal reversals; FVGs and EQ signal trend continuation. He says he combines them "in a sequence" at the market open to form his strategy. Order blocks are not on the list. Examples are on S&P 500 and Nasdaq across daily, hourly and lower timeframes.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity** — "resting orders on a chart". They lie above highs and below lows. High = a move up then a move down; low = a move down then a move up [00:33–01:08].
- **Liquidity sweep** — the resting orders are "swept or filled" and price moves in the opposite direction. It is a **reversal** confluence [00:33; 03:53]. The market "draws towards liquidity" and sometimes sweeps it in order to make a move [01:41].
- **Liquidity's two uses** — it gives "the opportunity to enter and then also the opportunity to exit" (entry after a sweep, target at opposing liquidity) [00:33].
- **Fair value gap (FVG)** — "a gap on the chart that shows an imbalance", i.e. "a lack of orders within that area". It is identified as the gap between candle wicks (he points to "this candlestick wick to this candlestick wick"; the three-candle wick gap, inferred) [04:26–04:58]. It is a **continuation** confluence: FVGs form in the trend's retracements, and "as long as these fair value gaps stay getting respected, the trend stays intact" [03:19–04:26].
- **Inverse fair value gap (IFVG)** — a with-trend FVG that price **closes through** (bullish FVG closed underneath, or bearish FVG closed above), "disrespecting" it. Because FVGs should cause continuation, a close through one tells him "price wants to change the trend and reverse". It is a **reversal** confluence: "we can safely assume that the trend is broken" [07:45–09:26]. The trigger is a candle **close** beyond the far side of the FVG [08:19; 09:26].
- **Equilibrium (EQ)** — the 50% of the most recent swing, drawn with TradingView's Gann box with all levels off except 50%. It is a **continuation** confluence [11:06–11:39]. Uptrend: draw from the most recent swing low to the most recent swing high after a higher high; at or below 50% = discount (buy zone) [12:13]. Downtrend: draw from the most recent high to the most recent low; above 50% = premium (sell zone) [12:45–13:20]. He also mentions "Fibonacci retracement levels" as an alternative but focuses on EQ [11:06].
- **Break of structure (BOS)** — a candle close beyond the most recent swing against the trend. Uptrend broken = "a candlestick closure underneath the most recent low"; downtrend broken = closing above the most recent lower high. It signals a change in trend, "very similar to an inverse fair value gap" [15:01–16:41].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Do not decide sweep direction from liquidity alone: "how do I know when the liquidity is getting swept ... that's what we use our other confluences for" [03:19].
- IF price is trending AND a with-trend FVG is closed through, THEN treat the trend as broken and expect a new trend in the opposite direction [08:52; 09:26].
- IF in an uptrend after a higher high, THEN look for longs on the retracement into a bullish FVG or into discount (≤50% EQ). Mirror for downtrends: bearish FVG or premium [05:32; 12:13; 14:27].
- Use FVG/EQ for continuation entries when you missed the initial post-sweep entry: "if you see that price has already made a liquidity sweep and is already trending ... and you missed the entry ... use a fair value gap to spot another entry on the continuation" [07:12].
- After a BOS, the lower high (or higher low) can be located with continuation confluences such as EQ [17:48].
- Targets: liquidity, e.g. the lows below a short taken from EQ [15:01].
- Timing: "I get on the markets at market open and I use all of these confluences together in a sequence" [17:48]. No time window is stated.

## Process / order of operations
He implies a sequence without spelling it out (inferred from the rankings and examples):
1. Mark liquidity (highs/lows) [00:33].
2. Watch for a sweep, then a reversal signal: IFVG or BOS [03:19; 07:45; 15:01].
3. Enter on a retracement into an FVG or EQ in the new direction (continuation) [07:12; 14:27].
4. Target opposing liquidity [15:01].
