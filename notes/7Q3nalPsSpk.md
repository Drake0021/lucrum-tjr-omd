## Summary
Recap of an ES long that lost (stopped by 2.7 points, correct bias, entry timing off) and a GBPUSD long that won (TP1 hit, covered the ES loss) but was a bad trade, entered on sloppy price action inside a long consolidation. Lesson: judge trades by process, not outcome. He was more satisfied with the correct ES bias than with the lucky GU win. Getting bias right daily is what makes a profitable trader, and execution and stops will hit and miss. He shows what the correct GU trade would have been (wait for the breakout from consolidation, a retest of FVG/accumulation, and a 5m reaction candle). He says he risks less on S&P trades than on forex trades. He plans to avoid trading CPI, PPI, and Friday's heavy news.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Breakout of downtrend → areas of accumulation** — the "new strategy" (he calls it "clickbait strategy"): price breaks a downtrend, then look for areas of accumulation that could cause legs up, with liquidity resting beneath them. [00:35]–[01:09]
- **Third confluence via a second BOS** — if the FVG reaction is weak (filled too deep, can't close back above), wait for a new high plus a second 1m BOS. [01:09]–[01:44]
- **Breakout with "very obvious volume"** — the proper trigger after a consolidation is a decisive breakout of the range, followed by a retest. [07:19]–[07:53]
- **Bias vs execution** — bias is the most important skill. Execution and stops are the hardest part and will hit and miss. [10:45]–[11:18]

## Process / order of operations
1. Daily/HTF: identify a downtrend breakout (bullish shift).
2. Mark the areas of accumulation that could cause legs up, plus liquidity beneath.
3. Wait for a liquidity sweep → BOS up.
4. Wait for a reaction off the FVG/accumulation. If it is weak, wait for a new high and a second BOS.
5. Enter. Stop under the sweep/accumulation low.
6. In consolidation, do not enter inside the chop. Wait for a breakout with volume and a retest.
