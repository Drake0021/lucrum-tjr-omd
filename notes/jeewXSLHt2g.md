## Summary
A beginner lesson defining break of structure (BOS) from first principles. It covers trend (HH/HL, LH/LL, consolidation), swing high and low (two-candle definition), and BOS (a candle body close beyond the most recent opposing swing). He walks through ES examples on the 4H and on a lower TF, including a high-impact news candle. The main points: wicks are not BOS; in a downtrend only the most recent high matters, so higher lows don't change the trend; and BOS alone is never a trade signal. He promises a consolidation-breakout example but never shows one.

## Concepts & definitions
- **BOS** — "a trend change". BOS = change in order flow = change in trend = change in structure; "all of those are literally the same exact thing" [00:00–00:34].
- **Trend** — uptrend = higher highs and higher lows; downtrend = lower highs and lower lows; consolidation = sideways with no identifiable trend. A breakout from consolidation is "a form of a break of structure, but a little bit different" (not elaborated) [01:08–01:40].
- **High** — "a move up and then a move down": a green (up) candle followed by a red (down) candle. The price is the highest point (wick or body) of the two candles. Two up candles do not make a high [02:47–03:22; 06:47–07:20].
- **Low** — a down candle followed by an up candle. The price is the lowest point of the two candles [02:47].
- **Timeframe-specific swings** — a point that looks like a high but is "a move down then a move down" is not a high on the current TF (4H), though it may be one on lower TFs [06:47–07:20].
- **Bearish BOS (in an uptrend)** — a candle closes beneath the **most recent low** within the uptrend [04:30; 09:35].
- **Bullish BOS (in a downtrend)** — a candle closes above the **most recent high** within the downtrend [04:30; 09:35].
- **Body close required** — "We need a full candlestick body close above a high or underneath a low ... in order for price to break structure." A wick through is not a BOS, even three wicks in a row [11:14; 15:39–16:11].
- **Higher low inside a downtrend** — still a downtrend, "because the most recent high has not been closed above yet". In a downtrend monitor only the most recent high; in an uptrend only the most recent low [08:27–09:35].
- **Quick trend read** — look left. If the most recent high has been closed above and the most recent low has not been closed below, it is an uptrend, and vice versa [13:57].
- **Updating the monitored swing** — in an uptrend, each new low (down-then-up) becomes the new level to monitor [15:04–16:45].

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- BOS on its own is never an entry: "if you just try and use it on your own by pressing buy when we break structure ... you are going to lose all of your money." It must be combined with other confluences [11:46; 12:21; 14:31].
- Don't flip bias on wicks; only body closes change the trend [16:11].
- Treat a high-impact news candle with huge wicks by the same close-based logic [12:53–13:25].

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify the current trend by looking left at the most recent high and low and whether either was closed beyond [13:57].
2. In an uptrend, track the most recent low; in a downtrend, the most recent high [09:00].
3. A candle body close beyond it is the BOS, after which the trend flips and you track the opposite swing [10:09–10:41].

## Confluences mentioned

- Combined with: liquidity sweep, FVG, **breaker blocks**, EQ [17:18].
