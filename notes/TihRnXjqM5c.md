## Summary
TJR explains the BPR (Balanced Price Range, ICT term; TJR calls it a "high quality imbalanced price range"): the overlap zone between a bullish FVG that gets disrespected and an opposing bearish FVG (or vice versa). He says he found it himself before learning it was an ICT concept and explains it "how I understand it". It is an extra confluence used mainly at entry, on the 15m–5m execution timeframe, after the usual bias → liquidity sweep → break of structure sequence; it is explicitly not required. Examples are shown on 4H and 5m charts (instrument not stated; likely ES/NQ, inferred).

## Concepts & definitions
- **BPR (balanced price range / "imbalanced price range")** — Two fair value gaps in opposite directions that overlap. Sequence: violent move up forms a bullish imbalance (bullish FVG); later a violent move down forms a bearish imbalance that overlaps the bullish one. The overlap zone is the BPR. Described as "combining inverse fair value gap along with fair value gaps." [03:00][03:32][04:42]
- **Which part is the BPR** — Only the overlapping portion of the two FVGs; he "shrinks" the box to the overlap, sometimes just a sliver or line. [06:25][07:01][09:30]
- **Condition: bullish FVG gets disrespected** — The bullish imbalance must be "formed and then disrespected" (invalidated, i.e., price trades through it — the IFVG element), then a bearish imbalance forms/overlaps. [03:32][09:30]
- **Why it works** — Price moved swiftly through the zone both up and down, so the whole range is still imbalanced and price "will more than likely want to come back and balance out." He treats it as a higher-attraction area than a single FVG. [04:42][10:06]
- **Size of FVGs** — Look for big displacement candles ("the big size candlesticks"), not tiny FVGs found by zooming way in. [05:15][05:51]
- **Nested FVGs** — If one FVG fully encapsulates the opposite one, just use the smaller fully-contained one (overlap = the contained gap). [14:36]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Use BPRs with bias already established; they are an entry refinement on the 15m–5m timeframe after liquidity sweep → break of structure, when scaling down to execution TF. [15:53]
- If there are two candidate FVGs for entry and one of them overlaps an opposite-direction FVG, prefer the BPR one — it's "the most likely area that price is going to be looking to fill." [16:27]
- In bearish context: price retraces up into the BPR, then expect continuation lower (and vice versa for bullish). [04:42]
- Not mandatory: "Is this something that's like make or break we need to use this in our trade? No." [15:53]

## Process / order of operations
1. Have bias (HTF).
2. Wait for liquidity sweep.
3. Wait for break of structure.
4. Scale down to execution TF (15m–5m); identify FVGs; where a bullish and bearish FVG overlap, shrink box to overlap → entry zone. [15:53]
