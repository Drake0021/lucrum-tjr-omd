## Summary
TJR explains FVG invalidation and his version of the inverse FVG (IFVG). Two uses: (1) an FVG in the direction of your bias that gets a candle close through it is invalidated — stop using it/scrap that trade idea; (2) an FVG opposite your bias (e.g., bearish FVG when bullish) that gets closed through is a bullish confluence — can be an entry trigger (enter on the close above) with stop below the old FVG. He says he's "not a big fan" of waiting for the classic IFVG retest and calls the IFVG "a pretty made-up concept", preferring the invalidation close itself. It only works with the other confluences (sweep, BOS, bias); an opposite FVG invalidated inside an obvious opposite setup is not a reversal signal. Filmed in Hawaii; examples on ES/NQ, hourly/15m/5m/1m.

## Concepts & definitions
- **FVG invalidation** — An FVG is invalidated "by a closure underneath it or a closure above it" (candle close through the gap, not a wick). [01:08][04:42]
- **Use 1: invalidates a trade idea** — If an FVG in your bias direction gets closed through → "scrap that fair value gap, it's not working for us"; no trade off it. [04:42][05:15][22:25]
- **Use 2: confluence for entry** — When bias is bullish and a bearish FVG gets a close above it → bullish confluence/entry reason; stops under the old bearish FVG. Mirror for bearish. [05:15][06:21][22:25]
- **IFVG (inverse fair value gap)** — The invalidated FVG used as support/resistance on a retest. TJR: "in my eyes an inverse fair value gap is a pretty made up concept"; "not a big fan of ... waiting for it to come back down in and get it tapped into"; acknowledges retest gives better entries. [00:34][05:15][06:21][19:00][19:33]
- **Two in one** — BOS candle that simultaneously invalidates a bearish FVG and creates a bullish FVG. [10:52]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Requirement: must already have bias, liquidity sweep and (ideally) BOS; "it can't just be ... bearish FVG gets [invalidated] → bullish." [08:03][08:36][09:10]
- Entry option: after BOS (bullish bias), enter long on the candle that closes above a bearish FVG; stop under the old bearish FVG. [06:21][08:03]
- Alternate stop: under equilibrium (invalidation point) if entering off equilibrium retest. [20:46][21:18]
- Don't treat an opposite FVG invalidation as a reversal when price is in an obvious opposite setup (sweep + BOS + OB/breaker + still in premium/discount via equilibrium). [08:36][09:10][09:43]
- Can scale down to 1m for BOS entry after IFVG/equilibrium retest; TP at nearby highs (~1:1 in example). [13:52][14:30]

## Process / order of operations
1. Bias (e.g., hourly lows swept, hourly BOS). [05:49]
2. Identify opposite-direction FVGs.
3. Close through it = confluence; enter on the close or on retest of the IFVG/equilibrium with LTF BOS. [06:21][19:00]
4. Stop under old FVG or equilibrium; target liquidity highs. [21:18]

## Confluences mentioned
- FVG invalidation / IFVG — "supporting confluence and also sometimes occasionally as an entry confluence"; used occasionally. [21:18]
- Equilibrium, breaker block, order block — combined with IFVG. [13:18][18:25][20:46]
