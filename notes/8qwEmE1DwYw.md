## Summary
Day 3 of the 2026 Path to Profitability series: a beginner lesson on TradingView setup, Japanese candlestick anatomy (OHLC), how timeframes nest, and TJR's mechanical definitions of highs, lows, uptrends and downtrends. Key operational details for a bot: charts must be on New York (Eastern) time; a swing high is defined with a simple two-candle rule (up candle followed by down candle, take the higher wick of the pair) and a swing low is a down candle followed by an up candle (take the lower wick). Trends are series of HH/HL or LH/LL; he warns that trend alone is not a reason to enter. He previews the series' upcoming topics: liquidity, FVGs, advanced liquidity, IFVGs, advanced imbalance concepts, SMT divergences and time theory.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Technical vs fundamental trader** — TJR is a technical trader: trades "purely based off of what the chart is telling me"; fundamental traders (e.g., Warren Buffett) trade off news/earnings. [01:04]–[02:14]
- **Charting platform** — TradingView for live charting; free plan is enough. He uses premium only to have more indicators; "I personally don't use any indicators to actually take trades... but I use indicators to help me mark out my confluences." [02:46]–[03:53]
- **Chart colors** — avoids green/red candles because they psychologically associate with making/losing money; he uses blue (up) and black (down), white background, no grid lines. [06:48]–[08:28]
- **Time zone = New York** — all charts set to Eastern time regardless of where you live; "market opens at 9:30 a.m. Eastern time". Session opens/closes in the series are in ET. [08:28]–[10:08]
- **Candle types** — uses Japanese candlesticks (not Heikin-Ashi, Renko, line, etc.) because they give four data points per period. [11:46]–[12:20]
- **Candle anatomy (OHLC)** — body = open-to-close; wicks ("snail trails") show the extremes reached. Up candle: bottom of body = open, top of body = close. Down candle: top of body = open, bottom of body = close. Top of upper wick = high, bottom of lower wick = low. [14:42]–[19:12]
- **Timeframes nest** — each candle = one period of the selected timeframe (1D = 24h, 4H, 1H, 15m, 5m, 1m); one 4H candle = four 1H candles; lower TFs show the same price movement in more detail. He will cover which timeframes to use later in the series. [13:34], [22:30]–[26:29]
- **High (swing high)** — "a move up followed by a move down". Operationally: an up candle followed by a down candle; the high is the highest wick of those two candles. [29:45]–[33:03]
- **Low (swing low)** — "a move down followed by a move up". Operationally: a down candle followed by an up candle; the low is the lowest wick of those two candles. [30:20], [32:30]
- **Three market states** — uptrend, downtrend, consolidation (sideways). [30:52]–[31:24]
- **Uptrend** — series of higher highs and higher lows. Example: S&P 500 on high timeframes over months/years is "forever and always" in an uptrend (COVID drop aside). [33:39]–[35:52]
- **Downtrend** — series of lower highs and lower lows. [35:52]–[38:02]
- **Order flow / break of structure** — mentioned as upcoming topics (how new trends form); not taught here. [28:07], [36:56]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- All analysis and session times are in New York / Eastern time; market open = 9:30 a.m. ET. [09:02]–[10:08]
- Swing-point marking rule: up candle then down candle -> mark the higher of the two highs as a high; down candle then up candle -> mark the lower of the two lows as a low. [32:30]–[33:39]
- Do not enter purely because of trend direction: "trends can break. Trends can change." Being in a downtrend is not on its own a reason to sell. [38:02]
- Indicators are only aids for marking confluences, never entry signals. [03:53]
