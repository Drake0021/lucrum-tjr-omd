## Summary
Three trades: an ES long stopped out (an impatient entry on a bare FVG touch with a 5-point stop), an ES re-entry in the same bias that hit TP1 and TP2 and was then closed on a 1m BOS down, and a GBPUSD long (TP1 hit, aiming for 1:5.8). Lessons: (1) if the trade is stopped but the bias is not invalidated (the sweep low holds), re-enter on new confirmation; (2) wait for the "third confirmation" (a reaction off a 5m FVG, equilibrium, or OB); (3) budget daily risk dynamically ("leveraging risk"). He started at 50% of the daily budget. After a loss and a BE runner, he had 25% committed and could deploy the remaining 75% on a third high-confidence trade.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Change of trend → area of accumulation** — when price breaks out of a downtrend (change of trend), find the area of accumulation that caused the change. That is the leg down that filled the orders for the leg up. Wait for price to revisit it. [01:43], [06:14]
- **Third confirmation** — after (1) price taps the accumulation area and (2) BOS up, wait for (3) a reaction off a 5m FVG, 5m equilibrium, or 5m OB. [06:46]
- **Fractal re-application** — after a 5m BOS, find the accumulation area that caused that 5m BOS, wait for price to draw back into it, and then look for a 1m BOS. [07:21]
- **Bias invalidation** — the bullish bias stays valid as long as the sweep low is not taken out. Getting stopped out does not invalidate the bias. [04:32]
- **Invalidation point for stops** — the stop should sit beyond the actual invalidation level (the sweep low), not at an arbitrary fixed distance. [05:06]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Do not enter immediately on an FVG fill. Wait for a reaction. [02:50]
- Do not use an arbitrary tight stop (5 points). Put the stop under the invalidation point (sweep low). [02:50], [05:06]
- Re-entry rule: if stopped out but the bias is intact (the sweep low has not been taken), you still have daily risk available, and new confluence forms (BOS up + FVG fill + reaction), then re-enter with the stop under the invalidation point. [03:25]–[05:06]
- Management (ES re-entry): at TP1, manage 50% of the position. At TP2, manage another 50% of the open position. Close the rest on a 1m BOS against the position (down) off high-timeframe liquidity. [05:06]–[05:40]
- GU management: TP1 → 50% off, stop to BE. TP2, TP3, and TP4 at an HTF area of accumulation. [07:53]
- Dynamic daily risk budgeting: when other setups may form, open the first trade at about 50% of the daily risk. After a loss (−50%) and a winner hitting TP1 (making it about −25% net with a BE stop), you may risk the remaining 75% on another high-confluence setup. Worst case equals 100% of the daily budget. [03:25]–[03:59], [08:59]–[10:06]
- He waited deep into the New York session for the GU setup, which he had planned in pre-market. [13:25]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify a downtrend that breaks (change of trend) on the HTF/LTF.
2. Mark the accumulation (leg down that caused the leg up). Wait for the tap.
3. Get a BOS up (5m).
4. Third confirmation: a reaction off a 5m FVG, EQ, or OB. If none comes and lows are swept, look for a new 5m BOS, find the accumulation that caused it, wait for the revisit, and enter on a 1m BOS / 5m reaction candle.
5. Stop under the invalidation point.
6. Partials at each TP, BE after TP1. Close the rest on an opposing 1m BOS when appropriate.
7. Re-allocate the remaining daily risk based on realized and locked results.
