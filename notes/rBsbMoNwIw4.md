## Summary
This is part 2 of the order-block lesson in the 2024 "Trading Transformation" series. It covers identification only; how to trade OBs is explicitly left for later. TJR defines the OB strictly: it is only the leg that causes a liquidity sweep, and a break of structure in the other direction is what proves the sweep happened. He then walks through weekly, monthly and lower-timeframe examples (instrument not named; likely ES/SPX, inferred). Each example shows price revisiting the leg and continuing in the BOS direction.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Order block (recap)** — "a price range where large amount of orders are able to get filled that change price direction or cause market to move." [00:00]
- **Why OBs matter** — If price revisits a bearish OB and selling power comes out of it, price is likely to continue down. Bullish is the mirror. [00:34–01:07]
- **Where OBs form** — Large orders fill where liquidity lies, i.e. above highs and below lows. You know those orders were filled when price pushes above highs and then breaks structure the other way. He frames the OB as combining two tools: liquidity sweep + BOS. [01:39–02:17]
- **Bearish OB** — "the leg up that sweeps liquidity that causes the break of structure to the downside." [02:51]
- **Bullish OB** — "it's the leg down that causes the liquidity sweep ... just the move down ... it's not any of the move up." [03:58]
- **Sweep validation** — "how do we know it's a sweep? when we see the break of structure." The BOS must come right after the sweep. [03:27; 04:34]
- **OB extent** — The whole leg, from the leg's origin swing to the swept extreme ("from this low all the way up to this high, it's the whole move up"). [05:11; 08:01]
- **Fractal** — OBs, like every tool in the series, appear on every timeframe. They are harder to spot on lower timeframes because there are so many highs and lows. [06:57; 11:00]
- **Rejected example** — He discards an example "because the sweep is on an up candle." This implies a clean bullish OB's sweep should be made by the down-leg itself (inferred; vague). [09:45]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- To identify an OB: (1) find a sweep of a high or low; (2) confirm it with a BOS (candle close beyond the opposite swing) right after; (3) box only the leg into the swept extreme. Do not include the subsequent move. [02:51–04:34]
- OB use condition: price revisits the leg **and** shows selling/buying power out of it. A touch alone is not the signal. No entry rules are given in this video. [01:07; 05:49]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify the trend and swing (move up then move down = high) [06:23].
2. Spot the sweep of the prior high or low.
3. Confirm with a close-beyond-swing BOS in the opposite direction.
4. Box the sweeping leg = OB.
5. Wait for a revisit and a reaction out of it [13:19].

## Confluences mentioned
- Liquidity sweep + BOS — required to define an OB. [01:39]
