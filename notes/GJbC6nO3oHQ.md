## Summary
Explains **where** liquidity lies: above highs and below lows. Above highs sit breakout buyers and the buy-stops of shorts; below lows sit breakout sellers and the sell-stops of longs. Institutions/market makers use those pools to fill opposite-direction orders, which causes reversals and BOS. That is probabilistic: not every high or low is used. Examples: a 5m/1H sweep of lows inside an uptrend, a weekly sweep of a high in the 2021–22 downtrend, and the 2020 COVID monthly sweep of lows (index, likely S&P/NASDAQ). Liquidity sweeps and their confirmation are explicitly deferred.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity (recap)** — "resting orders or a price range where orders can get filled." It moves the market on every timeframe (1m to monthly). [01:41–02:16]
- **Buy-side liquidity above highs** — Breakout longs entering when a high is pushed above, plus short sellers' stop losses above highs, which force them to buy back. "Two sets of resting orders of people buying." [02:48–05:03]
- **Sell-side liquidity below lows** — Longs' stop losses below lows (forced to sell), plus breakout shorts entering when lows are pushed below. [05:03–06:49]
- **"Liquidity resides above highs and below lows."** [06:49]
- **Smart money uses it to reverse** — "There's a ton of buy orders resting above highs, so when this high gets pushed above ... they can fill their massive amounts of sell orders and cause a change of direction, cause those break of structures ... and start a downtrend." [08:28–08:59]
- **Probabilistic, not every level** — "does that mean they're going to short at every single high ... no but we know there's just some sort of probability." If every high were sold, price would be "a constantly expanding consolidation", which isn't how trends work. [06:49–07:56; 08:59–09:32]
- **Price is drawn to highs and lows** — "that's where the majority of orders are going to get filled ... it's drawn towards highs and it's drawn towards lows." [18:46]
- **Trader-error framing** — Retail sees a big bearish candle break a "support zone" and sells; the market is actually filling buys below lows in an HTF uptrend. [11:11–11:44]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Don't go long into a high that may be swept just because it broke ("do we want to go long when there's like a potential sweep? no"). Mirror for shorts below lows. [17:39–18:12]
- Don't automatically fade every new high/low either; a sweep needs confirmation, which is not taught yet. [07:23; 18:12]
- Read the higher timeframe: a 5m sweep below lows was buying inside a 1H uptrend. [11:44]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify the trend.
2. Mark highs (buy-side) and lows (sell-side) on all timeframes as liquidity pools.
3. Expect price to be drawn to them; a reversal is possible after they're taken (confirmation deferred). [09:32–11:44; 18:46]

## Confluences mentioned
- Liquidity (highs/lows) as draw and as possible reversal location. HTF trend context (1H uptrend) for interpreting LTF sweeps. [11:44]
- "Support zone" is mentioned only as retail's mistaken view. [11:11]

## Trade examples
- Unnamed instrument, 5m/1H: in an uptrend a big bearish candle thrusts below lows (stops longs, lures breakout shorts), then the market goes long; the 1H was in an uptrend. [09:32–11:44]
- Weekly index chart (likely S&P/NASDAQ, inferred): in the 2021–22 downtrend, after a weekly BOS, a high forms; a rally pushes above it (breakout longs plus short stops), then the market fills sell orders and drops. [11:44–13:33]
- Monthly index chart, Feb–Mar 2020: the COVID crash swept monthly lows, filling buy orders and stopping out longs, before the rally to all-time highs. [14:50–16:33]
