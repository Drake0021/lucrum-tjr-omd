## Summary
TJR shows how to identify breaker blocks on charts, including an hourly example and lower-timeframe examples around the market open (instrument unnamed; likely ES, inferred). Operationally he describes a breaker as "the opposite of an order block": the leg **in the direction of the new BOS** that formed just before the BOS. When the breaker overlaps the sweep leg, either may be marked; he notes the breaker sometimes "offered a better entry." He shows breakers combining with FVGs and OBs. If one confluence is invalidated (an FVG closed through), the breaker can still hold. He admits the examples are cherry-picked and that a breaker alone is never an entry. He says he "really has not traded much at all over the past like two weeks."

## Concepts & definitions
- **Breaker (recap)** — In an uptrend, retracements are where sell orders filled. Price then breaks structure through them, so the range lacks sell orders, and buying power on a revisit means continuation. Note: the drawn direction here is garbled in the captions. The logic matches Day 21: a broken retracement range, traded in the new BOS direction. [01:07–01:42]
- **Identification shortcut** — "it's literally the opposite of an order block." A **bullish breaker is the move up prior to the break of structure to the upside**. A **bearish breaker is the move down prior to the break of structure to the downside**. The captions at 02:48 mix up up and down. The chart examples consistently use "move up prior to BOS up" for bullish and "move down prior to BOS down" for bearish. [02:48–03:24; 04:00; 05:09; 07:28; 09:13; 09:57]
- **Which leg** — In the hourly example he marks the up candle/leg before the bullish BOS as the breaker. "If you wanted to, you could also consider this a breaker." Some people mark the legs before the liquidity sweep. He himself typically looks for "the leg down that causes the liquidity sweep" (his OB), "but in reality ... this offered a better entry." Which leg to box is ambiguous (vague). [04:00–05:09]
- **Breaker + FVG** — "Paired with a fair value gap is a pretty awesome confluence." [01:42]
- **Confluence stacking** — In one example a breaker overlaps an FVG, and a black down candle is also an OB: "now you're starting to see how all of our confluences can start to come together." [07:28–08:04]
- **Invalidated confluence ≠ dead setup** — If price closes past an FVG ("it got invalidated") but a breaker remains below it, price can still draw into the breaker and react. [09:57–10:30]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- A breaker is not drawn "1 million trillion percent" — price doesn't always revisit it. If it does revisit and reacts, assume continuation. [05:45]
- Never enter just because a breaker is hit ("breaker bet spam ... no"). Several confluences are required. [08:40–09:13]
- FVG invalidation = a candle **closes past** it (through it). Fall back to the next confluence, e.g. the breaker. [10:30]

## Process / order of operations
1. Find the BOS.
2. Box the last leg in the BOS direction that formed before the BOS (bullish: move up before the BOS up).
3. On a revisit, look for a tap and rejection (a reaction candle) in the BOS direction.
