## Summary
This is a conceptual intro to fair value gaps / imbalances: what they are and why they help. TJR defines an FVG as an imbalance of market orders, a price range lacking orders opposite to the move. He classifies FVGs and OBs as **continuation confluences**. These differ from liquidity, which is a magnet, and they don't have to be revisited for price to continue. No chart identification is shown; that comes in pt.2.

## Concepts & definitions
- **FVG = imbalance** — The terms are used interchangeably. [00:32]
- **Definition** — "an imbalance of market orders ... a price range that lacks orders in the opposite direction of market sentiment." A big rip up leaves a range lacking sell orders; a big rip down leaves a range lacking buy orders. [01:07–02:45]
- **Why it matters** — If price revisits the range and buying (or selling) power comes out of it **in the same direction as the original move**, assume more orders were filled and price continues. [04:01–04:35]
- **Continuation confluence vs. liquidity** — Liquidity is a draw/magnet; price seeks highs and lows to fill orders. FVGs and OBs are continuation confluences: "they don't necessarily have to get hit to keep market going." When they are hit and react, they are "extra confluence." [06:20–07:29]
- **OB as continuation confluence** — Price need not return to the OB. If price does return and selling/buying power comes out, it continues. [08:04–09:11]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Only use an FVG in the direction of the move that created it (same-sentiment reaction). [04:35; 05:10]
- An FVG or OB never gets traded just because it exists. A revisit **plus** a reaction (buying/selling power out) is the signal. [04:01; 07:29]
