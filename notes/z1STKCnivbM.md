## Summary
A beginner lesson on candlestick anatomy (open, high, low, close; wicks; timeframes) on ES/S&P charts at 15m and daily. He classifies candles only as "full" candles vs doji (indecision), and explains long wicks as rejection: a long upper wick closing bearish suggests bears are in control. He deliberately skips engulfing and other named patterns as over-valued and confusing. He points viewers to the Boot Camp and Trading Transformation.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Why candlesticks** — Japanese candlesticks give the most price information on every timeframe; he uses blue (up) and black (down) candles. [00:34] [01:06]
- **OHLC** — up candle: open at the bottom of the body, close at the top; down candle: open at the top, close at the bottom. Upper wick tip = high, lower wick tip = low (where price went during the candle but did not close). [02:19]–[04:02] [06:49]–[07:24]
- **Timeframe** — the time each candle has to form before it closes (15m = 15 minutes of price action; daily = a day). The next candle's open is typically about the prior close. [04:37] [05:09] [05:42]
- **Lower TFs compose the higher TF** — a daily candle can be seen as 4H/1H/5m/1m candles. [10:52] [11:25]
- **Doji** — a very small body with large wicks relative to it = indecision, a "50/50" battle between bulls and bears. "Don't put too much value" on them. [11:57]–[14:11]
- **Full candle** — a candle with a substantial body (the other of his two classes). [13:06]
- **Long wick (rejection)** — a long upper wick with a bearish close = price could not sustain the highs; buy orders were not strong enough; "bears are probably in charge" (depends on timeframe). A long lower wick with a bullish close = bulls overpowered. [14:47]–[16:25]
- He notes a long-wick rejection candle is often also a liquidity sweep. [16:25]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Do not rely on named candlestick patterns (engulfing etc.): "people give them way more value than they should be given". [13:06]–[13:39]
- Read a long wick against a close as a momentum clue, not as a standalone signal (inferred from "depending on what time frame you're on"). [15:51]
