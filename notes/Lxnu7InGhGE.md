## Summary
First of a planned three-part liquidity series; TJR calls liquidity "probably the largest topic and the most important topic of my strategy". This part is conceptual only (no charting): liquidity = resting orders (stop orders and buy/sell limit orders) clustered at price points. Banks/institutions ("market movers") need opposite-side orders to fill their huge positions, so they drive price into liquidity. TJR's logic: identify where liquidity rests, let price take it, then require a confirmation — a break of structure / market structure shift — before entering, which lets him catch tops and bottoms of moves. No sweep + no shift = hands off. He contrasts this with support/resistance, which he calls guessing.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Liquidity** — "resting orders": stop orders (taking people out of trades) and limit orders (buy limits / sell limits entering people). A price point where, when reached, many orders activate — a "pool of money" where lots of money cycles through the market. [02:13]-[02:48]
- **Market movers** — banks / institutions with massive orders that actually move the market. [03:21]-[03:56]
- **Why price seeks liquidity** — every buy needs a seller; to fill massive orders, banks push price to where many people exit or enter the opposite direction, i.e., they take out liquidity ("liquidity sweeps") so their orders fill and they can move the market where they want. [03:56]-[06:09]
- **B-booking** — offshore brokers taking the other side of client trades, assuming clients lose ("98% of traders fail"). Mentioned in passing. [04:28]-[05:02]
- **Confirmation** — after liquidity is taken, a break of structure / market structure shift is the confirmation that "liquidity has been swept and orders have been filled causing the market to change direction". (Full confirmation details promised for Part 3.) [06:43]-[07:16], [08:57]-[09:30]
- **Support & resistance** — rejected; "that's like guessing", "story time", because it gives no reason why price moves. [01:05]-[01:40], [07:16]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Do NOT enter just because price reaches a liquidity level — no immediate buy/sell; need more confirmation. [08:24]
- Sequence required: (1) price pushes into a resting-order (liquidity) price point, (2) liquidity is taken, (3) a shift in market structure (BOS / MSS) occurs → then trade in the new direction. [08:24], [08:57]-[09:30]
- If no market structure shift follows the sweep → "hands off, we're not doing anything". [08:24]
- Do not try to execute exactly when the banks execute; wait a little for confirmation — still gives "an incredibly good entry". [10:01]
- Goal of the model: enter at the top or bottom of a move (reversal from liquidity), not in the middle of an existing trend — "we catch the full move". [10:01]

## Process / order of operations
1. Identify where liquidity (resting orders) is — method promised in Part 2. [05:36]-[06:09]
2. Wait for price to reach and take that liquidity. [08:24]
3. Wait for confirmation: a break of structure / market structure shift. [07:16], [08:24]
4. Enter in the direction of the shift, catching the start of the move. [07:50], [10:01]-[10:35]
