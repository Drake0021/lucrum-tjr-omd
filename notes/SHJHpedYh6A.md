## Concepts & definitions
- **Day trading** — enter and exit within the same day. Both long ("buy") and short ("sell") positions are available. [12:48] [13:55]
- **Stop loss / take profit** — SL = where you exit when the idea is invalidated. TP = where you exit when the idea is complete. On a long the SL sits below entry and the TP above; on a short, the reverse. [44:01] [45:08] [52:26]
- **Partial close** — close part of the lot size (e.g., 0.05 of 0.1 lots) at a level and leave the rest running. [42:52] [43:26] [3:51:24]
- **Candlestick** — price movement within one timeframe period. Body = open→close. Wicks ("snail trails") = the extremes traded. Bullish = close > open. The next candle usually opens at the prior close. [1:01:28]–[1:05:58]
- **Timeframes** — each candle = one period. A daily candle splits into 6 × 4H candles, a 4H into 4 × 1H, and so on. Higher TFs have bigger candles. "Every single thing that I'm teaching you guys on here is applicable to every single time frame." [1:06:33]–[1:15:04] [1:39:57]
- **Sessions (ET clock)** — Asia opens 7:00 PM ET and closes 4:00 AM ET. London opens 3:00 AM and closes 12:00 PM ET. New York opens 8:00 AM and closes 5:00 PM ET. There is a gap between NY close (5 PM) and Asia open (7 PM). [1:19:00] [1:19:35] Later he says "Asian session starts at 1800 eastern time" (inconsistent with 7 PM). [3:42:48] NYSE opens 9:30 AM ET and NYSE pre-market opens 8:30 AM ET. [3:36:12]
- **Session suitability** — Asia: low volume and volatility; "never trade during Asian session", even for JPY pairs. London: forex and commodities move best; indexes "do not move well at all during London". NY: best for indexes (S&P 500, NASDAQ), and forex also moves. US indexes move only when US money enters, so trade them only in NY. [1:20:15] [1:20:49] [1:23:06] [1:23:40] [1:24:13]
- **Session high/low marking window** — for indexes, London session H/L = the high/low **before 8:30 ET** (the London candles from 3:00 to 8:30). For forex, London H/L = before 8:00 ET. A post-8:30 high that exceeds London's does not count. [3:55:28] [3:56:04]
- **Why session H/L matter** — when a new session starts, new money and algorithms come in and "manipulate the previous session" by taking out the prior session's traders, then move price the way they want. Forex example: at London open the Asian low is manipulated, then price legs up. [3:53:09] [3:53:44] [3:54:17]
- **High (swing high)** — a two-candle pattern: an up candle followed by a down candle. The high = the highest wick of the two. **Low** = a down candle followed by an up candle; the low = the lowest wick of the two. Two consecutive same-colour candles ("black then black") do **not** form a high or low. [1:28:42] [1:29:15] [1:32:39] [1:33:12]
- **Trend** — uptrend = higher highs + higher lows; downtrend = lower highs + lower lows. "The trend is your friend." [1:36:01] [1:37:42] [1:38:15]
- **Consolidation** — sideways, no trend: random alternating highs/lows. He draws it as a rectangle. Example: the daily S&P had been in consolidation "for the past couple months" (as of Mar 2025). [1:41:40] [1:42:15] [1:43:23]
- **Retrace vs extension** — the retrace is the counter-trend move (down-moves in an uptrend, up-moves in a downtrend). The extension is the with-trend leg. [2:14:55] [2:15:29]
- **Confluence** — "a reasoning to get into a trade or a reasoning for why price moves." [1:44:28]
- **Break of structure (BOS)** — "when the current trend gets disrespected."
  - Bearish BOS: in an uptrend, a candle **closes below the most recent low** ("emphasis on most recent"). Bullish BOS: in a downtrend, a candle closes above the most recent high. [1:45:39] [1:47:21] [1:48:29] [1:49:04]
  - A wick through is not a BOS. [1:52:57] [1:54:38]
  - A wick that takes out several older lows (a "lower low") is still not a BOS unless a candle closes below the *most recent* low. You remain in the uptrend. [1:52:23] [1:53:30] [1:54:05]
  - While waiting for a bullish BOS, keep monitoring the newest high: if a new lower high forms, that becomes the level. [1:51:17]
  - Process: (1) identify the current trend, (2) watch for that trend to be disrespected by a close. [1:49:37]
- **Liquidity** — "resting orders above highs and below lows." Buy orders rest above highs: breakout buyers, plus the stops of shorts who sold the retrace. Sell orders rest below lows. Market makers/algorithms need opposite orders to fill size, and liquidity "gives it the ability to change directions." [1:57:32] [1:58:04] [2:00:16] [2:01:23] [2:03:01]
- **Liquidity sweep** — "the market manipulates a high or a low within the current trend to fill their massive amounts of orders to change the direction of the trend." He calls it "by far the most important confluence" and "the goat of concepts."
  - Each push beyond a low gives the market the *opportunity* (not the obligation) to reverse. [1:56:58] [2:03:35] [2:04:10] [2:04:46]
  - A sweep is confirmed by a BOS in the opposite direction ("orders were filled, we just changed the trend"). [2:12:40]
  - Example: the 2020 COVID crash swept 3 HTF lows. [2:05:53] [2:06:28]
  - Example: "today" (≈3 Mar 2025) an HTF sweep of three lows. [2:08:11]
- **Fair value gap (FVG) / imbalance** — the same thing: "imbalanced price action that needs to be balanced out via a retrace." It is a 3-candle pattern.
  - **Bullish FVG** = from the top of candle 1's wick to the bottom of candle 3's wick; it lacks sell orders. **Bearish FVG** = from the bottom of candle 1's wick to the top of candle 3's wick; it lacks buy orders. [2:14:21] [2:17:10] [2:19:29] [2:22:21] [2:24:39]
  - If the wicks of C1 and C3 overlap, there is no FVG. [2:20:03]
  - Only the middle candle's colour matters; C1/C3 colour is irrelevant. **Size does not matter.** [2:20:37] [2:21:11] [2:21:46]
  - FVGs are a *continuation* confluence. "Nothing without context." [2:25:12] [2:28:35]
- **FVG respected vs disrespected** — Respected: price fills it **without a candle closing beyond it**, then a candle closes in the trade direction. The reaction need not be the very next candle: one, two or three candles may close inside first. A wick beyond the FVG is fine ("if it's a wick we don't care"). Disrespected: a candle **closes** through it, and it is no longer valid. [2:30:45] [2:31:18] [2:32:26] [2:32:59] [2:34:06]
- **Inverse FVG (IFVG)** — "a fair value gap that gets disrespected within the current trend that in turn shows a new change of trend." It is a *confirmation* confluence, treated "similar to a break of structure." Its advantage: it often prints **before** the swing high/low is closed through, so it confirms the reversal earlier and gives a better entry than waiting for the BOS. It happens on every TF. Not every IFVG reverses, so it needs context. [2:35:16] [2:35:50] [2:36:58] [2:38:08] [2:40:21] [2:40:56]
- **Order block (OB)** — "the leg up or leg down prior to the liquidity sweep", i.e. the leg *that caused* the sweep before the BOS/IFVG.
  - Bearish OB = the up-move that swept the high before the bearish BOS. Bullish OB = the down-move that swept the low before the bullish BOS. [2:44:56] [2:45:30] [2:46:35] [2:47:10]
  - Rationale: the sweep leg is where large opposite orders were filled, so a return there can fill more. [2:46:03]
  - **Drawing:** the OB can be 1, 2, 3 or more **consecutive** same-direction candles, boxed from the bottom of the first candle to the top of the last (bearish OB case). A single candle whose wick swept the high also qualifies. A mixed sequence (up, down, up, up) is not one OB; only the final consecutive run that did the sweep counts. [2:47:43] [2:48:17]
  - Example: a bearish OB of 5 consecutive up candles. [2:49:57]
  - **Invalidation:** unlike an FVG, price **cannot even wick beyond the OB**. A close beyond it is also a BOS back. The exception is an SMT divergence, but then the trade is taken off the SMT, not the OB. [2:51:06] [2:51:42]
  - OB is a continuation confluence. [2:58:25]
- **Breaker block** — "the move up or down prior to the break of structure … in trend shift": the **retrace leg** just before the final leg that swept liquidity. The bearish breaker is the down-move (a retrace inside the uptrend) before the up-leg that swept the high. The bullish breaker is the up-move before the down-leg that swept the low.
  - Rationale: it is a *failed retrace*. That retrace should have fuelled continuation but did not fill enough orders, so the zone lacks orders, like an FVG.
  - It usually spans roughly the same price range as the OB. [2:52:49] [2:53:23] [2:53:59] [2:54:34] [2:55:07] [2:55:41]
  - A continuation confluence; examples where only the breaker is filled. [2:56:13] [2:57:19] [2:58:25]
- **Equilibrium (EQ)** — the 50% of the most recent swing range. Bullish: most recent low → most recent high, and below 50% = discount (buy). Bearish: most recent high → most recent low, and above 50% = premium (sell). He draws it with the **Gann box**.
  - He calls EQ the "**last resort**" continuation confluence, used when there is no FVG, OB or breaker. [2:59:33] [3:00:41] [3:01:15] [3:01:48] [3:03:47] [3:04:23]
  - Common error: anchoring EQ to random or older swings. Re-anchor when a new low forms. [3:04:55] [3:05:27] [3:07:44]
- **SMT divergence** — **indexes only (S&P 500 vs NASDAQ)**; not for forex/gold. [3:10:00] [3:11:06]
  - Bearish SMT: one index makes a higher high while the other makes a lower high. Bullish SMT: one makes a lower low while the other makes a higher low. "It doesn't matter which one" makes which. [3:11:43] [3:15:10] [3:17:22]
  - It is a *confirmation* confluence and "typically happens at key levels such as draws on liquidity". The biggest required context is a key level, because SMTs occur constantly. [3:13:28] [3:14:01] [3:17:56]
  - **Which index to trade:** the one that made the *new extreme* (higher high in a bearish SMT) is called the "lagging" index, and he trades **that** one. The other index (the one that failed to make the new extreme) has "already" reversed and "tells the future". [3:14:34] [3:15:43] [3:16:16] [3:19:38]
- **Confluence taxonomy** [2:25:12] [2:25:48] [2:26:22] [2:26:55]:
  - **Reversal:** liquidity sweeps (the sign of reversal).
  - **Confirmation:** BOS, IFVG, SMT (confirm the trend break/shift).
  - **Continuation:** FVG, OB, breaker block, EQ (proof of the new trend).
  - Sequence: reversal → confirmation → continuation → execution.
- **Key levels** — "draws on liquidity and imbalances": highs and lows plus FVGs, marked on the **1H and 4H**, together with **session highs/lows**. In the step list he also names the previous-day high/low. [3:34:32] [3:37:49] [3:38:24] [3:42:15]
- **Risk-to-reward** — "for every one that I risk I'm getting X in return." He measures it with the long/short position tool. [4:05:02] [4:10:42]
- **Two trader types** — (a) high RR (1:8–1:10) with a low win rate (~30%), who must risk less; (b) TJR's type: low RR (1:1.5, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4 "if it's a crazy trade") with a high win rate, who can risk a bit more. [4:11:51] [4:12:24] [4:12:59] [4:13:35]
- **Post-trade clarity** — a journal field: what you realise after the trade, used to spot recurring emotional mistakes. [4:18:41]–[4:23:13]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
**Instrument / session filters**

- Index traders trade NY. London-session traders trade forex/commodities (EURUSD, GBPUSD = starter pairs; GBPJPY, gold = faster and more volatile). [1:23:06] [1:26:27] [1:27:04]
- Never trade the Asian session. [1:20:49] [3:35:38]

**Time windows (ET)**
- **Indexes: take trades only from 9:50 to 10:30.** Be on the charts at least 30 min before the 9:30 open, ideally 1 hour before. [3:35:38] [3:36:12] [3:45:39]
- If no trade is found by 10:30, don't trade that day. [3:37:17]
- Forex in NY: 8:00–10:00 AM, and if no trade in that window, skip the day. Forex in London: 3:00–4:00 AM, with no trades after 4:00 AM. [3:36:44] [3:37:17] [3:37:49]
- A setup that completes before 9:50 is **not** taken. In the 4 Mar example a 1m BOS + IFVG printed before 9:50: "it's not 950 yet so I just have to sit on my hands and wait." [3:49:38] [3:50:11]

**Key-level marking (pre-open)**
- Mark key levels in this order [3:42:48]–[3:45:05] [3:55:28]–[3:58:53]:
  1. Asia and London session H/L.
  2. 4H highs/lows and FVGs.
  3. 1H highs/lows and FVGs.
- Discard any level that is already invalid [3:43:57] [3:44:32] [3:56:38] [3:57:12] [4:06:46]:
  - a session or 4H high/low already traded through (e.g. an Asian high already exceeded by London "with no reaction");
  - an FVG already filled and reacted to, or already disrespected;
  - a 1H low taken out before 9:30.
- Ignore levels too far from where price opens (e.g. a high requiring a 2.7% day). Mark them later "if price decides to push up there." [3:44:32] [3:57:12] [3:57:46] [3:58:53] [4:07:20]
- Do this on both ES and NQ. [3:58:20]

**Execution model ("final strategy")** [3:38:24]–[3:41:43]
- IF price hits a key level (1H/4H high/low, 1H/4H FVG, Asia/London session H/L, previous-day H/L) THEN drop to the **5m** and wait for a **confirmation confluence**: a 5m BOS, a 5m IFVG or a 5m SMT divergence.
- After 5m confirmation, do **not** enter yet. Wait for price to retrace into a **5m continuation confluence**: FVG, OB, breaker block or EQ.
- When price is in that 5m confluence, drop to the **1m**. The 1m will usually be trending into the zone (counter to the 5m). Wait for a 1m trend shift (**1m BOS or 1m IFVG**) and enter. When both form, the 1m IFVG "gives us a better entry". [3:40:03] [3:40:34] [4:08:59]
- **Stop:** above (shorts) or below (longs) where the idea is invalidated. If the trade is off a FVG, put it above the FVG. Alternatives he allows: just beyond the entry IFVG, or beyond the swing highs/lows. In the long example he says "there's no reason to put your stop loss so tight", so put it a little beyond the lows. [3:40:34] [3:41:09] [3:50:46] [4:01:10] [4:09:32]
- **Targets:** the opposing key levels (1H FVG, 4H low, London session low, etc.), because they have a high probability of reversing price. Use multiple partial TPs at successive key levels. If recent price has none, "look to the left" for HTF (4H) lows. [3:41:09] [3:41:43] [3:51:24] [3:51:58]
- Not fully systematic: the confluences differ day to day (one day EQ, another FVG; some days a plain sweep + 5m BOS + OB + 1m IFVG). You must be fluent in all of them. [4:02:51] [4:03:23]
- Don't chase when price runs toward TP1 without a 5m retrace. Wait for the retrace (FOMO warning). [4:08:27]

**Validity rules for zones**
- FVG: valid until a candle *closes* through it. Wicks through are OK. [2:31:18] [2:34:39]
- OB: invalid if price even *wicks* beyond it, unless the trade is an SMT setup. [2:51:06] [3:49:38]
- All confluences mean "nothing without context". Never trade any one in isolation (FVG, OB, breaker, EQ, SMT, BOS, IFVG). [2:28:35] [2:34:06] [2:40:56] [2:50:32] [2:57:52] [3:08:20] [3:17:22]

**Risk**
- The stop is a **fixed $ amount per trade**; the TP is what varies. [4:09:32] [4:10:05]
- Only take trades with **RR ≥ 1:1**. Skip the trade if invalidation is too far for 1:1. [4:11:17] [4:11:51]
- Risk 1–3% of the account per trade for high-win-rate/low-RR traders (TJR). Risk 0.25–1% for high-RR/low-win-rate traders. Never 10% ("10 shots before your account is gone"). [4:13:35] [4:14:42] [4:15:17]
- Trades per day: "you guys should only be taking like one or two trades in a day … if we're risking 1 to 3% of our account per day." Typically only one setup occurs between 9:50 and 10:30. [4:30:02]

## Process / order of operations
1. Pre-session (≥30–60 min before 9:30 ET): open ES and NQ charts in ET. [3:36:12]
2. Mark session levels: Asia (7 PM/6 PM–4 AM) and London (3:00–8:30 AM for indexes) highs/lows. Discard any already traded through. [3:42:48] [3:55:28]
3. Mark 4H highs/lows and 4H FVGs, then 1H highs/lows and 1H FVGs (plus PDH/PDL). Discard filled/reacted or disrespected levels and levels too far away. [3:43:57] [3:45:05]
4. Mark the 9:50 time line. The entry window is 9:50–10:30. [3:45:39]
5. Wait for price to hit a key level (often in the 9:30–9:50 period). [3:46:47]
6. On the 5m: confirmation via BOS, IFVG or SMT (ES vs NQ; trade the "lagging" index that made the new extreme). [3:38:57] [3:47:21]
7. On the 5m: wait for a retrace into an FVG, OB, breaker or EQ. [3:39:30]
8. On the 1m: within the zone, wait for a 1m BOS or IFVG (the IFVG is preferred for price). Enter at the trigger candle close, only at or after 9:50. [3:40:34] [3:50:11]
9. Stop beyond invalidation (the zone/IFVG or swing extreme). TPs at the next opposing key levels, with partials. RR ≥ 1:1. [3:40:34] [3:41:09] [4:11:17]
10. One trade. Win or lose, done for the day. Journal it. [4:49:02] [4:45:10] [4:18:07]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Learn on a demo account first. Profitable on demo ≠ profitable live; fear and greed appear only with real money. [30:30] [4:50:41]
- The stop is a fixed $ loss per trade, RR ≥ 1:1, risk 1–3% (TJR) or 0.25–1% (high-RR traders). [4:10:05] [4:11:17] [4:14:42]
- One or two trades per day at most. Usually only one setup occurs from 9:50 to 10:30. [4:30:02]
- **After a loss: done for the day.** "Take ourselves out of the market for that day." If the stop is hit, "there's no reason to even look for another trade because my idea is gone." [4:45:10] [4:46:16] [4:50:07]
- **After a win: done for the day.** "You won on the day … take your money and leave." [4:49:02]
- **After a missed trade (hesitated): done for the day.** "Go home, you're done for the day." Don't force another. [4:53:32]
- Don't extend or remove TPs out of greed. Take profits at the planned key level. [4:45:41] [4:51:50]
- Never move the stop back, and always set the stop before entering. [4:31:44] [4:40:41]
- Detach from money: "your goal isn't to make money in trading, your goal is to be right about where price wants to go." Don't set daily $ goals, because chasing them compounds losses. [4:46:49] [4:49:34]
- Three fears: of being wrong, of losing money (both cause hesitation) and of missing out (causes forcing). "The market will be here forever." [4:51:50] [4:52:23] [4:52:59]
- Think in probabilities; no one has a 100% win rate. Losing streaks happen, and 10 losses in a row is possible, which is why risk stays low. [4:14:08] [4:54:38]
- Journal every trade with: win/loss, pair, bias (which key levels you expected), confluences, RR and "post-trade clarity". Use it to derive your win rate and average RR (which sets your risk %) and to find setups that keep losing. [4:16:25] [4:18:07] [4:18:41] [4:23:47]
