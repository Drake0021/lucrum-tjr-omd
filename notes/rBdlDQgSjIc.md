## Concepts & definitions
- **Completed SMT becomes a sweep** — once the bullish SMT completes (ES takes the highs), "we no longer can be looking to take longs and use that as a confluence". It turns into the sweep for the opposite move [02:50; 01:07].
- **Leading vs lagging index for execution** — "we would probably have to take the short on NASDAQ because ES would be the lagging index ... NASDAQ would be the leading index on this SMT divergence" [03:23]. Later: "ES was the leading index on the lower time frame ... we kind of should have taken the trade on ES" [24:28–25:02; 27:52]. In short, trade the index leading in the trade direction (the one that did NOT make the new high) (inferred synthesis).
- **Illiquid / imbalanced price range** — "when we see a swift move down, we can expect a swift move up back through that same exact illiquid price range". After swift moves both ways it is "still an imbalanced price range" and "a good spot ... to retrace into to then finally end up balancing out", useful both as a POI and as the TP [03:59–05:06].
- **5m sequence for shorts** — "high time frame sweep, bearish SMT divergence, we get a five minute retrace on ES then boom, we start moving down" [14:39]. Next step after the SMT + BOS: "waiting for a five minute retrace into equilibrium from this high down to wherever the low gets formed" [05:42–06:21].
- **1m SMT as an early trigger** — "why did I go short before seeing a one minute inverse fair value gap or a one minute break of structure to the downside? Because we had this one minute bearish SMT" [24:28; 25:49].
- **Time-stamped highs for SMT matching** — compares the 9:39 highs on both indexes and the 9:37 highs to identify which index is leading [22:42–23:15].
- **79% extension close = 1m change in order flow** — "We just need to see a change in order flow on the one minute. So, if we can close above the 79% extension, I'll go long" [35:24–35:57].
