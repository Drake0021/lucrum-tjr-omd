## Summary
TJR explains how to respond to a losing streak: never change the strategy, but diagnose losses (via journaling) and make small tweaks — e.g., switch instrument when price action is bad, or require extra entry confluence when executions are failing while bias is correct. He reveals his own current tweak: stop entering on a bare low-timeframe liquidity sweep + break of structure and require an order block / FVG / equilibrium entry after the BOS. Remainder is mindset: the market is never at fault, adapt to what price shows (including flipping bias on a HTF BOS against you), and review both wins and losses daily.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Losing streak (his threshold)** — Two losing days is NOT a losing streak; he would consider two or three losing *weeks* a losing streak. Losing two trades is normal. [08:16]
- **Diagnosing losses: analysis vs execution** — Separate whether the market analysis/bias was wrong vs. whether execution was wrong. If price keeps fulfilling your bias after stopping you out, the analysis is fine and the execution needs more patience/confluence. [06:38], [07:12]
- **"Bad price action"** — Periods where price chops, regular confluences don't play out, stops get hit, and there's never a clear bias (his example: S&P 500 ~two months before this video). [05:32]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Never change your strategy during a losing streak; a working strategy does not "get patched." Make only small tweaks/adjustments targeted at the identified pattern of mistakes. [01:02], [03:18], [05:32]
- IF the instrument you trade has bad/choppy price action (confluences not playing out, no clear bias) THEN a valid small tweak is switching to another market you've traded successfully before (he moved from S&P 500 to Forex and had a $100K week). [01:02], [06:04]
- ENTRY TWEAK (his updated execution rule): do NOT enter on a low-timeframe liquidity sweep + break of structure alone (price was sweeping, breaking structure, stopping him out, then going his way). Only enter on: [02:10], [03:18]
  - liquidity sweep → BOS → **order block** entry;
  - liquidity sweep → BOS → **fair value gap** entry;
  - liquidity sweep → BOS → **order block + FVG + equilibrium** entry.
- IF you get a high-timeframe break of structure against your bias (e.g., bullish bias, HTF BOS to the downside) THEN adapt and switch the bias; holding the old bias in a HTF bearish trend means repeated stop-outs. [09:21]
- Be patient; don't force trades; if you see nothing, don't take a trade — especially on a losing streak. [04:25]
- Don't try to "make back" money on a losing streak. [04:25]

## Process / order of operations
1. Journal every trade so patterns in losses become visible. [02:43]
2. After a loss: review — what could I do better, where did I go wrong, how can I improve. [11:35]
3. Determine if bias was right (price eventually went your way) vs. execution wrong. [07:12]
4. Identify the recurring mistake(s) across losses and "chop off" each one with a small rule tweak (more confluence, more patience, different instrument). [07:44]
5. Also review wins: why did I win, how could I have gotten better R:R / a better trade. [08:16], [12:08]

## Risk & psychology rules
- Worst thing on a losing streak is continuously trying to make back money. [04:25]
- Confidence comes from having proven profitability; losses will be recovered over time. [04:25]
- It is never the market's fault or the strategy's fault; it's always your execution. Trade what the market is showing, not what you think it should do. [08:48], [10:28]
- You must be okay with losing and learn from every loss. [09:55]
- Spend hours daily watching charts and journaling. [11:00]
