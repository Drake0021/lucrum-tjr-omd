## Summary
Educational walkthrough of his intraday Forex session strategy ("the first strategy that turned me profitable", ~1.5 years of payouts incl. funded accounts). Same logic as his S&P approach (wait for a session open → liquidity sweep → BOS) but liquidity = previous-session highs/lows marked on the 30m as body-to-wick boxes, executed on the 5m. Entry on the 5m BOS candle close (optionally refine into an FVG/OB if RR is poor), stop beyond the "sweep", TPs at previous session highs/lows and other liquidity; 50% off at TP1 + stop to BE, another 50% at TP2. Bias from the 4H on forex (vs. 1H for the S&P). Ends with a 6-step checklist. Several GJ and gold bar-replay examples (RR 1:2.4–1:6).

## Concepts & definitions
- **Forex session theory** — "session highs and lows will be respected or played off of by following sessions" (not his invention; not from ICT; forgot source). [03:29]
- **Session clock (TradingView set to London time)** — Asia 00:00–08:00, London 08:00–13:00 (London pre-market from 07:00), New York 13:00–00:00 (13:00 = NYSE pre-market; NY "closes ~21:00"), then spread hour and Asian pre-market. [05:17] [05:52] [06:26] [28:35]
- **Spread hour** — hour between NY close and Asia when spreads are huge; untradeable. [06:59]
- **Session high/low box** — from the body of the candle to the wick tip ("base of the candle up to the wick"), because price sometimes reacts in the wick (e.g., a LTF order block) without a full sweep. [07:32] [08:07] [29:09]
- **"Sweep" in quotes** — price may just respect the box (fill into it) without sweeping; still valid. [08:39] [28:01]
- Session high/low = "the lowest low of the session and the highest high of the session" on the 30m. [21:41]
- Opposite-type levels count as targets: a London session *high* can be a short TP because sessions respect levels either way. [11:59]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Bias for forex: 4H BOS direction ("on the S&P 500 [we find bias] on the 1 hour; on this ... the 4 Hour"). [18:52]
- WHEN a previous-session box gets hit after a new session opens THEN go to the 5m and wait for a BOS (candle close beyond the most recent opposing swing). [09:44] [27:23]
- Short: high pushed through → monitor the most recent low → close below = enter; stop above the sweep. Long mirror. [10:18] [13:08]
- Track the swing to be broken as it moves ("now this is the high ... now this is the high" until a close above). [15:57]
- If RR isn't to your liking, you may refine the entry with an FVG or OB after the 5m BOS. [11:25]
- TP1: previous session low/high or nearby liquidity that gives decent RR (1:1+ fine); TP2/3 further session levels; extended TPs at older levels. TPs may be placed at the bottom (body) of a session box rather than its wick. [10:18] [13:41] [17:08]
- Management: TP1 → take 50% off, stop to breakeven; TP2 → another 50% off; keep going. [14:14] [24:32]
- Stop may be placed under the lower lows for safety; adds slightly above the close for fill slippage. [15:57] [23:26]
- Don't manage too early — stick to stop and TPs through 5m drawdown. [26:16]
- Asian session open: tradable but he doesn't recommend it. [21:06]
- Use manual vertical lines for session opens, not indicators. Set alerts at box bases. [28:35] [29:09]
- He "likes to just stick to one trade a day, keep my risk low". [30:14]

## Process / order of operations (his on-screen checklist) [26:51]–[29:42]
1. Find session opens and mark them (vertical lines; London time: 0–8 Asia, 8–13 London, 13–0 NY).
2. Mark session highs and lows on the 30m with a box (base of candle body to wick).
3. When session highs/lows get hit → go to the 5m.
4. Wait for a 5m BOS.
5. Enter; stop under/above the "sweep".
6. Take profit at previous areas of liquidity / previous session highs/lows (or FVG/OB "high confluence areas").

## Risk & psychology rules
