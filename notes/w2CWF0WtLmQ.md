## Summary
First "discipline" episode. TJR announces the course will alternate days between trading and discipline/mindset, because he believes the only real difference between him and students trading the same strategy is mindset. The homework is to choose one new, uncomfortable, beneficial habit and do it every day for the rest of the summer (he chose reading 15-20 pages/day). He cites habit formation at ~21 days and routine at ~60 days, and stresses discipline over motivation. No chart content; one trading-related reminder about risk management and not over-trading/over-leveraging.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Course structure** — alternate days: discipline/psychology day, then trading day (Day 1 discipline, Day 2 trading, Day 3 discipline, Day 4 trading...). [01:09]
- **Mindset as the gap** — students trade "the same strategy" and take similar or the same trades, but lose by bailing out of trades or lacking confidence to take them; the trading part "is relatively easy". [03:20]-[03:53]
- **Habit vs routine** — he cites a statistic: a habit forms in ~21 days; it becomes routine/comfortable at ~60 days. [09:25]-[10:31]
- **Motivation vs discipline** — motivation fades after ~a week; "the discipline is all that you have left". [26:40]-[28:21]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Professional traders trade with risk management, without emotion, with discipline; do not treat trading like a casino; no over-trading or over-leveraging — "you'll never be profitable if you do that". [24:59]

## Process / order of operations
1. Choose one habit that is outside your comfort zone and genuinely beneficial (gym, reading, walking, etc.). [07:11]-[08:50]
2. Do it every day, starting today, and continue through the summer and beyond. [29:27]
3. Use alarms/reminders; speak to yourself to get up ("third person" view of yourself). [11:37], [25:33]
4. Track progress (e.g., weekly photos) and optionally document the journey daily (video diary/TikTok) for accountability. [22:13], [31:41]-[32:46]
5. Model your "dream self": what does that person do daily to maintain and keep improving — copy it. [23:53]-[24:27]
