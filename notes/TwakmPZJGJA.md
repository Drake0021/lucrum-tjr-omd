## Summary
Psychology day on fear and doubt, the counterpart to overconfidence. TJR's answer is a staged progression that builds proof and confidence: demo (no reason for fear) → a small live account risking 1% (~$1 on a $100 account) to get used to real money → re-prove on demo if the live account is blown → funded account or personal capital (>$10k you can afford to lose) → live account funded by payouts. His graduation criterion is ~3 months of profitability with ~10% gain, one trade a day, 1% risk. He stresses that the step-by-step strategy and proven track record should make execution robotic and boring. Second half (~20:00 onward) is about his workload and motivation, no trading content.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Fear/doubt** — hesitation to place a trade, rooted in subconscious attachment to money; on demo there should be none since nothing is lost. [01:40] [02:44] [10:03]
- **Profitable (definition)** — "consistently making money from the market"; winning on demo does not make you profitable. [07:15]
- **Psychology is "90% of trading"** — it's "you versus you"; each trader's issue differs (greed, overconfidence, fear). [13:55] [14:29]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Standard risk parameters (stated repeatedly): one trade a day, risk 1% per trade/day. [06:40] [17:13] [18:24]
- Demo → live criterion (his personal rule): at least three months of profitability with over 10% gain, taking one trade a day risking 1%. (Vague whether 10% total over three months or per month; at [12:17] he says "at least like a 10% gain over the course of three months straight"; at [12:50] he says funded challenges require "win 10% a month".) [06:40] [12:17] [12:50]
- First live account: small, e.g. $100 risking 1% ($1 per trade) — to practice the feeling of winning/losing real money without attachment. [10:37] [11:11]
- If you blow/lose the live account → go back to demo, re-prove profitability, re-learn why you use the strategy and risk management. [08:22]
- Only put money in a live account that you can afford to lose. [08:55] [09:30]
- Capital rule: if you have over $10,000 of capital you're willing to lose → you can trade a personal live account; otherwise → go to a funded (prop) account, get 1–2 payouts, put payouts into a live account. [12:50] [13:23] [17:48]
- Execution should be robotic; the proven step-by-step strategy is what should give the confidence to enter. [15:34] [19:35]

## Process / order of operations
Confidence/proof progression [12:17]–[13:23] [19:35]:
1. Demo, one trade/day, 1% risk, until ~3 months profitable (~10% gain).
2. Small live account (~$100, $1 risk) — get used to real money; prove profitable again (~3 months).
3. If blown, return to demo and re-prove.
4. Funded account (pass challenge, 1–2 payouts) or own capital if >$10k you can afford to lose.
5. Move payouts into a live account. ~9 months of proof ≈ "damn near profitable trader status".

## Risk & psychology rules
- Encourage/accept losses while learning; losing on demo is free learning. [01:40] [05:30]
- A 10% day is a red flag of excessive risk/trades — "even though you made 10% today you still did something wrong". [04:58] [05:30]
- Odds of blowing the first live account ~90% (his estimate) because money gets to your head → over-leveraging/over-trading. [07:48]
- Train emotional neutrality with tiny stakes: don't be excited about wins, don't be sad about losses. [11:44]
- Trading should be boring: risk management, one trade a day, 1% risk, follow the trading plan every day. [17:48] [18:24]
- Treat trading as a job with a step-by-step execution plan; don't come in with a new strategy every day. [19:01]
