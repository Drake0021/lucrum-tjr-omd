## Summary
TJR explains SMT divergence between ES and NQ: one index makes a higher low (bullish) or lower high (bearish) while the other makes a lower low / higher high at the same time, forecasting that the lagging index will follow. Bias must be set first (HTF), SMT is checked at HTF levels / liquidity sweeps, on the 5m–15m (not 1m), and lows are used for bullish SMT, highs for bearish. He typically executes on the index that made the "worse" swing (the one that swept), e.g. ES when ES made the lower low. Examples are from ES/NQ around the NY open and pre-market, May 2024. He also says he streams every market open at 8:30 ET on Kick.

## Concepts & definitions
- **SMT divergence** — "One index forecasting what another index is going to do." [03:20]
- **Bullish SMT** — At the same point in time, one index creates a higher low while the other creates a lower low. Example: NQ low then higher low; ES low then lower low → ES likely goes higher. [04:31][05:04]
- **Bearish SMT** — One index makes a high then lower high while the other makes a high then higher high, at a HTF level / HTF liquidity sweep. [09:04][09:37][20:32]
- **Only lows for bullish, only highs for bearish** — "when we're looking for bullish SMT we're only using the lows and when we're looking for bearish SMT we're only looking for highs." [09:04]
- **Magnitude** — Typically the divergence is much smaller than his "drastic" example (the higher low barely above the prior low). [04:31]
- **Lag interpretation** — The index with the lower low (bullish) is "lagging behind" and will "follow suit"; more opportunity on the lagging index. [19:49]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- First establish overall bias; without bias you'll find conflicting SMTs. [05:37]
- Only take SMT that is in line with bias. [09:37]
- SMT is relevant when price is coming into a HTF level / HTF liquidity sweep. [09:04][19:15]
- Bullish bias + into bullish level: look for higher low on one index and lower low on the other; execute on the one that made the lower low (e.g., ES), since it's lagging. [19:15][19:49]
- Bearish bias + into bearish level: lower high on one index, higher high on the other; execute on the one that made the higher high (inferred from symmetry; he says "this one is going to follow suit of that index"). [20:32]
- Timeframe: "I do not like to use SMT divergence on the one minute"; typically on 5m or 15m; also works well on HTF. [10:09]
- After SMT, scale down to lower timeframes and find entries (his normal entry model). [09:37][18:37]
- Target example: after bullish SMT, expected ES to draw to 15-minute highs. [05:37]

## Process / order of operations
1. Determine HTF bias (e.g., bullish on 4H even if 1H isn't). [03:20][05:37]
2. Wait for price into HTF level / liquidity sweep (e.g., hourly lows swept). [03:54][17:29]
3. Compare ES vs NQ swings on 5m/15m at the same times: bullish → lows; bearish → highs. [10:09]
4. Identify the lagging index; scale down on it to find entry; target nearby liquidity (e.g., 15m highs). [05:37][18:37]
