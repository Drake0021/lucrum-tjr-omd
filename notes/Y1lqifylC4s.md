## Summary
Daily bias recap with charts (ES/S&P, GBPUSD, gold). Key principle: "higher time frame holds higher power" — a lower-timeframe BOS against the higher timeframe is just a retracement of the higher timeframe (15m BOS = 1H retrace, 1H BOS = 4H retrace, 4H BOS = daily retrace, daily BOS = weekly retrace). Use the weekly to know when a daily move is likely a weekly retrace (so use retracement tools and moderate targets), the daily for bias, and the 4H/1H building blocks (liquidity, imbalance, order block, 50% discount/premium) for high-confluence zones; then execute on 15m/5m confirmation. If a marked zone gives no reaction, it's ignored. Always aim to trade the extension, not the retracement. Shows a GBPUSD short taken the previous day and a gold short setup with multi-target R:R (1:4.79 first target, 1:13.5 to the final).

## Concepts & definitions
- **Higher time frame holds higher power** — HTF direction dominates; a LTF BOS against it is a retrace of the next TF up: 15m BOS → 1H retrace; 1H BOS → 4H retrace; 4H BOS → daily retrace; daily BOS → weekly retrace. Don't get "trapped into the low time frame bias" (e.g., a 1m BOS may be just a 5m retrace). [00:33], [07:47], [08:20]
- **Weekly's role** — if daily breaks structure down while weekly is up, odds are it's a weekly retrace → use retracement tools (equilibrium, FVG) to estimate how far the daily move goes; the weekly retrace is over (next weekly extension starts) when the daily breaks structure back in the weekly direction. [00:33], [05:33], [14:36], [15:10]
- **Retracement minimum = "discount" beyond 50%** — "the bare minimum that I look for for retracements is: is it even in a discount" (i.e., past equilibrium of the HTF range). He uses "discount" even for bearish setups where price retraces up (standard term: premium) — see contradictions. [11:12], [13:30]
- **Order block (HTF)** — the leg/candle that swept liquidity and caused the opposite move ("big leg down taking out liquidity, rallies higher off of it, orders were filled with this move down"); also "the move down that caused this entire move up". [06:40], [11:46], [12:21]
- **Quarterly order block / smart money narrative (S&P)** — personal macro view: S&P swept COVID-era lows/took ATH liquidity; a quarterly OB barely tapped; he personally thinks price wants lower to take COVID lows, but admits monthly broke structure up. Opinion, explicitly "just how I feel". [01:37]–[05:01]
- **Next draw on liquidity in a downtrend** — "the next draw on liquidity in a downtrend is just the most recent low", then the discount level, then further liquidity, then the order block. [14:02], [14:36]
- **Building blocks as high-probability price ranges** — FVG/OB/liquidity are price ranges with high confluence for orders to be filled; you execute only off confirmation within them. [18:36]
- **Extension vs retracement** — always aim to trade the extension (big leg in HTF direction), not the small counter-leg. [08:52]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Bias = daily market structure; confirm 4H and 1H in same direction ("even better"). [05:33]
- Use 4H/1H building blocks to find HTF high-confluence areas; when price enters, map 15m/5m confluences; execute on LTF liquidity sweep + 5m BOS in bias direction. [06:06], [07:14]
- IF price comes into a marked zone and gives no reaction (no LTF confirmation) THEN do not execute — "it was never there". [09:26], [10:01]
- If the HTF has "not much to go off of" (no clear confluence), don't trade (he skipped S&P on Friday for this reason). [06:06]
- Ignore counter-bias LTF BOS (e.g., a 15m BOS down in a bullish 1H = 1H retrace). [07:47]
- IF daily trend is down but weekly up THEN take shorts but expect limited downside; use daily confluences as take-profits for small-timeframe trades. [11:12]
- Don't short "underneath in a premium" (he means in the cheap half of the range for a short — inferred) when the 4H broke up; wait for price to retrace into the HTF zone past 50% plus 4H OB + 1H OB + 15m/5m reaction. [13:30], [14:02]
- A 4H BOS up while daily is bearish = daily retracement; once in the daily retracement zone (past 50%), look for reversal points with LTF confirmation. [16:54]
- Entry trigger for gold example: wait for 15m BOS down (not yet present → no execution). [18:01], [19:09]
- Targets: sequential HTF draws — most recent low (liquidity), 50% level, further liquidity, order block(s). Multiple take-profits; he takes partials rather than holding to the final target. [14:36], [19:43], [20:48]
- He doesn't like holding over the weekend or for 3–4 days. [12:55], [19:43]

## Process / order of operations
1. Weekly: direction; is a daily counter-move likely a weekly retrace? [05:33]
2. Daily: market structure → bias. [05:33]
3. 4H and 1H: direction; mark building blocks (liquidity under 1H lows, imbalances, OBs, discount/premium). [06:06], [06:40]
4. Wait for price into a high-confluence zone. [06:40]
5. 15m/5m: map more confluence; wait for liquidity sweep + BOS (5m/15m) in bias direction. [07:14]
6. Execute; target daily draws (TP1, TP2, ...), taking partials. [14:02], [19:43]
7. No reaction → no trade. [10:01]
