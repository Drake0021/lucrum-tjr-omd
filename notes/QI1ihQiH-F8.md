## Summary
This is an intermission in the confluence series. It explains why a strategy must be **systemized**: a fixed, step-by-step sequence of timeframes and confluences. With a fixed sequence, journaling can show which component loses, and that component can be removed. He tells traders to trade one instrument and one session. He also admits his earlier Boot Camp failed to give a systemized strategy. His own systemized strategy is promised for later. The example sequence he gives is explicitly not his.

## Concepts & definitions
- **Systemized strategy** — A fixed step-by-step program: one timeframe gives an input → rotate to the next timeframe → decide based on the prior inputs → execution confluence → reaction. Inputs must be the same every day. [05:41–06:14]
- **Unsystemized trading** — Using different timeframes and confluences each day, e.g. daily+4H on Monday, 1H+4H on Tuesday, the weekly on Wednesday. "You guys have like 30 different strategies." [04:35–05:41; 09:39]

## Rules (model / entry / stop / target / management / filters / risk)
- Trade only **one pair/instrument** and only **one session**. [04:03]
- Don't mark every confluence on every timeframe (weekly → 1m). [04:35]
- Keep the bias timeframes, execution timeframe and confluence list fixed. Use journaling to find the confluences with low win rates (e.g. "90% of the time when I enter off order blocks I lose") and drop them. [03:30; 07:57–08:30]
- Execution is mechanical once the steps are met: press buy/sell, set SL and TP. Otherwise "sit on your hands." [09:04]
- An illustrative (NOT his) sequence: daily bias → 4H bias agrees → 15m: sweep of HTF liquidity → BOS → one entry confluence (OB, FVG, EQ, breaker block) → reaction. "That's not my strategy ... don't use that." [06:49–07:23]
